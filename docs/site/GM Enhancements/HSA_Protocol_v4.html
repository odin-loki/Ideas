PROGRAMME OVERVIEW

The HSA Protocol is a speculative three-phase genetic and bioprinting enhancement programme designed to systematically expand the biological capabilities of Homo sapiens beyond evolved baselines. Version 4.0 incorporates revised organ replacement logic, a dedicated skeletal fortification module relocated to Phase I (pure gene therapy, no bone bioprinting), new longevity modules (Klotho, GDF11, mTORC1 genetic modulation), a gut and metabolic axis module, brown fat thermogenesis, and a split of the neural module into Hardware (structural/myelination) and Software (neurotransmitter/receptor) tracks.

Design Philosophy

  • Future-realistic: all interventions grounded in peer-reviewed science as of 2025; extrapolated, not invented
  • Layered delivery: AAV vectors, base/prime editing, LNP, ex vivo lentiviral, and 3D bioprinting deployed in sequence
  • Reversibility built-in: CRISPRi/dCas9-KRAB shutdown, ASO/siRNA suppression, dox-inducible transgene withdrawal, surgical implant removal
  • Organ logic: replace via bioprinting where gene therapy cannot achieve the required structural gain; enhance genetically where native architecture must be preserved
  • Risk stratification: full monitoring, oncological kill-switch, cytokine storm, xenogene rejection, neurological shutdown, cardiac ICU protocols

FOXO3 Longevity Note

FOXO3 is one of only two human genes with consistent, replicated association with extreme longevity across diverse populations (Japanese-Americans, Germans, Danes, Italians, Brazilians). The longevity alleles (rs2802292, rs4946935) are intronic enhancers that increase FOXO3 expression in response to IGF-1 pathway signals. Centenarians carrying these alleles show reduced pro-inflammatory cytokines and lower SASP. CRISPRa targeting the rs2802292 regulatory element is preferred over coding-region overexpression. FOXO3 functions: stem cell maintenance, autophagy induction, ROS destruction, proteostasis, cell cycle regulation, anti-inflammatory cytokine balance.

I.9 Skeletal Fortification [NEW in v4]

Version 4.0 moves all bone enhancement to Phase I via systemic gene therapy, eliminating the need for bone bioprinting. The approach combines sclerostin suppression (mirrors romosozumab mechanism but genetically encoded), LRP5 G171V gain-of-function (the Connecticut high bone mass family variant), RANKL/OPG ratio optimisation, and collagen matrix enhancement. LRP5 G171V is introduced as a heterozygous base edit to avoid the cranial complications of homozygosity.

LRP5 G171V — The High Bone Mass Mutation

LRP5 G171V is a naturally occurring gain-of-function mutation first described in a Connecticut family (Boyden et al. NEJM 2002). Carriers have bone mineral density T-scores +3 to +5 SD above mean, have survived major trauma fracture-free, and no adverse systemic effects have been reported from heterozygosity. The G171V substitution renders LRP5 resistant to sclerostin (SOST) and DKK1 inhibition, maintaining constitutive Wnt signalling in osteocytes. Heterozygous knock-in mice (Lrp5 G171V+/-) replicate the HBM phenotype and are protected from disuse osteoporosis and ovariectomy-induced bone loss. Note: homozygous G/G mice show cranial nerve entrapment and forehead bossing — heterozygous expression via ABE8e is the target.

REST/NRSF Caution

REST/NRSF is a transcriptional repressor of neuronal genes whose nuclear localisation in aging neurons is neuroprotective — repressing oxidative stress and beta-amyloid toxicity gene programmes (Lu et al. Nature 2014). REST is highly expressed in supercentenarian brains; its loss correlates with Alzheimer's and Parkinson's. REST evolved under strong positive selection in humans and peaks in expression during human aging (not other primates). HOWEVER: (a) chronic overexpression promotes neural excitability; (b) nuclear localisation — not just expression level — is the key variable; (c) age-inducible promoter preferred over constitutive. Approach: CRISPRa targeting endogenous regulatory elements, not exogenous overexpression cassette.

II.6 mTORC1 Genetic Modulation [NEW in v4]

mTOR inhibition is the most replicated longevity intervention across organisms. Rapamycin extends lifespan 9-14% in mice even starting at 600 days (Harrison et al. Nature 2009). The 2025 PEARL trial (48-week RCT, n=114) confirmed safety of 5-10 mg weekly rapamycin in healthy adults. A 2025 rapamycin + trametinib combination achieved 30% average lifespan extension in mice via non-overlapping pathways.

V4 introduces genetic modulation as the primary long-term approach. The goal: reduce mTORC1 (pro-senescence, pro-SASP, hyperactivated in aging) while preserving mTORC2 (insulin sensitivity, cell survival, actin cytoskeleton). Rapamycin is retained as pharmacological adjunct.

Reversibility Architecture

  • CRISPRi (dCas9-KRAB): gene suppression without DNA editing — delivered after any CRISPRa intervention to reduce expression. Reversible by cessation.
  • ASO / siRNA: sequence-specific mRNA knockdown. Transient (days-weeks). Ideal for rapid intervention at any stage.
  • Dox-inducible OSK / transgene withdrawal: remove doxycycline — circuit silenced within 24 hours. Standard for Phase II reprogramming.
  • Rapamycin dose titration: pharmacological, adjustable in real time. mTOR pathway effects visible within days.
  • Surgical implant removal: bioprinted organ supplements and cardiac patches designed for surgical accessibility; removal feasible in all phases.
  • CCR5 deletion: irreversible in edited cells at DNA level. Pre-edit HSC backup stored (autologous cryopreservation) before any editing. HSC compartment replacement possible if required.
  • Base edits (LRP5 G171V, MYBPC3 rs1052373, SWS1 Phe86Val, COMT Val158Met): single-base changes. Subsequent base editing can revert. Most are gain-of-function — CRISPRi of downstream pathway is alternative.
