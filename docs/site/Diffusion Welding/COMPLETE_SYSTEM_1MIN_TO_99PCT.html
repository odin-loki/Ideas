  2. Fixture setup (10 min) - Position parts - Apply moderate pressure (20-30 MPa) - Attach electrical leads - Position ultrasonic horn

BONDING (60 minutes) 4. Heat to 250°C (10 min) - Ramp gradually - Monitor temperature uniformly

  1. Activate bonding (60 min at 250°C) - Turn on DC power (3-5V, 200 A/m²) - Activate ultrasonic (2 W/cm², continuous) - Monitor current (should gradually decrease) - Maintain temperature ±5°C

  1. Cool down (15 min) - Controlled cooling to <100°C - Release pressure only after cooling

  2. Cleanup and inspection (5 min) - Remove excess substrate - Visual inspection - Optional: NDT verification

TOTAL TIME: 2 hours 20 minutes

Safety & Environmental

Safety Considerations

Chemical Hazards: - Ionic liquids: Low toxicity, eye irritation possible - Gallium: Stains skin (harmless), don't ingest - Organometallics: Handled in sealed substrate, minimal exposure - Standard PPE: Safety glasses, gloves

Electrical Hazards: - Low voltage (3-5V) - minimal shock risk - High current (up to 8000 A/m²) - proper insulation required - GFCI protection recommended

Thermal Hazards: - ULTRA-FLASH: 150°C - standard burn precautions - ULTRA-99%: 250-300°C - furnace-grade protection - All regimes: Allow cooling before handling

Ultrasonic Hazards: - Hearing protection (20-40 kHz audible to some) - No direct skin contact with operating horn - Standard industrial ultrasonic safety

Overall: Much safer than fusion welding - No UV radiation - No toxic fumes (unless substrate overheated) - No molten metal - No high voltage

Environmental Impact

Substrate disposal: - Ionic liquids: Recyclable or dispose as industrial chemical waste - Gallium: Recyclable metal, valuable - Small volumes per joint (<10 mL typical)

Energy consumption: - 100x less than fusion welding per joint - Low carbon footprint - Suitable for off-grid/renewable power

Waste products: - Minimal - substrate consumed into joint - No slag, spatter, or filler material waste - Cleaner than traditional welding
