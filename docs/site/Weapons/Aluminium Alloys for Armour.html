Executive Overview

The AlNiCyN Family represents a comprehensive suite of aluminum-based armor alloys designed to provide rolled homogeneous armor (RHA) equivalent performance at significant weight reduction. The family consists of three distinct alloy systems, each targeting different application requirements and technology readiness levels.

Family Architecture

├── Technology: Proven aluminum metallurgy
├── TRL: 7-8 (System prototype demonstration)
├── Cost: $10,030/metric ton
├── Applications: Near-term military procurement
└── Performance: 1.0x RHA equivalent, 60% weight reduction

AlNiCyN-7000 (HIGH-PERFORMANCE)

Common Processing Technologies

Melting & Casting

Vacuum Induction Melting (VIM)

Principle:        Induction heating in vacuum/controlled atmosphere
Advantages:       
  - Excellent chemistry control
  - Low hydrogen pickup
  - Homogeneous melt
  - Reactive element compatibility

Equipment:
  Capacity:       10-100 ton furnaces available
  Power:          500-5,000 kW
  Vacuum:         10⁻³ to 10⁻⁵ torr
  Cost:           $2-10M depending on size

Process Control:
  Temperature:    ±3°C with pyrometry
  Atmosphere:     Programmable gas mixtures
  Stirring:       Electromagnetic (no contact)
  Sampling:       Pin sampler for chemistry

Critical Parameters

Melt Temperature:
  - Too low:      Incomplete dissolution
  - Too high:     Excessive oxidation, grain growth
  - Optimal:      Liquidus + 30-50°C

Stirring Time:
  - Minimum:      5 minutes for homogenization
  - Maximum:      15 minutes (oxide pickup risk)

Degassing:
  - Method:       Rotary degassing with argon
  - Duration:     10-15 minutes
  - Target:       <0.15 cc H₂/100g Al

Thermomechanical Processing

Hot Rolling Fundamentals

Purpose:
  - Shape transformation
  - Grain refinement
  - Texture development
  - Property optimization

Key Variables:
  Temperature:    Recrystallization kinetics
  Reduction:      Stored energy, grain elongation
  Speed:          Strain rate effects
  Cooling:        Final microstructure

Roll Configuration:
  4-high:         Standard for plate
  6-high:         Better flatness control
  Cluster:        Thin gauge capability

Rolling Schedule Design

Breakdown Passes (1-3):
  Purpose:        Eliminate cast structure
  Reduction:      15-20% per pass
  Temperature:    Hottest (near recrystallization)

Working Passes (4-7):
  Purpose:        Grain refinement, shaping
  Reduction:      10-15% per pass
  Temperature:    Controlled descent

Finishing Passes (8-10):
  Purpose:        Final gauge, surface, texture
  Reduction:      8-12% per pass
  Temperature:    Above minimum for cracking

Heat Treatment

Solution Treatment Principles

Objective:       Dissolve soluble phases into solid solution
Temperature:     Below incipient melting point
Duration:        Sufficient for diffusion
Quench:          Retain supersaturated solid solution

Critical Factors:
  Heating Rate:   Minimize thermal gradients
  Soak Time:      Function of thickness and elements
  Uniformity:     ±3-5°C maximum variation
  Quench Rate:    >100°C/min for most Al alloys
  Transfer Time:  <10 seconds to quench

Aging Treatment Mechanisms

Natural Aging (Room Temperature):
  - Formation of GP (Guinier-Preston) zones
  - Coherent with matrix
  - Moderate strengthening
  - Continues for months

Artificial Aging (Elevated Temperature):
  Stage I:   GP zone formation
  Stage II:  Transition precipitates (η', θ')
  Stage III: Equilibrium phases (over-aging)

Peak Aging:
  - Maximum precipitate density
  - Optimal size distribution
  - Coherent/semi-coherent interfaces
  - Maximum strength

Multi-Stage Aging for AlNiCyN-7000

Stage 1 - Nucleation (120°C, 8 hours):
  Purpose:        High GP zone density
  Benefit:        Fine precipitate distribution
  Risk:           Under-aging if shortened

Stage 2 - Growth (165°C, 18 hours):
  Purpose:        η' precipitate development
  Benefit:        Peak strength
  Risk:           Over-aging if extended

Stage 3 - Stabilization (180°C, 8 hours):
  Purpose:        Optimize strength-toughness
  Benefit:        Improved ductility
  Risk:           Slight strength reduction
