Abstract

This paper presents a technical analysis of the 57mm Enhanced Dual-Purpose System (EDPS), an advanced infantry support weapon capable of both direct and indirect fire using 57×441mm calibre ammunition. The system weighs 32kg and features a titanium-steel composite construction enabling a sub-30 second deployment time. Direct fire parameters include a muzzle velocity of 350 m/s and an effective range of 800m, while indirect fire capability extends to a maximum range of 2,000m at angles up to 85° with 5m CEP at 1,000m. The EDPS fills a critical gap in infantry support by combining the precision of direct fire anti-armour engagement with the area-effect suppression of indirect mortar fire in a single crew-portable weapon system. This paper examines the mechanical design principles, sighting systems, and operational doctrine implications, with reference to comparable dual-role infantry weapons in current service.

1. Introduction

Infantry units operating without immediate artillery support require organic indirect fire capability to engage defiladed targets, suppress enemy in depth, and destroy light fortifications. Traditional approaches require separate weapons for direct fire anti-armour (RPG, LAW, ATGM) and indirect fire (mortar). Each system imposes logistical burden in terms of training, maintenance, and ammunition supply. A dual-purpose system capable of both roles from the same weapon and calibre addresses this inefficiency.

The 57mm calibre is particularly well-suited to dual-purpose application. At 350 m/s direct fire velocity, the HEIAP-T projectile retains sufficient kinetic energy for light armour defeat at 800m, while the 57mm bore diameter allows a projectile mass sufficient for effective indirect fire area effects at 2,000m range. The combination is achievable in a single 32kg system that can be broken into three components for man-portable transport.

2. Background

2.1 History of Dual-Purpose Direct/Indirect Fire Systems

The concept of a weapon capable of both direct flat-trajectory and high-angle indirect fire has precedents in both light mortar and recoilless rifle design. The Carl Gustaf 84mm recoilless rifle and its ammunition family represent a comparable approach to versatile infantry support, offering HEAT, HE, illumination, and smoke rounds from a single launcher platform. The M3 MAAWS variant weighs approximately 9kg but achieves significantly lower muzzle energy than the EDPS design. At the 120mm mortar end of the spectrum, the Israeli Soltam K6 provides high indirect fire capability in a company-level organic system.

The EDPS design is novel in combining a high-pressure direct-fire capability (2,800 bar operating pressure, 350 m/s muzzle velocity) with high-angle indirect fire through a mechanical quadrant and variable-angle mounting. This combination requires careful recoil management engineering to protect the mechanism and crew during both fire modes.

2.2 Titanium-Steel Composite Construction

The 32kg system weight is achieved through titanium-steel composite construction, where titanium alloy (typically Ti-6Al-4V) is used for primary structural elements where strength-to-weight ratio is critical, while steel is retained for bearing surfaces, barrel, and bolt group components where hardness and wear resistance are paramount. Ti-6Al-4V has a density of 4.43 g/cm³ compared to steel's approximately 7.85 g/cm³, enabling a 40-50% weight reduction in structural members without compromising strength.

4. Mechanical Design

4.1 Recoil System

The dual-spring progressive recoil system with hydraulic damper provides 250mm of recoil travel. Temperature-compensating valves maintain consistent recoil behaviour across the operational temperature range. The self-bleeding design eliminates the requirement for specialised hydraulic maintenance, while the self-contained unit format facilitates field replacement without workshop support.

At the 2,800 bar operating pressure of the 57×441mm cartridge with 350 m/s projectile velocity, impulse management is critical to both system durability and crew safety. The progressive spring system absorbs initial peak recoil force, with the hydraulic damper managing deceleration of the recoil mass throughout the travel distance. This dual-mechanism approach reduces peak force transmission to the mount by approximately 60% compared to a fixed-mount direct-fire arrangement.

4.2 Mounting and Base Plate System

The universal mount employs tapered roller bearings at the elevation pivot, providing zero-backlash traverse for both fire modes. The self-levelling base plate with expanding spade system provides stable mounting on varying terrain types. Anti-sink features distribute load to prevent progressive settlement during sustained indirect fire operations. The bipod arrangement supports quick positioning and emergency displacement.

4.3 Barrel System

The 1,200mm barrel with chrome-lined bore and quick-change system provides 800mm of effective direct fire barrel length for the 57mm projectile. Tool-less barrel removal and self-headspacing design enables barrel exchange without calibration instruments. The three-lug mounting with thermal indicators provides visual confirmation of safe barrel temperature, critical for sustained fire rate management. Enhanced cooling fins extend along the barrel exterior to accelerate convective cooling between engagements.

5. Sighting Systems

5.1 Direct Fire Sighting

The fixed 3× optical sight provides magnification for direct fire engagement out to 800m. The mechanical range cam translates target range input to elevation adjustment without electronic processing. Quick-adjust windage provides for lateral correction. Backup iron sights and tritium illumination enable night operations without active light sources. The simple fixed-magnification design is optimised for rapid target acquisition in direct fire scenarios where engagements are typically brief and at short-to-medium ranges.

5.2 Indirect Fire Sighting

The mechanical quadrant with bubble level system provides elevation setting for indirect fire. Sealed ball bearings in the mechanical calculator prevent debris ingress during field operation. The self-lubricating bushings with positive click stops enable precise elevation setting to 1 mil increments. Tritium illumination of reference markers supports night indirect fire missions. The position memory function enables rapid return to a registered firing position following displacement.

6. Safety Systems

The three-point mechanical safety system prevents firing under unsafe conditions. Out-of-battery detection prevents firing before the breech is fully locked. The firing pin block and drop safety provide additional passive protection against unintended discharge. All safety functions are fully mechanical with visual and tactile indicators, requiring no electronic sensing. The counter-balanced breech block with spring-assisted opening and self-cleaning extractor provide positive case extraction and chamber clearance after each shot.

7. Operational Doctrine

7.1 Direct Fire Application

In the direct fire role, the EDPS engages light armoured vehicles, field fortifications, and crew-served weapons at ranges up to 800m. The 350 m/s muzzle velocity of the HEIAP-T round provides sufficient kinetic energy for defeat of light armour at 300m, with approximately 2 mil accuracy enabling first-round hits on stationary vehicle-sized targets. The 6-8 rounds per minute rate enables rapid follow-up shots for target confirmation and multiple-target engagement.

7.2 Indirect Fire Application

In the indirect fire mortar role, the EDPS provides organic area suppression at ranges from 200m to 2,000m. The 5m CEP accuracy at 1,000m is competitive with standard 60mm mortar systems. The maximum 85° elevation angle enables engagement of targets in deep defilade. The 30-second setup time enables rapid displacement after fire-for-effect missions to avoid counter-battery response.

8. Maintenance

The field maintenance schedule requires a daily 2-minute basic check, weekly 15-minute cleaning, and monthly 1-hour service. Tool-less access to all maintenance points and clear visual inspection ports enable field-level service without specialist support. The simplified lubrication schedule and wear indicator system provide clear guidance on service requirements without electronic diagnostic support.

9. Conclusion

The 57mm Enhanced Dual-Purpose System provides infantry units with an organic direct/indirect fire support capability in a single 32kg crew-portable weapon. The titanium-steel composite construction achieves this weight target while maintaining structural integrity under both fire modes. The fully mechanical design, dual sighting systems, and sub-30-second deployment capability support rapid employment in dynamic tactical situations. The 57mm calibre shared with the AMAS autocannon system creates potential logistical simplification through common ammunition across multiple platforms.
