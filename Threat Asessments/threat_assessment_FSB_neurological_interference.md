## 4. NEUROSCIENCE ASSESSMENT

### 4.1 Transcranial Electrical Stimulation — Established Science

Transcranial electrical stimulation (tES) encompasses several well-documented modalities:

| Modality | Mechanism | Known Effects |
|---|---|---|
| tDCS (Direct Current) | Polarises neuronal membranes | Mood alteration, cognitive modulation, motor effects |
| tACS (Alternating Current) | Entrains neural oscillations | Disrupts/alters brain rhythm, affects perception |
| tRNS (Random Noise) | Stochastic resonance | Cognitive disruption, perceptual distortion |
| High-intensity protocols | Forced depolarisation | Pain, seizure induction, acute disorientation |

### 4.2 Induction of Schizophrenia-Like Symptoms

The target symptom profile — aggression, dissociation, compliance, cognitive fragmentation — maps onto specific neurological disruption targets:

**Increased Aggression:**
- Disruption of **prefrontal cortex (PFC)** function reduces inhibitory control over the amygdala
- Electrical interference with PFC-amygdala connectivity is achievable via frontal electrode placement
- Studies show tDCS inhibition of PFC reliably increases impulsive and aggressive responses

**Dissociation:**
- Disruption of **temporoparietal junction (TPJ)** and **posterior parietal cortex** produces dissociative symptoms
- TPJ is the neurological substrate for sense of self, body ownership, and reality grounding
- Electrical disruption here produces depersonalisation and derealisation consistent with schizophrenia-spectrum experience

**Increased Compliance / Reduced Autonomy:**
- Inhibition of **dorsolateral PFC (dlPFC)** reduces independent decision-making and increases suggestibility
- This is the neurological basis for compliance induction — reducing the subject's capacity for critical autonomous thought

**Cognitive Fragmentation:**
- Disruption of **gamma oscillations** (30–80 Hz) via tACS produces thought disorganisation
- Gamma disruption is directly implicated in the cognitive fragmentation characteristic of schizophrenia
- Achievable via alternating current at gamma frequencies applied to frontal and temporal regions

### 4.3 Lasting Effects

A key operational question is whether short-duration application produces lasting effects. The neuroscience suggests yes, under certain conditions:

- High-intensity stimulation can produce **long-term potentiation or depression (LTP/LTD)** — lasting changes in synaptic connectivity
- Repeated application compounds effects
- In subjects with underlying vulnerability (trauma, stress, prior substance use), effects may be significantly amplified and prolonged
- Stress-induced neuroplasticity means a subject in a high-threat environment is more susceptible to lasting alteration
