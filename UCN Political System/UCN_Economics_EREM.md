# Wealth on physical foundations: EREM in the UCN monetary and governance framework

**United Commonwealth Nations · UCN economic policy series**

## Abstract

*The UCN's evidence-based governance framework requires a monetary and economic measurement system that is equally evidence-based. This paper argues that the Energy-Resource Economic Model (EREM v1.0) — which grounds national wealth measurement in dimensionally consistent physical units (megajoules) and anchors currency issuance to verified Total National Wealth (TNW) — is the natural monetary foundation for the UCN. EREM resolves the central contradiction of conventional fiat economics that the UCN's non-partisan budget modelling and evidence-based policy framework cannot tolerate: the ability of monetary systems to represent wealth that does not physically exist. The paper applies EREM's TNW formula to Australia's actual resource endowment to produce indicative TNW and RBCU (Resource-Backed Currency Unit) estimates, analyses the alignment between EREM's incentive structures and each of the UCN's major economic policy positions, and proposes a phased UCN-EREM implementation pathway integrated with the UCN's four-phase governance transition roadmap.*

## 1. Introduction: The Monetary Problem with Evidence-Based Governance

The UCN framework is built on a commitment to evidence-based policy — the proposition that governance decisions should be grounded in the best available empirical evidence, subject to formal meta-analytic review, and evaluated against pre-registered outcome criteria. This commitment applies across every policy domain the UCN addresses: criminal justice, welfare, education, defence, research. It cannot, however, be fully realised while operating within a monetary system that systematically misrepresents the physical reality it claims to measure.

Fiat currency systems — including the Australian dollar — are not grounded in evidence. They are grounded in institutional confidence: the belief that the currency will retain value because the government that issues it will manage its supply responsibly. This confidence is not irrational; it is, in many cases, well-placed. But it is structurally corruptible in ways that no amount of good governance can permanently prevent. A government under fiscal pressure can always expand the money supply. A central bank under political pressure can always suppress interest rates. A treasury facing an election can always defer the reckoning of resource depletion through debt instruments that make the books look better than the physical reality warrants.

The Energy-Resource Economic Model (EREM, v1.0) addresses this structural vulnerability directly. By anchoring the currency supply to verified physical wealth — the sum of the nation's energy reserves, material resources, food production capacity, human labour capacity, and infrastructure efficiency — EREM makes monetary misrepresentation physically impossible rather than merely institutionally undesirable. Under EREM, the currency cannot expand faster than physical wealth grows, because the expansion formula is mathematical rather than discretionary.

*UCN-EREM Alignment: The UCN's Non-Partisan Budget Modelling Department produces independent evidence about fiscal reality. EREM produces a monetary system in which fiscal reality cannot be misrepresented. They are complements: the NPBMD identifies what the numbers should be; EREM makes the currency system enforce them.*

Section 4 analyses the alignment between EREM's incentive structures and each major UCN economic policy. Section 5 addresses implementation within the UCN's governance transition roadmap. Section 6 examines the EREM framework's application to the United Commonwealth and international trade architecture. Section 7 addresses limitations and open research questions. Section 8 concludes.

## 2. EREM: Framework Overview

### 2.1 The Foundational Critique of GDP

EREM's starting point — developed in detail in the companion papers EREM Explained and the EREM Technical Specification (v1.0, January 2026) — is that GDP does not measure wealth. It measures activity. This distinction matters enormously for evidence-based governance: a government using GDP as its primary performance metric is evaluating itself against a measure that goes up when you build a house and also when you demolish it, that rises when pollution is generated as well as when it is remediated, that increases when a fishery is destroyed and its catch sold. GDP is agnostic about whether economic activity makes the nation richer or poorer in any real, physical sense.

The UN's Inclusive Wealth Index provides the empirical evidence for EREM's theoretical critique: across 140 countries tracked over 25 years, 44 — nearly a third — showed declining per-capita inclusive wealth (genuine productive asset stocks) even as their GDP per capita grew. The Dasgupta Review (2021) reached the same conclusion from first principles: overreliance on GDP leads policymakers to substitute income for wealth, systematically undervaluing the natural capital on which all economic activity depends.

EREM's response is to replace GDP — a flow measure of monetary transactions — with TNW, a stock measure of physical productive capacity expressed in SI energy units (megajoules). The choice of energy units is not arbitrary: energy is the universal prerequisite for all physical work. Every material transformation, every unit of food production, every hour of human labour requires energy as its enabling substrate. Denominating all wealth components in energy units gives the TNW formula dimensional consistency that GDP's monetary aggregation cannot achieve.

### 2.2 The TNW Formula

Total National Wealth is computed as the weighted sum of five physically-grounded components:

TNW = (0.40 x NEW) + (0.25 x MWI) + (0.20 x FEW) + (0.10 x HLC) + (0.05 x IEF)

Where NEW = National Energy Wealth; MWI = Material Wealth Index; FEW = Food Energy Wealth; HLC = Human Labour Capacity; and IEF = Infrastructure Efficiency Factor. All components reduce to megajoule equivalents, giving the formula dimensional consistency (proof: NEW [kg] x [J/kg] = [J]; FEW [m2] x [J/m2/yr] x [yr] = [J]; all components reduce to energy units).

Each component encodes a physically motivated hierarchy. NEW (40%) captures both finite energy reserves (coal, gas, petroleum, uranium) and renewable energy infrastructure, weighted by Energy Quality Factors (Q-factors) that encode controllability, energy density, transportability, and waste profile. Nuclear energy is the Q-factor reference standard (Q=1.00); solar and wind receive lower Q-factors (Q=0.58 and 0.65 respectively) reflecting their intermittency and lower controllability.

The Material Wealth Index (25%) captures proven reserves of critical materials weighted by their industrial versatility and accessibility — platinum group metals with high versatility scores (V=16-18) receive a scarcity multiplier of 10x, reflecting their role as irreplaceable industrial catalysts. Food Energy Wealth (20%) converts agricultural productive capacity into energy-equivalent joules. Human Labour Capacity (10%) links demographic structure and nutritional security to productive energy flow. Infrastructure Efficiency Factor (5%) rewards efficiency innovation by measuring output per unit of thermodynamic minimum energy input.

### 2.3 The RBCU Currency Mechanism

The Resource-Backed Currency Unit (RBCU) anchors monetary supply to TNW through a conservative backing ratio:

Total RBCU issued = 0.85 x TNW

1 RBCU = (TNW / Total_RBCU) megajoules of real wealth

The 85% backing ratio provides a 15% buffer against measurement uncertainty in large-scale geophysical estimation. Currency can only expand when one of four physical conditions is satisfied: new reserves are discovered and independently verified; renewable energy infrastructure is installed and operating; infrastructure efficiency measurably improves; or population growth is matched by proportional food production increase. Under EREM, resource depletion automatically contracts the currency supply — the depletion is immediately visible in the monetary system, not externalised into a future generations' accounting problem.

## 3. Australia's TNW: An Indicative Assessment

### 3.1 National Energy Wealth (NEW)

Australia holds energy reserves of exceptional scale and quality across multiple resource categories. The Geoscience Australia (2024) data indicates: coal reserves (primarily in Queensland and New South Wales) of approximately 150 billion tonnes; natural gas reserves (including the North West Shelf and Browse Basin) of approximately 3.8 trillion cubic metres; uranium reserves of approximately 1.7 million tonnes of recoverable uranium oxide (the world's third-largest, behind Kazakhstan and Canada); and renewable energy infrastructure totalling approximately 50 GW of installed capacity as of early 2025, with government targets significantly exceeding this.

Applying the EREM NEW formula with Q-factor weightings (coal Q=0.42 at 24 MJ/kg; gas Q=0.68 at 55 MJ/kg; uranium Q=1.00 at 8.2x10^7 MJ/kg; renewable at weighted Q=0.62 for the installed mix):

NEW_coal ~ 150e9 t x 1000 kg/t x 24 MJ/kg x 0.42 ~ 1.51e15 MJ

NEW_gas ~ 3.8e12 m3 x 0.74 kg/m3 x 55 MJ/kg x 0.68 ~ 1.05e14 MJ

NEW_uranium ~ 1.7e9 kg x 8.2e7 MJ/kg x 1.00 ~ 1.39e17 MJ

NEW_renewable ~ 50e9 W x 0.62 x 0.35 CF x 8760 hr/yr x 25 yr ~ 2.37e15 MJ

NEW_total ~ 1.39e17 MJ  (dominated by uranium endowment)

The uranium endowment is the dominant component of Australia's NEW, a direct consequence of EREM's Q-factor framework assigning nuclear fuel the highest energy quality score. This is physically justified: uranium-235 contains approximately 3.4 million times more energy per kilogram than coal at Q-weighted values. Australia's position as the world's third-largest uranium reserve holder is, under EREM, a vastly more significant wealth asset than conventional mineral sector valuation (in USD/tonne terms) suggests.

### 3.2 Material Wealth Index (MWI)

Australia's material wealth extends far beyond energy resources. The nation holds globally significant reserves of: iron ore (approximately 52 billion tonnes, the world's largest); bauxite/aluminium (the world's second-largest at approximately 20 billion tonnes); nickel (substantial deposits in Western Australia); lithium (the world's largest reserves, approximately 6.3 million tonnes of Li metal equivalent); cobalt (world-class deposits); rare earth elements (significant deposits in Western Australia and South Australia); and rutile and zircon (the world's largest deposits).

Applying the EREM MWI formula with Tier classification — lithium (V=12, A=0.65, Tier 2, multiplier 3.0), cobalt (V=10, A=0.55, Tier 2, multiplier 3.0), rare earths including neodymium (V=15, A=0.35, Tier 2, multiplier 3.0), and applying Tier 1 for iron ore (V=9, A=0.85, multiplier 1.0) — Australia's material wealth index is substantial, particularly in the Tier 2 battery and technology metals that are critical to global energy transition infrastructure.

Australia's MWI contribution is arguably its most strategically leveraged resource position: the nation holds dominant global positions in precisely the materials (lithium, cobalt, nickel, rare earths) that are essential to decarbonisation technology. Under EREM, these materials carry high versatility and scarcity multipliers, making them disproportionate contributors to TNW relative to their conventional commodity value.

### 3.3 Food Energy Wealth (FEW) and Human Labour Capacity (HLC)

Australia's agricultural productive capacity is substantial relative to its population, with approximately 46 million hectares of arable and permanent cropland and significant additional livestock-supporting land. The FEW formula applies soil quality factors (0.65-0.85 for Australian conditions, reflecting the relatively low average soil nutrient density), water availability factors (0.55-0.80, reflecting the highly variable rainfall distribution), and climate stability factors (0.80-0.90 for most agricultural regions).

Australia's HLC is relatively modest in absolute terms given its population of approximately 27 million (2024), but high in per-capita terms: a well-nourished, highly health-resourced, majority working-age population with energy security delivers high labour energy equivalent. The HLC nutrition link is empirically strong in Australia — food insecurity is concentrated in specific regional and Indigenous communities but is not a population-level constraint on labour productivity.

### 3.4 Infrastructure Efficiency Factor (IEF)

Australia's IEF reflects both the strengths and inefficiencies of its energy and infrastructure systems. High efficiency in some industrial processes (world-class liquefied natural gas facilities, efficient grain handling) is offset by relatively low grid efficiency (coal-heavy generation mix, long transmission distances), high transport energy intensity (large freight distances, heavy reliance on road and air), and industrial processes reflecting the energy-abundance of a resource economy that has historically had little price incentive to optimise. The IEF improvement trajectory — as the energy mix decarbonises and industrial efficiency investment accelerates under UCN nuclear and grid reform policies — is positive, contributing to ongoing TNW growth without additional resource extraction.

### 3.5 Indicative TNW and RBCU Supply

An indicative calculation of Australia's TNW, acknowledging the substantial uncertainties in MWI conversion factors and FEW/HLC estimation:

TNW ~ (0.40 x 1.39e17) + (0.25 x MWI_est) + (0.20 x FEW_est) + (0.10 x HLC_est) + (0.05 x IEF_adj)

TNW ~ 5.56e16 MJ (approximate, energy-dominated)

RBCU supply ~ 0.85 x 5.56e16 ~ 4.73e16 RBCU

Per capita TNW ~ 5.56e16 / 27e6 ~ 2.06e9 MJ/person

The headline finding is that under EREM, Australia's true national wealth — dominated by its uranium and critical minerals endowment, augmented by its agricultural capacity and human capital — is orders of magnitude larger relative to its population than conventional GDP-based metrics suggest. GDP per capita (approximately AUD 90,000 in 2024) reflects current annual transaction flows; TNW per capita reflects the actual physical wealth stock that underlies and enables those transactions. The gap between them is the invisible subsidy of resource wealth that Australia's economy has historically consumed without accounting for.

*UCN-EREM Finding: Under EREM, Australia is not a middle-income nation with a resource sector. It is one of the world's wealthiest nations per capita — but only if it manages its resource wealth as a sovereign asset rather than liquidating it as current income. The UCN economic framework exists to enforce this distinction.*

## 4. EREM Alignment with UCN Economic Policy

### 4.1 The Wealth Cap and Distributional Policy

The UCN framework's AUD 100M personal wealth cap, developed in Paper II of the UCN Policy Series, is motivated by distributional equity and the concentration of economic and political power. Under EREM, the justification extends to a physical dimension: personal wealth above any sustainable threshold is, in physical terms, a claim on real resources — energy, materials, land — that exceeds any individual's physical capacity to deploy productively. Extreme wealth concentration under a TNW-backed currency is not merely inequitable; it represents a claim on physical national wealth disproportionate to any individual's contribution to creating that wealth through efficiency improvement, resource development, or productive innovation.

EREM's incentive structure aligns precisely with the UCN wealth cap's policy intent. Under RBCU, wealth cannot be accumulated through financial engineering — it can only be created by discovering reserves, improving efficiency, installing renewable capacity, or growing food production. These are productive activities with genuine physical wealth creation. The speculative asset accumulation that drives extreme wealth under fiat systems — real estate bubbles, financial leverage, rent extraction — cannot expand the RBCU supply, making it structurally less rewarding under EREM.

### 4.2 Housing Market Reform

The UCN's citizen-only residential property ownership rule and anti-speculative investment prohibition address a specific fiat currency failure mode: the disconnection of asset prices from physical construction costs through speculative leverage. In a fiat system, cheap credit expands the money supply without expanding the physical housing stock — the same number of dwellings are bid up to higher and higher monetary prices, transferring wealth from buyers to existing holders and from productive investment to unproductive speculation.

Under EREM, this mechanism is structurally blocked. RBCU issuance cannot expand through credit creation absent an increase in physical TNW. The asset value of housing is capped, in EREM's framework, at its energy construction cost — the actual megajoule equivalent of materials, labour, and energy consumed in building it. A house that was 'worth' 1,000 RBCU when built cannot become worth 10,000 RBCU through speculative leverage without a corresponding increase in TNW to back the expanded currency. The speculative premium is not fundable under EREM — there is simply no currency available to pay it.

### 4.3 Sovereign Resource Management

The UCN's sovereign resource management framework — treating critical minerals, uranium, agricultural land, and water as sovereign strategic assets whose extraction is managed for long-term national benefit rather than immediate revenue maximisation — is the natural policy complement to EREM's monetary framework. Under EREM, resource depletion automatically contracts the currency supply. Rapid liquidation of Australia's uranium or lithium reserves would, under EREM, cause deflation — the currency supply would shrink as NEW contracted, making every existing RBCU more valuable.

This EREM deflation mechanism is precisely the monetary incentive structure the UCN needs to enforce sovereign resource management. Under fiat, the government can deplete resources rapidly, collect the royalties, and spend them — the currency does not register the depletion. Under EREM, depletion is immediately visible in the monetary system, creating a genuine incentive for conservation and managed extraction that requires no separate regulatory framework to produce.

### 4.4 Research, Innovation, and the IEF Premium

The UCN Research Architecture (Paper V of the UCN Policy Series) creates a public knowledge mandate and a directed research obligation that generates an unprecedented volume of publicly-available innovation. Under EREM, this investment has direct monetary consequences: every improvement in industrial efficiency captured in the IEF component increases TNW and authorises RBCU expansion. A UCN nation that systematically invests in efficiency research is not merely improving productivity — it is expanding its currency-backing capacity through physical means.

The IEF component of EREM is the framework's 'innovation premium' — the explicit reward for doing more with less energy. At a 5% weight in the TNW formula, IEF is relatively modest in absolute terms, but its trajectory matters enormously. An economy that doubles its IEF over 30 years (plausible given historical rates of efficiency improvement in industrial and energy systems) adds directly to TNW and RBCU supply, creating a genuine monetary incentive for efficiency innovation that is entirely absent from fiat systems where efficiency gains increase profits but do not expand monetary backing.

### 4.5 Cryptocurrency Trade Architecture and RBCU

The UCN's international trade framework (Paper VII) proposes cryptocurrency settlement as a mechanism for insulating UCN trade from weaponised financial infrastructure (US dollar sanctions, SWIFT exclusions). The RBCU is not a cryptocurrency in the Bitcoin sense — it is not decentralised, algorithmically capped, or proof-of-work secured. However, the RBCU shares the key property that the UCN's cryptocurrency framework values most: it cannot be manipulated by any external actor. An RBCU's value derives from Australia's physical resource endowment, not from the Federal Reserve's interest rate decisions or the US Treasury's sanctions regime. No party can devalue the RBCU by printing money, because the RBCU cannot be printed — it can only be issued against verified physical wealth.

The UCN trade architecture proposes a UCN Sovereign Digital Trade Token backed by a basket of primary commodity exports. Under EREM, this token is not a departure from the RBCU framework but a natural expression of it: a commodity-backed export settlement instrument is a liquid, internationally-tradeable representation of TNW, with exchange rates determined by per-capita TNW differentials rather than central bank policy. The EREM trade valuation formula — incorporating entropy loss factors for shipping — directly addresses the thermodynamic cost of globalised trade that conventional trade accounting renders invisible.

## 5. UCN-EREM Implementation Pathway

### 5.1 Integration with the Four-Phase UCN Roadmap

The UCN Implementation Roadmap (Paper VIII) proposes a four-phase transition spanning approximately 12 years. EREM implementation is not an additional phase layered onto this roadmap; it is the monetary infrastructure that the roadmap's institutional reforms are building toward. The alignment is as follows:

- Phase One (Years 1-3): Establish the national resource audit infrastructure. The Non-Partisan Budget Modelling Department (NPBMD) — already the first Phase One priority in the UCN Roadmap — takes on the additional mandate of developing TNW estimation methodology. The National Research Repository and the Government Pharmaceutical Corporation establish the data infrastructure (resource verification, renewable capacity registries, efficiency baselines) needed for TNW calculation. No currency change is attempted; this phase builds measurement capability.
- Phase Two (Years 3-6): Issue RBCU alongside the Australian dollar, initially for commodity and resource transactions. Pilot bilateral RBCU trade settlements with consenting United Commonwealth partners (UK and Canada) and selected international partners. Publish quarterly TNW estimates through the NPBMD. Establish independent audit institutions for reserve verification with international methodology standards. Market-determined AUD/RBCU exchange rate provides a real-time test of EREM's valuation relative to fiat.
- Phase Three (Years 6-10): Expand RBCU usage to government transactions, tax collection, and bond issuance. The constitutional reserve audit mechanism is entrenched as part of the modular constitution (a Tier Two provision). As verification confidence grows, the backing ratio is held constant at 0.85 while TNW grows from nuclear infrastructure installation, renewable build-out, and efficiency improvements. RBCU becomes mandatory for all resource export transactions.
- Phase Four (Years 10-12+): RBCU becomes the primary UCN currency. The Australian dollar is phased out over a scheduled period. The NPBMD's mandate is formally redefined from inflation targeting to TNW stewardship. International exchange rates are determined by per-capita TNW ratios.

### 5.2 The Reserve Audit Institution

The most critical implementation challenge identified in the EREM Technical Specification is the political economy of the reserve audit institution: the body responsible for independently verifying TNW components, whose reports authorise RBCU issuance. The UCN framework's commitment to independent, non-partisan oversight bodies makes it better positioned than most governance systems to design a credible reserve audit institution.

The UCN Sovereign Wealth and Resource Audit Commission (SWRAC) is proposed as a constitutionally-independent body with the same design principles as the NPBMD: fixed non-renewable terms, funding formula entrenched in the constitution, published methodologies, open data, independent academic review of all estimates. The SWRAC reports to the parliament, not the executive, and its TNW estimates are the binding input to the RBCU issuance formula. An executive that wishes to expand the money supply cannot do so by pressuring the SWRAC — it can only do so by building renewable capacity, discovering reserves, or improving efficiency.

## 6. EREM in the United Commonwealth Context

### 6.1 Combined TNW of the United Commonwealth Three

The United Commonwealth of Australia, Canada, and the United Kingdom would represent, under EREM, one of the world's most substantial combined TNW entities. Canada holds the world's third-largest oil reserves (approximately 170 billion barrels), among the world's largest uranium reserves (second globally, approximately 560,000 tonnes), extraordinary freshwater endowments (approximately 20% of the world's surface freshwater), and globally significant agricultural capacity. The United Kingdom holds world-class research and human capital, North Sea hydrocarbon reserves, and significant offshore wind capacity.

The combined TNW of the three nations — were they to coordinate under a United Commonwealth EREM framework — would represent a genuinely multi-domain resource base: Australia's uranium and critical minerals, Canada's oil and freshwater, and the UK's human capital and efficiency infrastructure. The per-capita TNW of the combined entity would be among the highest in the world, supporting a combined RBCU supply of extraordinary backing depth.

### 6.2 United Commonwealth RBCU Exchange Architecture

The EREM international exchange rate formula — Exchange_rate(A/B) = per-capita TNW_A / per-capita TNW_B — creates a non-manipulable exchange architecture between United Commonwealth member nations' RBCU variants. Where all three nations adopt EREM, their bilateral exchange rates are determined by their respective physical productive capacities, not by monetary policy decisions. A United Commonwealth that coordinates TNW audit methodology and RBCU issuance standards creates the world's first currency zone grounded in physical rather than financial architecture.

This is not currency union. Each nation issues its own RBCU variant (AUD-RBCU, CAD-RBCU, GBP-RBCU) backed by its own TNW. But the exchange rates between them are determined by the same formula, the same methodology, and the same audit standards — making them transparent, comparable, and immune to competitive devaluation. The United Commonwealth EREM framework is the monetary expression of the same principle that animates the United Commonwealth's political architecture: genuine equality of status, grounded in physical reality rather than political convention.

## 7. Limitations and Open Research Questions

The EREM framework at v1.0 carries acknowledged theoretical limitations that the UCN must address before implementation. The most significant are:

- Weighting coefficient calibration: The TNW weights (0.40, 0.25, 0.20, 0.10, 0.05) are theoretically motivated priors, not empirically validated optima. Cross-country calibration across diverse economic structures is required. The UCN's SWRAC and NPBMD should commission this as a priority research program in Phase One.
- Knowledge capital and institutional quality: EREM's IEF component partially captures knowledge capital through efficiency, but institutional quality, human capital depth, and scientific capacity are not fully represented. The UCN Research Architecture generates precisely the data needed to develop knowledge-capital extensions to EREM — a virtuous research loop.
- Q-factor dynamics: As battery storage technology improves, the controllability penalty for renewable energy decreases, raising Q-factors for solar and wind over time. A dynamic Q-factor model incorporating technology learning curves should be developed within Phase One data infrastructure build.
- Ecosystem services: EREM v1.0 does not explicitly account for ecosystem services — carbon sequestration, water purification, biodiversity, soil regeneration — beyond their indirect effect on FEW factors. Australia's ecosystem services represent a significant fraction of genuine national wealth. A UCN Ecosystem Services Wealth component should be developed for EREM v2.0.
- Transition volatility: The transition from AUD to RBCU will involve a period of parallel currency operation and potential exchange rate volatility. The UCN's NPBMD must model this transition risk explicitly, and Phase Two should include circuit-breaker mechanisms to prevent destructive feedback between exchange rate volatility and TNW estimation uncertainty.

## 8. Conclusion: The Honest Monetary Foundation

The UCN framework asks of its citizens an unusual thing: it asks them to accept short-term disruption in exchange for long-term benefit, and to trust that the evidence justifies the exchange. This trust is not possible if the monetary system cannot be trusted to tell the truth about the national wealth those disruptions are intended to build and protect.

EREM provides that honest monetary foundation. A currency whose supply is determined by the physical reality of the nation's energy, material, food, and human wealth — not by a committee's assessment of political conditions — is a currency that cannot lie. It cannot be inflated to pay debts the nation has not earned. It cannot expand to conceal resource depletion. It cannot reward financial engineering at the expense of genuine production. It is not a perfect system — no system is, and EREM's own authors acknowledge the open empirical questions clearly. But it is the most physically honest monetary framework in existence, and it is the appropriate monetary architecture for a governance system whose central commitment is that evidence should determine policy.

Frederick Soddy wrote in 1926 that debts do not rot with old age while physical wealth is consumed by living. Nearly a century later, that asymmetry continues to corrupt the monetary systems of every nation on Earth. The UCN, by adopting EREM as its monetary foundation, becomes the first governance system to take Soddy seriously — to insist that the numbers on the currency reflect the wealth in the ground, the sun in the panels, the grain in the paddocks, and the intelligence of the people. Not what the treasurer wishes were true. What is.

*UCN Founding Principle on Money: A nation's currency should represent its wealth, not its aspirations. Real wealth is energy, matter, and the knowledge to transform them efficiently. The RBCU is the currency of real wealth.*
