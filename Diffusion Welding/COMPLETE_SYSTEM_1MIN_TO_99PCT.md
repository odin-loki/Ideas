## Complete Process Flow - 99% Protocol (Recommended)

### Using 250°C / 1 hour regime:

**PREPARATION (30 minutes)**
1. Surface preparation (15 min)
   - Wire brush to roughen surface
   - Degrease with acetone
   - Final wipe with IPA

2. Substrate application (5 min)
   - Apply ionic liquid substrate paste
   - 0.2-0.3 mm thickness
   - Ensure complete coverage

3. Fixture setup (10 min)
   - Position parts
   - Apply moderate pressure (20-30 MPa)
   - Attach electrical leads
   - Position ultrasonic horn

**BONDING (60 minutes)**
4. Heat to 250°C (10 min)
   - Ramp gradually
   - Monitor temperature uniformly

5. Activate bonding (60 min at 250°C)
   - Turn on DC power (3-5V, 200 A/m²)
   - Activate ultrasonic (2 W/cm², continuous)
   - Monitor current (should gradually decrease)
   - Maintain temperature ±5°C

**POST-PROCESSING (50 minutes)**
6. Post-bond anneal (30 min at 250°C)
   - CRITICAL STEP for 99% strength
   - Turn off current and ultrasonic
   - Hold at temperature
   - Allows grain reorganization and stress relief

7. Cool down (15 min)
   - Controlled cooling to <100°C
   - Release pressure only after cooling

8. Cleanup and inspection (5 min)
   - Remove excess substrate
   - Visual inspection
   - Optional: NDT verification

**TOTAL TIME: 2 hours 20 minutes**

## Safety & Environmental

### Safety Considerations

**Chemical Hazards:**
- Ionic liquids: Low toxicity, eye irritation possible
- Gallium: Stains skin (harmless), don't ingest
- Organometallics: Handled in sealed substrate, minimal exposure
- Standard PPE: Safety glasses, gloves

**Electrical Hazards:**
- Low voltage (3-5V) - minimal shock risk
- High current (up to 8000 A/m²) - proper insulation required
- GFCI protection recommended

**Thermal Hazards:**
- ULTRA-FLASH: 150°C - standard burn precautions
- ULTRA-99%: 250-300°C - furnace-grade protection
- All regimes: Allow cooling before handling

**Ultrasonic Hazards:**
- Hearing protection (20-40 kHz audible to some)
- No direct skin contact with operating horn
- Standard industrial ultrasonic safety

**Overall:** Much safer than fusion welding
- No UV radiation
- No toxic fumes (unless substrate overheated)
- No molten metal
- No high voltage
