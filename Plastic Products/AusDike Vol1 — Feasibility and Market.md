## 4. Product Concept — AusDike™ — The Design

A modular, self-ballasting, rapid-deploy flood-levee system. Two people. No tools. No machinery. Deployed in minutes from flat-pack storage. Final specification **D = 560 mm**.

### How it works — the physics

The fundamental insight behind AusDike™ is **using the floodwater itself as ballast**. The panel has an open bottom with a perimeter TPE ground seal. As floodwater rises on the protected side, it simultaneously enters through the open base of the panel and fills the interior cavity.

This means the pressure pushing against the front face is **almost entirely counteracted** by equal pressure inside the cell. Only the difference in water level — the "head difference" — creates net lateral force. In simulations this reduced the effective overturning force by **73 %** compared to a sealed panel design.

The more water, the heavier the panel. The heavier the panel, the harder it is to move. **The flood actively stabilises the barrier that is stopping it.** This is the same principle used by the commercial Boxwall system, but achieved with a fraction of the material cost via injection moulding.

### Six key features

1. **Open-bottom self-ballasting** — Floodwater fills cell from beneath. 73 % net force reduction.
2. **Side interlocking tabs** — 2 male trapezoidal tabs / 2 female recesses per panel side. Tool-free connection.
3. **Dovetail stacking tongue** — Mechanically locked vertical stack. Cannot separate under load.
4. **560 mm base depth** — Wider than it is tall. Gravity-stable 2-high stack without bracing.
5. **100 % recycled polypropylene** — Post-consumer / post-industrial feedstock. Consistent with Geohex and Biax.
6. **Flat-pack storage** — Panels nest. 50 m barrier fits one standard pallet.

## 7. Manufacturing — Production Feasibility

AusDike™ is designed from the ground up for injection moulding. All geometry decisions — wall thickness, draft angles, fillets, tongue profiles — were made with 2-part tooling in mind.

### Mould design

The panel is designed for a **2-part injection mould** (core and cavity) with the pull direction vertical — top to bottom. This means no side actions are required for the main body. The interlocking tabs on the left and right edges are oriented so the mould can close and open vertically without undercuts.

The dovetail tongue on the top edge requires **1.5° draft on the dovetail faces** — achievable with standard tooling. The female groove on the bottom is simply a recessed negative of the tongue, moulded in the same pull direction.

The front-face button-socket for vertical stack locking is a small boss (15 mm diameter, 8 mm tall) — standard injection-moulding geometry with no special tooling considerations.

### Tooling consistency with existing products

Holloway's existing Geohex tooling produces interlocking hexagonal cells of similar wall thickness (8 mm) and recycled PP material. The AusDike™ panel is **larger in footprint but simpler in geometry** than Geohex. The same PP processing parameters, gate locations, and runner-design principles apply. No new material handling or processing capability is required.

### Tooling requirements

- **2-part tool** — core and cavity. No side actions required for main body.
- **Pull direction** — vertical (top to bottom). 2° draft on all vertical faces.
- **Dovetail faces** — 1.5° draft. Within standard tooling capability.
- **Cooling channels** — required in core around rib intersections (thick wall junctions).
- **Gate location** — centre of top face, fan gate to distribute flow through ribs.

### Manufacturing verdict

AusDike™ is manufacturable on Holloway's existing equipment with a new mould tool. **No new capital equipment, no new materials, no new process knowledge required.** The panel's geometry is simpler than Geohex and uses the same recycled PP supply chain as Biax and Geohex. Estimated tooling investment: **$80,000–$140,000 AUD** for a production-grade 2-cavity mould. Unit economics will be competitive at volumes above ~500 panels / run.
