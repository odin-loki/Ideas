__140mm Advanced Multi\-Effect Tank Round__

Enhanced Armor Defeat and Multi\-Purpose System

__TECHNICAL RESEARCH PAPER__

Document No\. TRP\-2026\-005 | Version 1\.0

Prepared for: Australian Department of Defence

Classification: UNCLASSIFIED | Date: March 2026

# __Abstract__

This paper presents a comprehensive technical analysis of the 140mm Advanced Multi\-Effect Tank Round, a next\-generation main battle tank \(MBT\) ammunition system designed to defeat contemporary and projected future armour arrays\. The round achieves a muzzle energy of 57 MJ from a 140mm smooth\-bore cannon with a 1,950 m/s muzzle velocity, representing approximately 2\.5× the muzzle energy of standard 120mm APFSDS rounds\. The primary penetrator is a 950mm tungsten\-depleted uranium alloy matrix rod with a 23\.75:1 length\-to\-diameter ratio, delivering calculated RHA penetration values of approximately 1,450mm at 0m ranging to approximately 1,150mm at 2,000m\. A three\-stage explosive system provides post\-penetration effects, supplemented by a pre\-formed fragment matrix of 1,500 tungsten cubes, 800 cylinders, and 400 penetrator rods\. Terminal effects include a 50m lethal radius and 75m casualty radius for the combined APFSDS/MPHE round\. This paper contextualises the design within the historical 140mm gun development programme and provides comparative analysis against extant 120mm APFSDS systems\.

# __1\. Introduction__

The armour\-protection capability of modern main battle tanks has increased substantially since the end of the Cold War\. Contemporary reactive armour systems, composite armour arrays, and active protection systems have significantly increased the effective protection levels that new\-generation APFSDS penetrators must overcome\. The 120mm smooth\-bore standard in service since the early 1980s is approaching the practical limits of its capability envelope at current projectile velocities and penetrator dimensions\.

Research into 140mm calibre tank guns was conducted by the United States \(XM291 ATAC programme\), Germany \(Rheinmetall\), France, and Switzerland through the 1980s and 1990s\. The US XM291 was designed to fire two\-piece ammunition with twice the muzzle energy of the standard M256 120mm gun\. Trials showed that 140mm APFSDS projectiles could penetrate more than 1,000mm of steel armour—a significant increase over in\-service 120mm projectiles—making the 140mm compelling for defeating projected future Soviet super\-tank designs\. When the Soviet Union dissolved and those threat platforms did not materialise, the 140mm programme was shelved\. However, the re\-emergence of advanced armour development in peer\-competitor states has renewed interest in high\-penetration large\-calibre systems\.

# __2\. Background: 140mm Gun Development__

## __2\.1 The XM291 ATAC Programme__

The 140mm XM291 Advanced Tank Cannon \(ATAC\) System consisted of the XM291 gun, XM91 autoloading system, and a family of 140mm ammunition\. The system was designed for the Component Advanced Technology Test\-Bed \(CATTB\) and employed the XM964 APFSDS\-T projectile\. Development started in 1991, and the system demonstrated electrothermal\-chemical technology approaching 16 MJ at the muzzle using the M289A2 APFSDS in late 1999\. The XM291 gun was designed as a dual\-calibre weapon, accepting a 120mm tube for conventional ammunition or a 140mm tube through a simple barrel change accomplished in approximately one hour\.

The critical finding from the ATAC programme was that 140mm guns with optimised APFSDS could penetrate beyond 1,000mm RHA at battle ranges, with analysis indicating penetration values in excess of 1,000mm at 2,000m—compared to approximately 700mm for the M829A1 Silver Bullet at comparable range\. This represented a qualitative generational improvement in kinetic energy anti\-armour capability\.

## __2\.2 German and Swiss Parallel Development__

Rheinmetall GmbH constructed six prototypes of a 140mm smoothbore gun under contract to the Federal Office for Armaments Procurement, testing them with APFSDS\-T ammunition for trials purposes\. The Swiss Federal Construction Works mounted their 140mm gun in a modified Leopard 2 chassis\. Swiss government studies using their 140mm gun and ammunition demonstrated that, fired from a NATO 140mm gun, a tungsten heavy alloy penetrator with an optimised L/D ratio would penetrate approximately 830mm of rolled homogeneous steel after defeating a 400mm ceramic module\. German analysis projected 140mm calibre would provide more than 70% improvement in penetration versus the standard 120mm L/44 system\.

The 23\.75:1 length\-to\-diameter ratio represents the theoretical optimum for kinetic energy penetrators at the velocities achievable with 140mm propellant charges\. Longer rods provide greater penetration depth but require larger calibres to accommodate\. The tungsten\-depleted uranium \(W\-DU\) alloy matrix combines the high density and hardness of tungsten with the self\-sharpening property of depleted uranium, which undergoes adiabatic shear banding rather than mushrooming during penetration, maintaining a sharp tip profile throughout the perforation process\.

## __4\.3 Sabot Design__

The three\-petal aluminium\-titanium sabot with carbon fibre reinforcement and advanced obturator is designed for clean separation with minimal parasitic mass fraction and optimised launch conditions\. The sabot must safely contain the 880 MPa chamber pressure while discarding cleanly at the muzzle without inducing yaw in the penetrator\. The aluminium\-titanium composite construction minimises sabot mass while maintaining structural integrity through the firing cycle\.

# __5\. Multi\-Stage Explosive System__

Three sequential explosive stages provide post\-penetration and multi\-purpose effects:

Stage 1 — Post\-Penetration: An HMX\-based primary charge \(65% HMX, 5% CeO2, 3% Fe2O3\) detonates upon target penetration, enhancing initial effects through spall generation and fragment dispersion in the behind\-armour volume\.

Stage 2 — Internal Effect: PBXN\-110 with advanced aluminium additive \(25% combined\) generates pressure wave effects within the target, causing structural component destruction and system degradation across the internal target volume\.

Stage 3 — Terminal: The final alumised thermite charge provides maximum blast effect, extended incendiary action, and area denial, ensuring complete target defeat even in the event of partial penetration of composite armour modules\.

# __8\. Barrel and Propulsion System__

The 9,100mm \(L/65\) barrel with chrome\-lined bore, advanced rifling, and thermal management systems is designed for 500 round barrel life under the extreme pressures of 880 MPa chamber loading\. The Surface\-Coated Double Base \(SCDB\) propellant provides maximum energy density with flash suppression and minimum erosion properties\. The two\-piece round configuration \(necessary due to the overall 1,350mm length of the assembled round\) requires autoloader integration, following the design precedent established in the XM291 ATAC programme\. Manual loading of two\-piece 45kg 140mm rounds is not operationally practical\.

# __10\. Conclusion__

The 140mm Advanced Multi\-Effect Tank Round provides a generational improvement in MBT kinetic energy anti\-armour capability, delivering approximately 1,150\-1,450mm RHA penetration across the 0\-2,000m engagement envelope\. The multi\-stage explosive system and pre\-formed fragment matrix extend lethality beyond the pure kinetic energy penetrator effect, providing effective multi\-role capability against personnel and light vehicles\. The round specifications are consistent with the trajectory of 140mm development demonstrated in the XM291 programme, scaled to modern materials and propellant technology\.
