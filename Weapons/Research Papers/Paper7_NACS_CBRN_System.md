# NEXUS Adaptive Combat System — CBRN Camouflage and Undersuit: 72-Hour Sealed Warfare Operations Ensemble

*Technical Research Paper*

Document No. TRP-2026-007 | Version 1.0

Prepared for: Australian Department of Defence

Classification: UNCLASSIFIED | Date: March 2026

## Abstract
This paper presents a technical analysis of the NEXUS Adaptive Combat System Total \(NACS-TOTAL\), a 72-hour sealed Chemical, Biological, Radiological, and Nuclear \(CBRN\) protection ensemble designed for sustained military operations in contaminated environments. The system comprises four integrated components: the CORE undersuit based on Gore-Tex CHEMPAK composite fabric, the SHIELD overgarment with 65-92% infrared emission reduction, the GHOST Rebreather providing 72-hour oxygen delivery via dual 300-bar cylinders with lithium hydroxide CO₂ scrubbing, and the SEAL peripheral protection suite \(gloves, socks, balaclava\). The system is supplemented by a pharmaceutical support protocol incorporating cognitive enhancement agents for sustained operations. Total unit cost is approximately AUD$504 at 50,000-unit procurement volume. This paper examines the materials science and human factors basis for each subsystem and addresses doctrine implications for sealed CBRN operations.

## 1. Introduction

CBRN threats represent a critical operational challenge for modern military forces. The threat of chemical agent use has been demonstrated in multiple recent conflicts, including the documented use of chlorine and nerve agent compounds in the Syrian civil war. Biological agents, though subject to international prohibition, remain a proliferation concern. Radiological contamination scenarios arise from both weapons use and infrastructure damage \(Fukushima-type scenarios\). Effective CBRN protection requires a complete body seal against vapour, liquid, and particulate agents while maintaining sufficient mobility and endurance for combat operations.

Current military CBRN ensemble standards, including the US Joint Service Lightweight Integrated Suit Technology \(JSLIST\) and UK CBRN Individual Protection Equipment \(IPE\), provide 24-hour protection windows before physiological heat stress and material saturation require suit exchange. The 72-hour continuous protection target of the NACS-TOTAL represents a significant extension of this capability, driven by scenarios where decontamination facilities or suit exchange may not be accessible.

## 2. CORE Undersuit

### 2.1 Gore-Tex CHEMPAK Composite

Gore-Tex CHEMPAK is a multi-layer activated carbon-sphere fabric system developed for CBRN protection. The activated carbon spheres, impregnated throughout the polyurethane-bonded fabric matrix, adsorb chemical agent vapours before they can permeate to the skin surface. The composite construction incorporates a moisture-vapour-permeable \(MVP\) outer shell for sweat management, a CHEMPAK carbon sphere adsorption layer, and an inner comfort liner.

The CHEMPAK designation indicates chemical agent protective capacity meeting NATO STANAG 2826 requirements for vapour challenge at mustard agent \(HD\), nerve agent \(GB, GD, VX\), and lewisite \(L\) levels. Breathable construction reduces heat stress compared to impermeable rubber suits, extending wear duration before physiological heat strain limits are reached.

### 2.2 72-Hour Wear Duration

Achieving 72-hour wear duration requires managing four physiological challenges: thermal stress from reduced sweat evaporation, accumulated carbon sphere saturation, moisture management in the undersuit microclimate, and psychological factors from sustained enclosure. The CHEMPAK layer is designed with sufficient carbon sphere surface area for 72-hour accumulation of sweat vapour and incidental CBRN agent exposure without breakthrough. The moisture-wicking base layer maintains skin dryness to prevent maceration and associated skin integrity compromise.

## 3. SHIELD Overgarment — Infrared Signature Reduction

### 3.1 IR Signature Management

The SHIELD overgarment achieves 65-92% reduction in infrared emission across the 3-5 μm and 8-12 μm thermal IR bands through a combination of low-emissivity fabric treatment and thermal isolation layers. Human body surface temperature \(approximately 33-35°C\) radiates primarily in the 8-14 μm band. Low-emissivity \(low-ε\) metallic treatments \(emissivity 0.05-0.15 vs. 0.9-0.95 for untreated fabric\) reduce radiative emission by the factor \(1-ε\), providing the stated 65-92% reduction range depending on activity level and ambient temperature differential.

IR signature reduction is critical against thermal imaging surveillance systems including ground-based IR cameras, aerial UAV sensor payloads, and guided weapon seekers operating in thermal IR bands. The 65-92% reduction significantly degrades target detection range for typical military thermal imaging systems calibrated to battlefield target contrast levels.

### 4.2 Lithium Hydroxide CO₂ Scrubbing

LiOH-based CO₂ scrubbing is a well-established technology in submarine life support and space applications. The chemical reaction 2LiOH \+ CO₂ → Li₂CO₃ \+ H₂O removes carbon dioxide from exhaled breath and replaces it with supplemental oxygen from the stored cylinders. Military rebreather applications using LiOH have demonstrated effective absorption capacity of approximately 450L CO₂ per kg of LiOH, sufficient for extended duration operations when canister mass is optimised. The zero-breath-vapour-emission specification ensures complete containment of exhaled breath within the rebreather circuit, preventing both CBRN agent backward-contamination risk and visual condensation signatures.

### 4.3 Oxygen Delivery

Dual 300-bar cylinders provide redundancy against single-cylinder failure and capacity for 72-hour operation at human metabolic oxygen demand rates of approximately 0.5 L/min at rest to 2.0 L/min during combat exertion. Cylinder management protocols distribute demand between cylinders to maintain operational reserve. Emergency manual override enables cylinder switching in the event of regulator failure.

## 5. SEAL Peripheral Protection

The SEAL Gloves, Socks, and Balaclava complete the system seal at extremities and head. Each component interfaces with the CORE and SHIELD systems through sealed connection points with captive locking rings, preventing agent ingress at the most vulnerable connection points. Gloves maintain finger dexterity sufficient for weapon manipulation, communications equipment, and medical procedures.

## 6. Pharmaceutical Support Protocol

Extended 72-hour combat operations in full CBRN ensemble impose significant physiological and cognitive demands. The pharmaceutical support protocol incorporates cognitive enhancement agents to maintain operational performance: caffeine for sustained arousal, modafinil for wakefulness maintenance over 72\+ hour periods, and dextroamphetamine for acute cognitive performance restoration when fatigue has accumulated. All agents are employed under medical protocols with dosage supervision.

The use of pharmacological performance enhancement agents in military operations has precedent in multiple major military forces. Amphetamine use in WWII, Vietnam, and Gulf War contexts has been documented. Modafinil \(Provigil\) has been adopted by multiple air forces for extended flight operations. The NACS-TOTAL protocol integrates these agents with physiological monitoring to optimise dosage timing.

## 8. Comparison with Existing CBRN Systems

The US JSLIST system provides 24 hours of chemical agent protection at approximately USD$350 per suit without integrated rebreather capability. The UK Mark 4 CBRN ensemble similarly provides sub-24-hour protection windows. The NACS-TOTAL 72-hour capability with integrated rebreather represents a significant capability extension, at a cost premium justified by scenarios requiring extended sealed operations without access to decontamination facilities. The IR signature reduction of 65-92% also exceeds the 20-40% typical of standard military field uniforms with camouflage treatments.

## 9. Conclusion

The NACS-TOTAL system provides a comprehensive 72-hour CBRN protection capability combining state-of-the-art activated carbon adsorption technology, closed-circuit rebreather life support, and advanced IR signature management in a single integrated ensemble. At approximately $504 per system at scale procurement, it represents a cost-effective solution for units assigned to high-threat CBRN scenarios, where the alternative—mission failure or personnel casualties from CBRN exposure—carries substantially higher costs.
