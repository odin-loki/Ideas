# MP-6.8 Advanced Combat Rifle Mark II: Multi-Purpose Infantry Weapon — Complete Technical Analysis

*Technical Research Paper*

Document No. TRP-2026-010 | Version 1.0

Prepared for: Australian Department of Defence | March 2026

## Abstract
This paper presents a technical analysis of the MP-6.8 Advanced Combat Rifle Mark II, chambered for the 6.8×51mm Advanced Combat Round. The weapon delivers 1,000 m/s muzzle velocity and 4,000 J muzzle energy from a 406.4mm barrel, with 50-round capacity, 800 rpm full-auto cyclic rate, and 12mm RHA penetration at 300m. Weight is 3.4kg unloaded. The dual-action trigger mechanism provides hair-trigger single fire \(2.5 lbs, 3-round burst\) and full engagement auto \(4.5 lbs\) from a single trigger in a novel two-stage firing mode architecture. The enhanced short-stroke gas piston system with three-position regulator provides suppressed, standard, and adverse condition operation. This paper analyses the 6.8×51mm cartridge performance, the dual-action trigger concept, and the thermal management system for sustained fire operations.

## 1. Introduction

The 5.56×45mm NATO standard cartridge adopted in the 1980s has been the infantry rifle calibre for most NATO nations for over four decades. Its selection was driven by manageable recoil, light weight, and adequate performance at the 300m ranges typical of Cold War European and jungle warfare concepts. However, engagement ranges in Afghanistan regularly extended to 400-800m, where the 5.56mm's terminal performance and armour defeat capability were found inadequate against body armour-equipped adversaries.

The US Army's Next Generation Squad Weapon \(NGSW\) programme addressed this concern, selecting the SIG Sauer SPEAR rifle chambered in 6.8×51mm \(277 Fury\) in 2022. The programme requirement was specifically for a weapon capable of defeating Near-Peer Threat \(NPT\) body armour—representing a recognised need for higher-velocity, harder-hitting ammunition than 5.56mm could provide. The MP-6.8 described in this paper draws on the same calibre class, addressing the same operational requirement.

### 3.2 Thermal Management

The combined 24-flute barrel design \(8 primary at optimised depth \+ 16 micro-flutes for surface area optimisation\) provides substantially enhanced cooling compared to standard unfluted barrel designs. The aluminium-magnesium heat sink with integrated cooling fins manages sustained fire thermal loading. Thermal barrier coatings on bore-adjacent surfaces reduce heat transfer rate to the barrel steel, extending time-to-thermal-limit at maximum cyclic rates. Temperature indicator strips provide visible warning of barrel approach to safe sustained fire temperature limits.

The three-position gas regulator accommodates standard, suppressed, and adverse \(fouled/debris\) operating conditions without tools. Suppressed position reduces gas port opening to compensate for the increased back-pressure created by the 200mm suppressor, maintaining bolt velocity within design limits. Adverse position increases gas port opening to ensure reliable cycling in contaminated or worn operating conditions.

## 5. Dual-Action Trigger System

The dual-action trigger is a novel design concept providing two distinct fire modes from a single trigger in each of two safety positions. In safety Position 1 \(combat\), a light first-stage pull \(2.5 lbs, "hair trigger"\) fires a 3-round burst with a short reset, while a full second-stage engagement \(4.5 lbs\) fires full-automatic. In safety Position 2 \(precision\), the same trigger stages deliver single fire on the light pull and 3-round burst on full engagement.

## 7. Magazine System

The 50-round 7075-T6 aluminium magazine with triple spring system \(Inconel X-750 primary, Elgiloy secondary, chrome silicon anti-bind\) provides sustained feeding reliability for the high-energy 6.8×51mm cartridge. Progressive feed angle and optimised feed ramps manage the 1,150g loaded magazine mass \(8g/round × 50 rounds \+ 330g empty\). Witness holes every 5 rounds enable rapid round count assessment.

## 9. Comparison with 5.56mm Systems and the NGSW

The US Army NGSW programme selected the SIG SPEAR in 6.8×51mm following an extensive competitive evaluation, with the stated requirement to defeat near-peer body armour threats at 600m. The programme accepted higher weight and recoil of the 6.8mm cartridge as necessary costs of the required terminal performance. The MP-6.8 is aligned with this assessment—the 4,000 J muzzle energy represents a necessary increase from the 5.56mm standard to address the growing proliferation of advanced body armour in near-peer threat inventories.

## 10. Conclusion

The MP-6.8 Advanced Combat Rifle provides infantry squads with 4,000 J muzzle energy, 12mm RHA penetration at 300m, and 50-round capacity in a 3.4kg weapon with integrated suppressor. The novel dual-action trigger provides flexible fire mode selection without manual selector manipulation. The enhanced thermal management system supports sustained fire at 800 rpm without barrel heat degradation. The 6.8×51mm calibre places the MP-6.8 in the same capability class as the US Army NGSW, providing effective defeat of modern advanced body armour at extended engagement ranges.
