# __Abstract__

This paper presents a comprehensive technical analysis of the 57mm Advanced Mechanical Autocannon System \(AMAS\), an enhanced multi\-purpose combat platform designed for naval and ground\-based defence applications\. The system features a 57×441mm calibre autocannon with a 200\-220 rounds per minute rate of fire, muzzle velocity of 1,350 m/s, and an advanced multi\-stage HEIAP\-T ammunition system incorporating pre\-formed tungsten fragmentation matrices\. The system achieves 140mm RHA penetration and 420g TNT\-equivalent blast effect, with a lethal radius of 25 metres\. This paper contextualises the design within the existing 57mm naval gun development lineage, particularly the BAE Systems/Bofors Mk110 programme, and presents comparative terminal ballistics analysis\. The mechanical\-only operating principle ensures robustness and reliability in electronic warfare environments\. The research concludes that the AMAS represents a significant capability uplift for medium\-calibre direct fire support, with particular relevance to counter\-armour and area denial operations\.

# __1\. Introduction__

Medium\-calibre autocannon systems occupying the 30mm\-100mm range represent a critical capability layer in modern combined arms warfare\. They provide lethality against light armoured vehicles, personnel, and low\-flying aerial threats while remaining compact enough for vehicle and naval mounting\. The 57mm calibre specifically has a long history of development, most notably in the Bofors L/70 family that entered Swedish naval service in the mid\-1960s\.

The BAE Systems 57mm Mk110, internationally designated the Bofors 57Mk3, represents the current state of the art in this calibre class\. As noted by BAE Systems, it "provides high survivability and tactical freedom at all levels of conflict" and is the deck gun of choice for United States Navy Littoral Combat Ships as well as the US Coast Guard National Security Cutter class\. The Mk3 system was designed in 1995 for the Visby\-class corvette and entered service in 2000\.

However, the existing 57mm naval systems primarily optimise for anti\-aircraft and anti\-surface vessel roles\. The system described in this paper—the AMAS—instead targets a ground\-forces application with emphasis on anti\-armour and anti\-personnel multi\-role lethality through a mechanically\-operated, electronics\-independent design philosophy\. This paper analyses the technical basis for the AMAS specification, its ammunition design, and its terminal effects performance\.

# __2\. Background and Related Systems__

## __2\.1 The Bofors 57mm Development Lineage__

The Bofors 57mm SAK L/70 series began production with the Mark 1 variant in 1970, designed as a replacement for the twin\-barrelled 57mm SAK L/60\. The L/70 designation refers to the 70\-calibre barrel length, approximately 4 metres, which provides higher muzzle velocities for dual\-purpose anti\-aircraft and surface roles\. The Mark 2 arrived in 1981 with a lighter mount and improved servo stabilisation systems\. The Mark 3, used as the basis for the US Navy Mk110, introduced advanced programmable 3P \(Pre\-fragmented, Programmable, Proximity\-fused\) ammunition offering six fuzing modes including impact, time, and proximity settings\.

Modern Mk2 and Mk3 variants employ air cooling enabling sustained firing rates of up to 220 rounds per minute, a significant improvement over earlier water\-cooled variants\. The 3P round introduced in 2006 features a pre\-fragmented warhead containing over 8,000 tungsten fragments for enhanced lethality, combined with a multi\-mode electronic time fuze programmable immediately before firing via the gun's fire control system\.

## __2\.2 57mm in Ground Combat Roles__

While the Bofors family is primarily a naval system, 57mm\-class weapons have historical precedent in ground roles\. The Soviet S\-60 57mm anti\-aircraft gun saw extensive service in Korean and Vietnam\-era conflicts in both anti\-aircraft and direct\-fire ground support roles\. The calibre's combination of projectile mass, muzzle velocity, and manageable system weight makes it effective against light armoured vehicles, field fortifications, and personnel\.

Modern HEIAP \(High Explosive Incendiary Armour Piercing\) projectile design at 57mm calibre can incorporate multiple defeat mechanisms in a single round: kinetic penetration, explosive blast, and pre\-formed fragmentation\. The AMAS system exploits these multi\-mechanism effects through a three\-stage terminal effect sequence\.

## __3\.3 Explosive Matrix Composition__

The primary explosive matrix uses a composite formulation combining HMX \(65%\), PBXN\-110 \(20%\), cerium oxide nanoparticles \(5%\), iron oxide nanoparticles \(3%\), advanced aluminium powder \(5%\), and binders/stabilisers \(2%\)\. The use of cerium oxide and iron oxide nanomaterials is informed by research into reactive material additives that enhance incendiary and blast effects through metal oxide thermite reactions post\-detonation\. This formulation achieves a calculated TNT equivalence of approximately 420g through the combined heat of reaction\.

# __4\. Three\-Stage Terminal Effects__

## __4\.1 Multi\-Stage Defeat Sequence__

The HEIAP\-T round delivers effects through three sequential stages:

Stage 1 — Initial Penetration: The primary penetrator defeats the initial armour layer while the fragment matrix begins dispersal\. First\-stage detonation initiates penetration enhancement and spall generation in the immediate behind\-armour volume\.

Stage 2 — Behind Armour: A secondary charge activates Explosively Formed Penetrator \(EFP\) elements, dispersing fragments throughout the target interior\. Component destruction and system degradation occur in this phase\.

Stage 3 — Terminal: The main charge detonates with maximum fragmentation deployment\. Full pre\-formed fragment pattern coverage and extended incendiary/blast effect is achieved\.

## __4\.2 Anti\-Personnel Fragmentation Performance__

The three\-tier fragment system \(cubes, cylinders, rods\) provides overlapping coverage across different target types and ranges\. Fragment density at 25m range is calculated at approximately 8 fragments/m², sufficient for high probability of multiple hits on personnel targets\. The lethal radius of 25m and casualty radius of 35m represent significant area denial capability for a single 57mm round\.

# __6\. Mechanical Operating Principle__

A defining design characteristic of the AMAS is its fully mechanical operation, with no electronic fire control dependencies in the core operating mechanism\. This design philosophy provides three advantages in contested electromagnetic environments:

First, the system remains fully functional under Electronic Warfare \(EW\) conditions including high\-power microwave \(HPM\) attack and electromagnetic pulse \(EMP\) environments that could disable electronic fire control systems\. Second, mechanical operating systems require less specialised maintenance infrastructure\. Third, mechanical reliability correlates directly with quality of manufacture rather than software integrity, making verification and validation more deterministic\.

The rotary bolt with roller bearings and two\-position gas regulator provides rate control and debris management without electronic sensing\. The dual\-feed mechanical system allows multi\-ammunition\-type operation while maintaining full mechanical function\.

# __8\. Maintenance Requirements__

The 2,500\-round barrel life between replacements provides operational sustainability for sustained fire missions\. Field service intervals are as follows: inspection at 500 rounds, cleaning at 1,000 rounds, gas system service at 5,000 rounds, and operating parts replacement at 10,000 rounds\. The all\-mechanical design enables field maintenance with standard toolkits and without specialised electronic diagnostic equipment\.

# __9\. Comparative Analysis__

Compared to the Bofors 57Mk3 naval system, the AMAS differs in several key respects\. The Bofors system optimises for naval anti\-air and anti\-surface roles with electronic fire control and programmable ammunition\. The AMAS instead targets ground\-forces anti\-armour and area denial with a larger, multi\-stage HEIAP\-T projectile and a mechanical\-only operating system\. The muzzle velocity of 1,350 m/s exceeds the Bofors family's approximately 1,025 m/s, achieved through the longer L/60 barrel length and optimised chamber pressure of the 57×441mm cartridge design\.

The 140mm RHA penetration of the HEIAP\-T primary penetrator compares favourably to standard 57mm HE projectiles, which typically achieve penetration via blast and fragment effects rather than direct kinetic defeat of armoured targets\. This capability enables engagement of infantry fighting vehicles and light armoured personnel carriers that would be immune to conventional 57mm HE rounds\.

# __10\. Conclusion__

The 57mm Advanced Mechanical Autocannon System represents a ground\-forces optimised development of the 57mm calibre class, incorporating multi\-stage terminal effects, pre\-formed tungsten fragmentation, and a fully mechanical operating system for EW\-environment resilience\. The 140mm RHA penetration, 420g TNT\-equivalent blast effect, and 800\-unit cube/cylinder/rod fragment matrix provide multi\-role lethality against armoured vehicles, personnel, and field fortifications from a single ammunition type\. The system specification is consistent with the manufacturing and material capabilities available within the Australian and allied defence industrial base\.
