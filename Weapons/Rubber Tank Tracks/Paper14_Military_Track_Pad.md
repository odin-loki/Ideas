# __Abstract__

This paper presents a comprehensive technical analysis of a military rubber track pad system designed for a 10,000\-tank fleet, providing a complete Technical Data Package \(TDP\) covering material specification, tread design optimisation, attachment system, manufacturing process, and lifecycle cost analysis\. The pad employs a hybrid polymer blend of HNBR \(40 phr\), NBR \(30 phr\), NR \(25 phr\), and Neoprene \(5 phr\), achieving Shore A 72 hardness, ≥26 MPa tensile strength, ≥95 N/mm tear resistance, and self\-extinguishing flammability in 5 seconds\. The T\-slot bolt attachment system enables 12\-minute field installation with standard 8mm hex key tooling\. The hybrid military tread pattern achieves a performance score of 6,679/10,000 across six terrain types\. Twenty\-five year lifecycle cost is $282,235 per tank \($2\.82 billion fleet\), delivering 15\-20 dB noise reduction, 40% improved ice traction, and 80% reduction in road infrastructure damage versus bare steel tracks\.

# __1\. Introduction__

Main battle tank rubber track pads provide traction enhancement on paved surfaces, noise reduction for stealth operations, and road surface protection during transit through civilian infrastructure\. Steel tracks on paved surfaces generate acoustic signatures detectable at ranges of 1 km or more, substantially degrading operational concealment in urban and semi\-urban environments\. They also cause rapid pavement damage, generating significant diplomatic friction in NATO operations on allied civilian infrastructure\.

The Australian Defence Force 10,000\-tank fleet represents a significant procurement programme\. Current bare metal tracks incur approximately $5,000/year/tank in road damage penalties and provide no acoustic signature reduction\. Commercial rubber pads \($45,300 per tank over 25 years\) offer insufficient durability for military operations\. The Hybrid Military track pad system described here is optimised for military requirements at $282,235 per tank over 25 years—a premium justified by tactical advantages that cannot be quantified in direct cost terms alone\.

# __2\. Rubber Formulation__

## __2\.1 Polymer Blend Specification__

The hybrid blend combines four polymers selected for complementary properties:

HNBR is widely used in military and aerospace applications requiring sustained performance at elevated temperatures and in hydrocarbon\-contaminated environments\. Its excellent heat ageing resistance \(maximum continuous temperature \+150°C\) and oil resistance are critical for track pads exposed to vehicle drivetrains, exhaust systems, and operational contamination\. NBR complements the HNBR with additional abrasion resistance and cost efficiency\. NR provides the tensile strength \(≥26 MPa\) and hysteresis properties needed for wet traction\. The 5% Neoprene loading provides the self\-extinguishing flame resistance—a critical safety property for a track pad exposed to tracer rounds, fuel spills, and other fire hazards\.

## __2\.3 Cure System__

Cure conditions: 165°C, 150 bar pressure, 15\-minute cycle\. These parameters ensure complete crosslink development throughout the pad thickness \(25mm\) while managing heat distribution uniformity\. Post\-cure stabilisation at ambient temperature for 24 hours completes residual crosslinking and dimensional stabilisation\.

## __3\.2 Pattern Features__

The pattern incorporates three pairs of 45° chevron bars for primary traction, six centre drainage blocks for self\-cleaning, 64 edge sipes for ice and snow grip, and four corner stability blocks\. Contact ratio is 66% with 34% void ratio\. The 66% contact ratio provides adequate load\-bearing contact area for pavement operations while the 34% void ratio enables mud and snow self\-cleaning to prevent pack\-up on the tread surface\.

The 45° chevron bar orientation provides multidirectional traction components—forward, backward, and lateral—compared to the purely longitudinal resistance of straight\-bar patterns\. Edge sipes provide fine\-scale surface disruption that improves ice grip by increasing the number of independent rubber edges bearing against ice crystal irregularities\.

## __4\.2 Design Rationale__

The T\-slot system uses T\-nuts moulded integrally into the pad during vulcanisation, providing higher pull\-out resistance than post\-moulded inserts\. The T\-bolt engagement distributes clamping load across the full T\-slot length, preventing stress concentration at any single point\. Standard M10 Grade 10\.9 bolts provide 59 kN bolt proof load, substantially exceeding the 5,000 N pull\-out requirement\. The system requires only an 8mm hex key—standard in all military vehicle tool kits—enabling pad replacement without specialist tools\.

# __6\. Manufacturing__

## __6\.1 Production Process__

Three\-phase manufacturing: compound preparation \(banbury/internal mixer, 60\-80 minute cycle\), mould forming \(4\-6 pads per mould at 165°C/150 bar/15 min\), and post\-processing \(deflashing, insert installation, quality inspection\)\. Single\-line capacity: 500 pads/day \(two shifts\)\. Full military contract production requires 4 lines delivering 900,000 pads for 10,000 tanks in 6 months\.

## __6\.2 Quality Control__

Every 10th pad: hardness, dimensions, and visual inspection\. Every 100th pad: destructive testing of tensile, tear, and compression set properties\. Daily: compound viscosity and cure rate verification\. Batch: material certifications and first article testing per ASTM D412, D624, D2240, D395, and D5963\.
