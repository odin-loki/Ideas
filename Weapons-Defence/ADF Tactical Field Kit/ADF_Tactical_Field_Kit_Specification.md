## 1. INTRODUCTION AND DESIGN PHILOSOPHY

### 1.1 Purpose

This document specifies the complete design, materials, manufacture, procurement, and cost analysis for a next-generation individual field kit intended for issue to Australian Defence Force dismounted operators. The kit is designed around the special operations dismounted soldier but is scalable in configuration for general infantry, combat engineer, and reconnaissance roles.

The specification integrates the TACT-1 Mark II compact ration system and Advanced Sports Nutrition Powder energy drink as the nutrition foundation, the Hemp Harmony hygiene formulation as the personal care foundation, and a suite of purpose-designed titanium-alloy tools that represent a generational improvement over current ADF-issued kit in weight, durability, and capability.

### 1.2 The Operational Problem

The ADF dismounted operator in the contemporary threat environment carries a combat load that routinely exceeds 40kg including weapon, ammunition, water, communications, armour, and rations. The Army Research Laboratory's load carriage doctrine identifies 30% of body mass as the practical performance limit above which physical and cognitive capability degrade non-linearly. For a 75kg operator that is 22.5kg total. Current kit configurations routinely exceed this limit before the operator has picked up a weapon.

The individual field kit — the personal survival, sustainment, and utility equipment carried independent of mission-specific loads — typically accounts for 8–12kg of that budget under current IRP and issued equipment. This specification targets a total field kit weight of under 7kg excluding food and water, with the food system (TACT-1 Mark II) delivering a 5–14kg saving over standard IRP for a 72-hour operation, and the water system eliminating the need to carry more than 1.5L of treated water at any time.

The net effect is a field kit that is lighter than current issue, more capable than current issue, and designed to survive the full range of Australian operating environments — tropical north, central desert, alpine south, maritime littoral, and urban — without modification.

### 1.3 Design Principles

**Weight discipline.** Every component is justified by function. No redundancy between items. Titanium alloy throughout the tool suite. Cordura and aluminium throughout the pack. No steel where titanium performs equivalently or better. No polymer where aluminium performs equivalently. No dead weight.

**Modularity.** Every pouch, sleeve, and compartment is MOLLE-compatible and detachable. The operator can reconfigure the kit in under five minutes for different mission profiles. The IFAK, navigation kit, and hydration system can each be detached and handed off or worn independently.

**One-hand operability.** Every retention system, buckle, zipper pull, locking collar, and fastener is operable with one hand wearing a glove. This is non-negotiable. An operator with one hand occupied — holding a weapon, assisting a casualty, gripping a rope — must be able to access any part of the kit with the remaining hand.

**Thermal tolerance.** All materials and coatings are selected for the full operating envelope: -20°C to +65°C ambient, with individual components rated to higher temperatures where relevant (the TACT-1 carnauba coating survives 85°C; the titanium tools have no practical upper temperature limit in field use).

**Noise discipline.** No metal-on-metal contact between loose components. Titanium tools nested inside each other with TPE or foam spacers. All MOLLE attachments seated and taped for noise-critical operations. Zippers silenced with loop pulls that tuck away.

**Repairability.** Every zipper is field-replaceable with a standard slider tool. Every O-ring in the filtration system is a standard silicone size carried in a spare parts pouch. Every cam collar on the shovel handle accepts the same 4mm Allen key used throughout the kit. The operator can repair or replace every consumable part in the field.

### 1.4 Threat Environment

The kit is designed against the following operational environments without modification:

- Sustained ambient temperatures of -10°C to +55°C
- Precipitation including tropical monsoon (>300mm/day) and sustained immersion up to 30 minutes at 1 metre depth
- UV Index 15+ (northern Australia, equatorial Pacific)
- Dust and sand ingestion (central Australia, Middle East)
- Salt spray corrosion (maritime littoral operations)
- Crush loads up to 150kg (vehicle stowage, body weight on pack)
- Drop from 2 metres onto hard surface (helicopter fast-rope, wall entry)

## 2. LOAD CARRIAGE SYSTEM

### 2.1 Overview

The load carriage system is a 32-litre main pack with an integrated load distribution harness, hydration compartment, and full MOLLE external attachment system. It is designed around the principle that the pack is the operator's base of operations for the duration of any mission — it must be fast to access under stress, comfortable to carry at pace over rough terrain for 8+ hours, survivable in the full threat environment described above, and configurable for different mission roles without specialist tools or training.

The overall design language draws from the Badlands Superday and Mystery Ranch architecture — a tall rectangular column pack with a stiff-stay frame, load-bearing hip belt, and generous MOLLE real estate — but the materials, hardware, and dimensional specification exceed those commercial designs in every parameter relevant to military use.

### 2.2 Shell Materials

**Primary face fabric:** 1000D Cordura nylon with a 400g/m² weight and a polyurethane coating on the inner face at 2000mm hydrostatic head. Cordura nylon is a high-tenacity nylon 6,6 fabric with a registered specification maintained by Invista. The 1000D designation refers to the yarn denier — a 1000D yarn is approximately 3× the diameter of a 300D yarn and proportionally more resistant to abrasion and puncture. The 1000D specification is the minimum acceptable for the primary face; no substitution with 500D or 600D equivalents is permitted on the main body panels.

**High-wear overlay panels:** 500D Cordura on the base panel, lower back panel, and shoulder contact zones. These areas receive the highest abrasion loading — the base from ground placement, the lower back from webbing and kit contact, the shoulders from straps. The 500D overlay is sewn over the primary 1000D face, creating a double-wall construction at these zones.

**Back panel and hip belt face fabric:** 3D spacer mesh with a 6mm pile depth, constructed from polyester monofilament. The spacer structure creates an air gap between the operator's back and the pack face that allows sweat evaporation and reduces skin temperature by up to 4°C compared to a solid foam back panel in 35°C ambient. The mesh is treated with an antimicrobial silver-ion finish that inhibits bacterial growth in the moisture-laden environment between the pack and the body.

**Internal liners:** 210D nylon ripstop with a polyurethane coating, in high-visibility orange. The high-visibility internal colour is operationally important — the operator can see the bottom of the pack in low light when searching for items, and the bright interior reflects light into the compartment. The ripstop construction prevents small tears from propagating.

**Thread:** Bonded nylon 69 weight throughout primary seams; bonded nylon 138 weight at bar-tack reinforcement points. Bar-tacks are applied at every webbing attachment, every D-ring mount, every strap anchor, and every zipper end — a minimum of 42 bar-tack points on the complete assembly.

**Seam taping:** All external seams are taped on the inner face with 25mm polyurethane seam tape, heat-bonded. Taped seams are waterproof independently of the face fabric coating and prevent wicking along the thread path.

### 2.3 Frame System

The frame is a dual aluminium stay system — two 6061-T6 aluminium alloy stays, each 6mm × 4mm rectangular section, 520mm long, running vertically inside a dedicated sleeve sewn to the back panel and positioned 95mm apart (measured centre to centre). 6061-T6 aluminium has a tensile strength of 310 MPa and a yield strength of 276 MPa — sufficient to resist the bending loads imposed by a 25kg pack load without permanent deformation, while being light enough (total stay weight: 85g) not to penalise the kit weight budget.

The stays are pre-bent with a 15mm forward bow at the lumbar zone to match the average lumbar curve of an adult male at the 50th percentile by ADF population data. They are removable — the sleeve has a zippered opening at the top — for use as improvised splints, shelter frame members, or sleeping pad substitutes. When removed, the pack collapses flat for storage or air transport.

A single 4mm HDPE framesheet, 280mm wide × 380mm tall × 4mm thick, sits between the stays and the back panel foam in the lower two-thirds of the back panel. The framesheet provides lateral rigidity that the stays alone cannot — it prevents the lower pack from folding inward under side loads and maintains the separation between the pack body and the operator's back that keeps the hip belt correctly positioned on the iliac crest. The framesheet is also removable via the same stay sleeve.

### 2.4 Back Panel

The back panel is a 20mm thick closed-cell EVA foam insert, 280mm wide × 450mm tall, covered on the operator-facing side with the 3D spacer mesh described above and on the pack-facing side with a smooth nylon face that slides easily against the framesheet. The EVA foam is dual-density: a 60kg/m³ perimeter band 40mm wide provides structural support and transmits load to the hip belt; a 35kg/m³ central section provides comfort and vibration damping.

A 35mm wide central channel runs the full 450mm length of the back panel, formed by a 5mm depression in the foam. This channel creates the air gap between the operator's spine and the pack that prevents the thoracic spinous processes from contacting the pack under load. The channel also directs sweat downward rather than allowing it to pool — in a 35°C environment on a 60-minute movement, an operator will produce 1–1.5 litres of sweat on their back alone.

### 2.5 Compartment Layout

**Main compartment:** 32 litres total, 300mm wide × 450mm tall × 240mm deep at the widest point, tapering to 200mm depth at the base. The compartment is accessed by a U-shaped YKK AquaGuard 10 zipper running 380mm across the top and 350mm down each side. The internal organisation system uses three removable horizontal dividers on velcro attachment rails — 25mm hook-and-loop, rated to 8kg pull per 25mm length. Default configuration positions the first divider at 280mm from the base and the second at 420mm from the base, creating three zones:

- Lower zone (280mm tall): IFAK and medical items. Colour-coded red via a 100mm wide horizontal stripe of red nylon sewn to the internal liner at the zone boundary. This zone is accessible by reaching in from the top; the IFAK pouch itself is externally mounted for faster access (see Section 7) but the zone can hold spare medical supplies.

- Middle zone (140mm tall): primary equipment — entrenching tool when not externally mounted, extra rations, warm layer, communications.

- Upper zone (130mm tall): sleeping system interface or bivouac equipment.

The main compartment has four internal attachment loops sewn at the upper corners and midpoints of each long wall — 25mm nylon loops bar-tacked through the shell and liner, each rated to 15kg. These allow internal MOLLE pouches, stuff sacks, or equipment ties to be anchored.

**Top lid compartment:** A semi-rigid lid constructed from 3mm HDPE sheet covered in 500D Cordura, 300mm wide × 180mm wide × 60mm deep, with a YKK AquaGuard 8 zipper on three sides. The lid compartment is for immediate-access items — head torch, navigation cards, lozenge sachets, gloves, first-use items. It detaches from the main pack via two 25mm side-release buckles at the front and two YKK #5 zipper pulls at the rear, converting to a standalone hip pack when separated. The detachment operation takes under 15 seconds.

**Hydration compartment:** A dedicated sleeve between the main compartment and the back panel, 340mm wide × 500mm tall × 65mm deep. The sleeve is formed by a separate nylon panel sewn to the back panel perimeter with a YKK AquaGuard 8 zipper running the full back edge — the operator opens the hydration sleeve from the back of the pack without opening the main compartment. The bladder hangs from a stainless steel S-hook at the top of the sleeve, rated to 5kg.

The drink tube exits through a reinforced rubber grommet at the upper left corner of the sleeve. The grommet is angled 45 degrees forward to prevent kinking at the exit point. The tube routes along the left shoulder strap in a neoprene sleeve with a velcro closure every 150mm, terminating at a bite valve holster on the left shoulder strap at chin height. The filtration input port is a second rubber grommet at the lower right corner of the hydration sleeve — through which the pump output hose feeds directly into the bladder fill port (see Section 3).

**Front pockets:**

Lower front pocket: 250mm wide × 200mm tall × 85mm deep. YKK AquaGuard 8 zipper on three sides. Internal organisation: one mesh divider creating a 40mm-deep front section for flat items (cards, tape) and a 45mm-deep rear section for tools and sachets. One zinc-plated steel key clip on a 150mm nylon loop in the rear section. External face carries a full MOLLE grid.

Mid front pocket (admin): 250mm wide × 140mm tall × 40mm deep. YKK #5 zipper on three sides. Dedicated to flat documents, laminated cards, notepad, and pen. Internal: three elastic pen loops sewn to the back face, one clear vinyl window pocket 150mm × 100mm for a laminated ID card or reference card.

Upper front pocket: 200mm wide × 110mm tall × 40mm deep. YKK #5 zipper on three sides. Snack and immediate-access pocket — one day's worth of lozenge and energy drink sachets fits comfortably. Mesh internal divider.

**Side pockets:** Left and right side panels each carry an open-top elastic mesh pocket, 120mm diameter × 200mm tall, constructed from 4-way stretch mesh with a 65mm elastic hem at the opening. These accept standard 750ml Nalgene-type bottles or the collapsible source-water flask (see Section 3). The elastic provides retention without the stiffness that makes removing a bottle with one hand impossible — a common failure of fixed-side-pocket designs.

**Base compression:** Two horizontal 25mm webbing straps with side-release buckles run under the base panel and up the front face, allowing the pack height to be compressed when partially loaded. Reduces contents shift on movement by approximately 60% compared to an uncompressed partial load.

### 2.6 Zipper System

Every primary zipper on the pack is YKK AquaGuard 10 — the 10mm coil variant with a water-resistant polyurethane membrane heat-bonded to the coil on both faces. AquaGuard 10 is rated to 100,000 operating cycles before measurable degradation of the water-resistant membrane. Commercial-grade packs typically use YKK #5 or #8 coil zippers rated to 20,000–30,000 cycles — in a military context where a pack may be opened and closed 30–50 times per day on a sustained operation, a commercial zipper reaches end of life in under three years of moderate use. The AquaGuard 10 extends this to over eight years at the same usage rate.

Zipper pulls are custom-fabricated 6061 aluminium D-rings, 50mm × 30mm × 4mm cross-section, anodised black. The D-ring geometry is operable with gloved fingers; the 50mm major axis provides enough grip surface for a cold or wet hand. Every zipper has two opposing sliders — the zipper can be opened from either end and partially opened from the middle. Storm flaps of 500D Cordura, 35mm wide, are sewn over every external zipper run with a 10mm clearance above the coil, preventing direct water impingement on the coil gap and reducing UV degradation of the polyurethane membrane by approximately 40% over a five-year service life.

Secondary compartment zippers (admin pocket, upper pocket, hydration sleeve) use YKK AquaGuard 8 — the same specification at smaller gauge, appropriate for lower-stress applications.

### 2.7 Shoulder Harness

The shoulder straps are anatomically contoured — pre-formed in a 15-degree forward arc matching the average ADF male shoulder profile. Each strap is 80mm wide at the shoulder contact zone, tapering to 50mm at the sternum buckle and 38mm at the lower back attachment. The strap body is constructed from a 15mm dual-density foam core — 60kg/m³ polyurethane foam at the upper shoulder contact zone transitioning to 35kg/m³ EVA foam at the underarm contact zone — covered in the same 3D spacer mesh as the back panel. The foam-mesh assembly is enclosed in a 500D Cordura outer shell at the edges and bonded nylon 69 stitching along the centre line.

The dual-density construction is biomechanically significant: the firmer foam at the top of the shoulder resists compression under the primary vertical load, maintaining strap width and preventing the strap from rolling into a cord that concentrates pressure on the trapezius. The softer foam at the underarm prevents brachial plexus compression — a common cause of the hand numbness that operators experience on long movements with heavy loads.

**Load lifter straps:** A 25mm webbing strap connects the top of each shoulder strap to the pack frame at a point approximately 30mm below the operator's ear level. Adjusting these straps tightens the angle between the shoulder strap and the pack, shifting the pack top toward the operator's back and lowering the effective centre of gravity. The optimal adjustment is 45 degrees from vertical — at this angle, the forward pitch moment (the tendency of a loaded pack to pull the operator forward and down) is reduced by approximately 35% compared to a vertical strap. A 45-degree reference mark is laser-engraved on the strap at the correct position.

**Strap attachment and adjustment:** The shoulder straps attach to the pack via a yoke system adjustable in 20mm increments across six positions, accommodating torso lengths from 430mm to 530mm (measured from the iliac crest to the C7 vertebral process). The yoke adjustment is performed by the unit quartermaster at issue and does not require field adjustment under normal use. The lower attachment of each shoulder strap is a 40mm aluminium tri-glide buckle bar-tacked to the pack base panel at a point 80mm outboard of the centreline, providing a natural outward splay that matches the shoulder geometry without forcing the straps into a V at the lower back.

**Sternum strap:** A 20mm wide webbing strap on a slider running the full height of both shoulder straps, with a side-release buckle at the operator's mid-sternum. The buckle incorporates an integrated Fox 40 pealess whistle rated to 120dB — operable by squeezing the buckle sides. The sternum strap limits shoulder strap spread under lateral loads and prevents pack oscillation during running. It is positioned at 60% of the operator's sternum height (measured from the xiphoid process) by default — the height at which it is least likely to restrict breathing during sustained aerobic exertion.

### 2.8 Hip Belt

The hip belt is the primary load transfer mechanism of the system. A correctly fitted hip belt transfers 60–70% of the pack weight to the pelvis and femurs, reducing shoulder and neck fatigue by approximately 45% compared to a shoulder-only carry. The hip belt is constructed as an independent sub-assembly that attaches to the pack via a pivoting aluminium plate — this plate allows the hip belt to rotate ±15 degrees about the vertical axis, permitting the hips and pack to move semi-independently during scrambling, river crossing, and uneven terrain movement.

The belt wings are 120mm wide at the iliac crest contact zone, tapering to 50mm at the front buckle. Each wing is 260mm long from the pack attachment to the buckle end. The internal construction is dual-density foam — 60kg/m³ polyurethane foam at the iliac crest contact zone (the load-bearing zone) transitioning to 35kg/m³ EVA foam at the hip flexor zone at the anterior end of the wing. The iliac crest foam is anatomically contoured with a 10mm radius channel at the upper edge that prevents the belt from impinging on the iliac crest under heavy loads — a critical comfort feature that most commercial designs neglect.

The front buckle is a 50mm Duraflex side-release buckle rated to 2,000kg tensile load — this is a structural element, not an adjustment buckle, and must meet this rating. The buckle is on a continuous adjustment strap covering waist circumferences from 700mm to 1,050mm without gaps or fixed holes. A simple measurement-to-adjustment marking on the strap allows the unit quartermaster to set the correct length at issue.

The outer face of each hip belt wing carries a 100mm × 150mm MOLLE panel — two rows of standard 25mm webbing at 38mm spacing. This is the primary attachment point for the war pick sleeve (dominant hand side) and the signal mirror pouch (non-dominant side), and can accept additional mission-specific pouches up to 400g without compromising hip belt geometry.

### 2.9 MOLLE Grid

The front face of the pack carries a full MOLLE grid — 25mm 1000D Cordura webbing rows at standard 38mm centre-to-centre spacing, extending across 90% of the front face. Each webbing row is sewn at both ends and bar-tacked at every 38mm interval — the bar-tacks are structural attachment points, not decorative stitching. The webbing is the same denier and colour as the pack shell to prevent fraying contrast and UV-differential bleaching over time.

Side panel MOLLE covers 60% of each side panel — three rows on each side. The hip belt MOLLE panels are as described above. Total MOLLE attachment capacity: approximately 24 columns × 6 rows on the front face, 8 columns × 3 rows per side panel, 4 columns × 2 rows per hip belt wing. The complete external MOLLE real estate can accommodate up to 12 standard-sized pouches in a fully loaded configuration.

## 3. WATER FILTRATION AND HYDRATION SYSTEM

### 3.1 System Overview

The water system is an integrated assembly comprising a titanium-bodied hollow fibre pump filter with UV-C sanitation, a 3-litre hydration bladder, a collapsible 1-litre source-water collection flask, and a dissolvable mineral and bicarbonate lozenge for post-filtration mineralisation. The system interfaces directly with the hydration compartment of the pack — the pump output hose routes through the dedicated filtration grommet and connects to the bladder fill port, so filtered water goes straight to the bladder without an intermediate handling step. The operator never touches contaminated water with bare hands at any point in the process.

The design philosophy is that the operator carries the filtration hardware, not the treated water. The hardware weighs 510g total. A litre of water weighs 1,000g. On a 72-hour operation in a temperate environment requiring 4 litres per day, the total water carry is 12 litres at any point only if there is no access to source water — in any Australian operating environment with a functioning waterway, the actual carry at any moment is 3 litres (the bladder capacity), and the filter produces unlimited safe water from any source.

### 3.2 Filter Housing

**Material:** Ti-6Al-4V (grade 5 titanium alloy), investment-cast and machined. Grade 5 titanium is selected over polymer housings because it is biologically and chemically inert — it does not leach, does not absorb contaminants, does not degrade with UV exposure, and does not crack under crush loads that would destroy a polymer housing. A field-dropped polymer filter housing can fail silently — the housing cracks invisibly but the filter appears intact; the operator continues using it while raw source water bypasses the membrane. A titanium housing loaded to 150kg crush shows no deformation.

**Construction:** The housing is two threaded halves — forward half (inlet and membrane housing) and rear half (UV chamber) — meeting at a 45mm diameter fine-pitch thread with M45×1.5 metric thread form and a silicone O-ring seal in a 2mm × 2mm recessed groove. The O-ring is a standard 44mm ID × 2mm cross-section silicone O-ring — a part number carried in the spare parts pouch. The thread requires 2.5 turns to fully engage, providing a positive mechanical stop that prevents cross-threading.

**Dimensions:** 210mm long × 52mm diameter assembled. Weight: 218g.

**Inlet port:** Forward end cap carries a 20mm push-fit hose barb with a spring-loaded stainless steel ball-valve check valve. The check valve closes when the hose is disconnected, preventing contaminated water from draining back toward the pump. The end cap threads onto the forward housing half with a 35mm diameter thread — accessible for field disassembly and O-ring replacement without tools other than the grip of a bare hand.

**Outlet port:** Rear end cap, identical construction to the inlet, carries the outlet hose barb with an identical check valve. The outlet hose connects to the bladder fill port via the pack's hydration sleeve grommet — a 300mm × 8mm ID silicone hose rated to 200kPa burst pressure.

**Pressure rating:** The complete housing is rated to 500kPa (5 bar) hydrostatic pressure — 10× the maximum pressure generated by the pump under normal use. This margin ensures that even a blocked filter — which would drive pressure up — cannot burst the housing.

### 3.3 Hollow Fibre Membrane

**Type:** PVDF (polyvinylidene difluoride) hollow fibre ultrafiltration membrane.

**Pore size:** 0.01 microns nominal. This is ultrafiltration territory — the membrane removes all bacteria (minimum 0.2 microns), all protozoa and cysts (minimum 1 micron), all particulate matter, and approximately 99.99% of viruses at or above 0.02 microns. Standard hollow fibre consumer filters operate at 0.1 microns — adequate for bacteria and protozoa but not viruses. The 0.01 micron specification is driven by the threat environment, which includes tropical waterborne viruses (Hepatitis A, Norovirus, Rotavirus) in northern Australian and Pacific operating theatres.

**Fibre specification:** 1,200 individual PVDF hollow fibres, each 0.8mm outer diameter × 0.5mm inner bore, potted at both ends in a two-part epoxy disc. The epoxy pot seals each fibre tube individually — a breach in one fibre does not compromise the pot; only the affected fibre loses function, and the system continues operating at 99.9% capacity. The fibre bundle is 140mm long × 40mm diameter when potted.

**Filtration mechanism:** Dead-end outside-in. Source water enters the exterior of the fibre bundle at the inlet end, passes radially through the 0.01 micron pores, and exits through the inner bore. Clean water from all fibres collects in the interior bore and flows toward the outlet. Retained contaminants accumulate on the exterior of the fibre bundle — this accumulation is removed by backflushing.

**Flow rate:** 15–20ml per pump stroke at 0.01 microns. Lower than a 0.1 micron filter for the same pump effort because the smaller pores require greater transmembrane pressure. A full 3-litre bladder fill requires approximately 150–200 pump strokes — 3–4 minutes of pumping. This is a deliberate and accepted trade-off for the pathogen coverage the 0.01 micron membrane provides.

**Rated capacity:** 100,000 litres from clean source water; 20,000–50,000 litres from turbid source water depending on turbidity. At 4 litres per day per operator over a 10-year service life, the membrane handles approximately 14,600 litres — comfortably within the low-turbidity rating and significantly within the clean-water rating.

**Backflush procedure:** The pump is bidirectional — reversing the pump handle direction reverses flow through the membrane, dislodging accumulated contaminants from the exterior of the fibre bundle and ejecting them through the inlet port. Standard backflush: 10 reverse pump strokes after every 10 litres filtered, 30 reverse strokes after each day of use. Backflush water exits through the inlet hose and the collapsible source flask — the operator tips the flask to discard the backflush effluent. Procedure time: 30 seconds.

**Storage:** The membrane must not be allowed to freeze while wet — ice crystals destroy the fibre structure. A sachet of food-grade propylene glycol solution (20ml) in the spare parts pouch is used to flush and wet-store the membrane during freeze-risk periods. The membrane must be flushed with 500ml of water before use after propylene glycol storage.

### 3.4 UV-C Sanitation Stage

**Purpose:** The UV-C stage addresses the residual viral load that passes through the 0.01 micron membrane. While 99.99% of viruses are removed by the membrane, the UV-C stage provides an independent redundant disinfection mechanism for the remainder, and independently disinfects against any viral pathogen regardless of size.

**UV-C LED:** A single surface-mount UV-C LED emitting at 265nm peak wavelength — the wavelength of maximum DNA/RNA absorption in microorganisms, providing the highest disinfection efficiency per unit of optical power. The LED is driven at 50mA continuous from the battery supply, producing approximately 40mW of UV-C optical power. At the flow rate produced by the pump (15–20ml/stroke, approximately 5ml/second during continuous pumping), the UV dose delivered is approximately 60 mJ/cm² — exceeding the 40 mJ/cm² required for 4-log (99.99%) inactivation of Cryptosporidium, Giardia, E. coli, norovirus, and hepatitis A in the published disinfection literature.

**Reflector:** A polished Ti-6Al-4V disc at the far end of the UV chamber reflects UV photons back through the water, increasing the effective path length and UV dose by approximately 30%. Titanium reflects approximately 65% of UV-C at 265nm — lower than an aluminium or PTFE reflector but resistant to pitting in the water chemistry encountered in field source water.

**Water path:** The UV chamber is a helical channel machined into the bore of the rear housing half — 4mm wide × 4mm deep, 6mm pitch, 22mm diameter, producing approximately 180mm of total water path length in 50mm of axial length. The helical path ensures all water passes within 8mm of the LED face at some point during transit — critical for achieving the target UV dose at the edges of the flow stream.

**Quartz window:** The LED face is protected by a 12mm diameter × 1mm thick synthetic quartz (fused silica) disc bonded into a titanium retaining ring with UV-stable epoxy. Standard borosilicate glass transmits only 8% of UV-C at 265nm — quartz transmits 92%. The quartz disc is field-replaceable: the retaining ring has a 1mm hex socket and unscrews with the Allen key carried in the kit.

**Activation:** A miniature paddle-wheel flow sensor in the UV chamber inlet detects flow above 1ml/second and triggers the UV LED via the control circuit. The LED activates within 0.5 seconds of flow detection and deactivates within 2 seconds of flow cessation. There is no manual activation switch — activation failure is not possible unless the batteries are depleted or the circuit fails.

**Power supply:** Two AA lithium batteries (Energizer L91 or equivalent) in a waterproof compartment integral to the rear housing half, sealed with an O-ring-sealed end cap. Lithium AA cells are specified over alkaline because lithium cells maintain rated voltage down to -40°C — alkaline cells lose 30% capacity at 0°C and 50% at -20°C, which would compromise UV dose at low temperatures. Each set of two AA lithium cells provides approximately 1,500 pump cycles of UV treatment — equivalent to approximately 22,500 litres at 15ml/stroke. **LED rated life:** 10,000 hours continuous operation — at 30 seconds of UV activation per litre (the approximate pump time), this equates to approximately 1,200,000 litres of treated water before LED replacement. ## 4. ENTRENCHING TOOL

### 4.1 Overview

The titanium telescopic entrenching tool is a ground-up redesign of the military entrenching tool with two primary objectives: to match the ergonomics of a full-size shovel at full extension, eliminating the back strain that accumulates from sustained use of a compact-format shovel with a short handle; and to carry at a weight and stowed length compatible with external MOLLE attachment to the field kit pack. The design achieves both: full extension produces a 1,050mm tool with full-size leverage geometry; stowed length is 560mm including the blade.

### 4.2 Shovel Head

**Material:** Ti-6Al-4V investment cast and forge-pressed. The casting is performed at 1,750°C in a ceramic investment mould, producing a near-net-shape head that requires minimal machining. The casting is followed immediately (while still at 900°C) by a single-hit forge press at 120 tonnes that aligns the grain structure along the primary bending stress axis — the junction between the blade face and the socket tube. This junction is the fracture initiation point for 90% of entrenching tool failures. Forged Ti-6Al-4V at this junction has approximately 3× the fatigue life of a cast structure in the same geometry.

**Dimensions:** 220mm wide × 280mm long. The blade face has an 8mm concave dish across the 220mm width — this dish is load-bearing, not merely cosmetic. A flat plate of the same dimensions and wall thickness would flex approximately 12mm under a 20kg point load at the blade edge; the dished plate flexes less than 2mm under the same load. The blade profile is rectangular with slightly rounded lower corners (20mm radius) — the rounded corners prevent the corner stress concentration that causes corner cracking in prying applications.

**Spine thickness:** 4mm at the upper face, tapering to 1.5mm at the cutting edge. The taper is linear across the full 280mm blade length. The cutting edge bevel is 25 degrees inclusive, ground on both faces. A 25-degree inclusive bevel is sharp enough to cut roots, fibrous soil, and turf without tearing; it is durable enough not to roll on gravel, hardpan, or clay. A sharper bevel (say, 15 degrees) would cut better but fail faster.

**Stiffening ribs:** Three longitudinal ribs on the upper (non-digging) face of the blade, 3mm proud of the surface, 8mm wide, running from the socket to 20mm short of the blade edge. The ribs are formed as part of the casting rather than added later — integral ribs are stronger and lighter than welded-on ribs. The rib geometry increases the second moment of area of the blade cross-section by approximately 180% without adding material to the digging face where it would increase soil adhesion.

**Socket:** 35mm internal diameter, 80mm long, 4mm wall at the base tapering to 2.5mm at the open end. Two M6 × 1.0 stainless steel cross-bolts pass through the socket wall and the handle outer tube at the assembled position. The socket bore is machined to H7 tolerance (35.000mm to 35.025mm) for a precision sliding fit with the handle outer tube (h6 tolerance, 34.984mm to 35.000mm). This fit eliminates the 0.5–1.0mm of play that produces rattle and wobble in conventional entrenching tools.

**Surface treatment:** Micro-arc oxidation (MAO) on all surfaces — a ceramic-like conversion coating 20–30 microns thick, integral to the titanium surface (not a separate coating that can flake or peel). MAO produces a surface hardness of approximately 1,000 HV (compared to the underlying Ti-6Al-4V at approximately 320 HV) and a matte grey-black appearance with minimal reflectance. The coating is porous at the micro scale, providing excellent grip in muddy conditions and paint adhesion if additional camouflage treatment is required.

**Weight:** 482g.

### 4.3 Telescopic Handle

**Material:** Ti-6Al-4V seamless cold-drawn tubing throughout. Cold drawing is specified over extruded tubing because the drawing process produces tighter dimensional tolerances (±0.05mm on OD versus ±0.15mm for extrusion) and aligns the grain structure circumferentially — the correct orientation for a tube loaded in bending, where the maximum stress is at the outer circumference.

**Three-section assembly:**

Outer tube: 34mm OD × 2.5mm wall × 320mm long. This is the primary structural member — it fits into the shovel socket and carries the majority of the bending load. At the upper end, the outer tube receives the mid tube and carries the first cam collar.

Mid tube: 28mm OD × 2.0mm wall × 340mm long. Slides inside the outer tube. At the upper end, carries the second cam collar.

Inner section: 22mm OD × 2.0mm wall × 280mm long. Slides inside the mid tube. Carries the grip over-mould and end cap.

**Stowed length:** With all sections nested and both cam collars locked in the collapsed position, the handle assembly measures 380mm from the base of the outer tube to the end cap of the inner section. Combined with the 280mm blade, stowed tool length is 560mm.

**Extended length:** Mid tube extended 270mm from the outer tube, inner section extended 200mm from the mid tube. Total handle length from end cap to socket entry: 830mm. Combined with the 280mm blade: 1,050mm overall — equivalent to a standard D-handle spade in digging leverage.

**Cam locking collars:** Each collar is a 40mm wide anodised 6061-T6 aluminium ring with an internal eccentric cam machined from the same billet. Rotating the collar 90 degrees (clockwise to lock, anti-clockwise to unlock) drives the eccentric cam against the inner tube, applying a compressive force of approximately 2,000N distributed around the tube circumference. The cam engages a machined detent at the locked position — a 0.5mm deep × 3mm wide circumferential groove in the collar bore that produces an audible and tactile click. Unlocking requires deliberate rotation against the detent — accidental release under digging load is not possible.

The cam lever extends 32mm from the collar body with a 12mm diameter thumb pad — operable with a winter-weight glove. Total cam collar weight: 28g each, 56g for the pair.

**Weight (handle assembly complete, without grip):** 340g.

## 5. WAR PICK

### 5.1 Design Philosophy and Historical Basis

The war pick is among the oldest purpose-designed armour-defeating close-quarters weapons in recorded military history. Its dominance in medieval mounted warfare against fully armoured opponents rested on a single physical principle that remains as valid against modern body armour as it was against 14th-century plate: concentrated point impact. An edged weapon distributes force along a cutting edge that may be 100–200mm long — the contact pressure is force divided by edge length. A war pick spike concentrates the same force into a contact area of approximately 1–5mm² — contact pressure is 100 to 200 times higher for the same impact energy. A Level IIIA soft armour vest rated to stop a 9mm FMJ at 390m/s will not stop a war pick spike swung at moderate velocity by an average-strength adult. The physics are unambiguous.

The modern field kit war pick is not a medieval weapon — it is 400g where a horseman's pick might have been 800–1,200g, and its handle is 350mm where a cavalry pick's might have been 500–600mm. These dimensions reflect the contemporary use case: a secondary close-quarters tool for a dismounted operator in confined spaces, not a primary weapon for a cavalry charge. The shorter handle and lighter head produce a faster, more controllable tool in hallways, trenches, and vehicle interiors. The reduction in mass is compensated by the geometry of the spike — a purpose-engineered pyramid optimised for penetration rather than the blunt functional geometry of a field pick or entrenching tool point.

### 5.2 Head Design

**Material:** Ti-6Al-4V billet, hot forged in a closed-die tool. Forging is mandatory for this component — no casting alternative is acceptable. The spike experiences a stress concentration factor of approximately 12–15 at the tip geometry under impact loading. A casting porosity of 0.1mm at the base of the spike — invisible to the naked eye — would produce a fatigue crack after 50–100 impacts. Forged Ti-6Al-4V is free of casting porosity, and the closed-die forging process forces the grain flow to follow the spike geometry, aligning the grain boundary planes parallel to the spike faces. This orientation maximises toughness in the direction of the primary stress vector.

**Spike geometry:** Square pyramid cross-section, 80mm long from the eye web face to the tip. Base cross-section 14mm × 14mm at the eye web face. Each face tapers at 8 degrees to the tip, producing a tip cross-sectional area of approximately 0.04mm² before any radius. The actual tip radius is 0.3mm after final grinding — this is the minimum radius that survives repeated hard impacts without the tip folding. A sharper tip (say, 0.1mm radius) would penetrate more easily but would fold on the second or third hard impact.

The square cross-section over a round spike is a deliberate tactical choice. When a round spike penetrates armour, the spike body wedges into the hole it creates, requiring significant extraction force. A square spike creates four linear stress concentrations on the hole perimeter — one per corner — which propagate as cracks during penetration, enlarging the hole relative to the spike body and reducing wedging. Extraction of a square spike requires approximately 40% less force than a round spike of equivalent cross-sectional area, which is significant when the operator needs to strike again immediately.

**Hammerhead face:** The rear of the head opposite the spike carries a 32mm × 32mm flat face, surface-ground to 0.01mm flatness and hardened to 62 HRC by selective induction hardening of a 3mm deep surface zone. This face is used for tent peg driving, hardpan breaking, utility hammering, and secondary striking applications. The mass of the hammerhead (approximately 95g) provides rotational balance to the head — without it, the spike-forward mass distribution would cause the head to pitch forward at the end of the swing, requiring the operator to actively correct trajectory. With the hammerhead, the centre of mass of the head is approximately 10mm forward of the eye, producing a neutral-to-slightly-spike-forward balance that is self-correcting.

**Eye geometry:** 25mm wide × 18mm tall oval through-hole, with a 5-degree taper on both entry faces (both sides of the head). The oval geometry resists rotation of the head on the handle under the off-axis torsional loads that occur when the spike strikes at an angle. A round eye would require a separate anti-rotation key; the oval eye is inherently anti-rotation. The 5-degree taper on each face produces a wedge-fit with the handle tenon — the harder the impact, the tighter the fit. This is the correct engineering solution: the fastening force is proportional to the use case.

**Surface treatment:** Manganese phosphate (Parkerising) to MIL-DTL-16232 specification. Parkerising produces a micro-porous iron phosphate conversion coating 5–15 microns thick on the underlying titanium — the micro-porosity retains oil, providing sustained corrosion protection without a surface film that can chip or peel. The resulting finish is flat matte dark grey, with a reflectance of approximately 3% in the visible spectrum — equivalent to flat black paint but mechanically superior. The hardened hammerhead face is Parkerised after hardening — the conversion coating is unaffected by the surface hardness.

**Total head weight:** 400g (target achieved by adjusting hammerhead face dimensions during design iteration).

### 5.3 Handle Design

**Material:** Ti-6Al-4V seamless cold-drawn tube, 22mm OD × 3mm wall × 350mm long. The 3mm wall is heavier than strictly required for the bending loads — a 2mm wall would be structurally adequate — but the additional wall thickness provides crush resistance for the eye tenon zone and stiffness that improves the feel of the tool during the swing. A too-flexible handle dissipates energy during the swing, reducing effective impact velocity; the 3mm wall produces a handle stiffness that transmits approximately 92% of the swing energy to the spike tip.

**Tenon:** The upper 60mm of the handle is turned to an oval cross-section — 25mm × 18mm, matching the eye geometry with a 5-degree taper on both faces (matching the eye taper exactly for full contact across the taper length). The tenon is machined on a CNC lathe from the solid tube wall — not a separate component, not welded. The transition from the round tube body to the oval tenon is a 10mm radius blend that avoids the stress concentration of a sharp shoulder.

**Cross-pin retention:** A 5mm diameter × 36mm long hardened stainless steel pin passes through the handle at the mid-point of the eye, secured with a 1.2mm diameter stainless split-pin at each end. The pin passes through both handle walls and the eye web of the head. The primary retention is the wedge taper — the cross-pin is a secondary safety retention that prevents separation under the absolute worst-case load (impact at the very tip of the spike, maximum torsional loading). Cross-pin replacement requires a 5mm punch — carried in the utility kit.

**Handle surface:** The section between the tenon and the grip zone (approximately 120mm of bare titanium tube) is micro-blasted to a matte finish — Ra 3.2 microns — that prevents glare and provides enough surface texture for bare-hand grip in emergency use.

**Grip zone:** The lower 160mm of the handle carries the same TPE over-mould as the shovel and knife — shore 50A, raised 3mm hemispherical dimple pattern at 8mm spacing, swelling from 22mm to 28mm diameter at the palm centre. The grip surface covers all contact points for both a forward grip (thumb toward the head) and a reverse grip (thumb toward the butt), which are the two primary striking grips.

**Glass-breaker butt cap:** Ti-6Al-4V disc, 28mm diameter, threaded M20×1.5 onto the handle end. Centre face carries a 6mm diameter tungsten carbide ball insert, 2mm proud of the face, retained with a press-fit and 0.5mm tack weld. Tungsten carbide at 1,600 HV concentrates the strike force into the glass surface; the ball geometry self-centres on any angle of strike without the tip skating. Effective for tempered glass (vehicle windows, building entry) and for concrete fracture initiation.

**Lanyard eyelet:** A 6mm diameter stainless steel swaged loop, recessed flush into the butt cap centre on the face opposite the tungsten insert, accepts a 4mm Dyneema loop lanyard. The Dyneema loop is 250mm circumference — long enough to clear a gloved hand in a full swing arc but short enough to keep the pick under control if the grip is lost.

**Total handle weight:** 195g.

## 6. SURVIVAL KNIFE AND BAYONET

### 6.1 Design Philosophy

The knife must satisfy two operationally distinct requirements simultaneously without compromising either. As a survival knife, it must be capable of sustained field tasks: food preparation, cordage processing, hide work, shelter construction, fire preparation, emergency medical use including tourniquet cutting and clothing removal, and tool maintenance. As a bayonet, it must be geometrically and mechanically compatible with the EF88 Austeyr service rifle bayonet lug system — the primary individual weapon of the ADF.

These requirements are in tension. A pure survival knife optimises for a longer, more slender blade with a fine tip for detail work. A pure bayonet optimises for a shorter, stiffer blade with a reinforced tip for thrusting into resistance. The resolution is to optimise the blade geometry for survival knife performance and engineer the bayonet mounting system around that geometry — accepting a slightly longer mounted profile than a purpose-designed bayonet in exchange for a dramatically more capable field knife.

The blade is designed for the 95th-percentile use case (cutting, slicing, carving, food prep) not the 5th-percentile use case (bayonet assault). In any contemporary operational context, the probability of bayonet use in combat is extremely low; the probability of sustained field cutting tasks is near-certain. The design prioritises accordingly.

### 6.2 Blade

**Steel:** CPM-3V powder metallurgy tool steel. Composition: 0.80% C, 7.50% Cr, 2.75% V, 1.50% Mo, 0.50% Mn, 0.40% Si. CPM-3V is produced by Crucible Industries via the crucible particle metallurgy (CPM) process — the alloy is atomised into powder, which is then hot-isostatically pressed into billet. This process eliminates the carbide segregation that forms in conventionally cast and rolled steels, producing a homogeneous microstructure with uniform carbide distribution and no inclusion clusters.

The result is a steel with exceptional toughness — specifically, resistance to edge chipping and tip breakage under the lateral loads generated by prying, chopping, and bayonet thrust. CPM-3V at 60 HRC has a Charpy V-notch impact toughness of approximately 27 ft-lbs — comparable to some stainless steels at 55 HRC but at significantly higher hardness and consequently better edge retention. No stainless steel at 60 HRC approaches CPM-3V's toughness at that hardness.

The trade-off is corrosion resistance. CPM-3V is not stainless — with 7.5% chromium it sits below the 11% chromium threshold for stainless classification. In a humid or marine environment it will develop surface rust within 24–48 hours of unprotected exposure. This is addressed entirely by the PVD coating (see below) and the operator's maintenance routine (light oil application every 3 days, using the oil sachet in the IFAK). The performance advantage of CPM-3V over any available stainless steel at this hardness is sufficient to accept the maintenance requirement.

**Heat treatment:** Austenitised at 1,025°C, double-tempered at 180°C to 60–61 HRC. The double-temper converts secondary martensite formed during the first temper cycle, maximising toughness at the target hardness.

**Dimensions:**
- Blade length (guard to tip): 190mm
- Blade width (maximum, at 30mm from guard): 35mm
- Spine thickness: 5mm
- Overall length with grip: 320mm

**Grind:** Full flat grind. The blade faces are flat planes from the 5mm spine to the zero-ground edge — no hollow, no convex, no sabre. The full flat grind is the highest-performance geometry for a given spine thickness: it removes the most steel from the face, producing a thin cross-section at the cutting zone that requires the least force per cut. It is also the easiest geometry to resharpen in the field — a flat stone or the flat face of the ceramic rod produces the correct geometry without guesswork.

**Edge bevel:** 20 degrees inclusive (10 degrees per side). Ground to a mirror polish (Ra 0.05 microns) on the first 6mm above the cutting edge, transitioning to a satin finish (Ra 0.4 microns) above that. The mirror polish at the edge reduces friction during cutting strokes, improves corrosion resistance at the most vulnerable zone, and makes edge degradation visible — a mirror-polished edge shows wear as a visible reflection change.

**Tip geometry:** Modified clip point. The spine drops in a concave clip beginning at 150mm from the guard — a true concave clip (not a straight dropped point) formed by a radius of 45mm. The clip reduces tip mass, improves penetration geometry for thrusting, and produces the classic clip-point silhouette that is visually unambiguous in the field. The clip is false-edged for 45mm — ground to 0.5mm spine thickness in the clip zone. This is not a functional cutting edge but reduces the tip cross-section from 5mm × 35mm to approximately 0.5mm × 20mm over the last 45mm, significantly improving thrust penetration geometry.

**Spine serrations:** A 65mm section of file-cut serrations on the spine beginning 40mm from the guard (leaving 40mm of unserrated spine near the guard for thumb-on-spine grip). Serration geometry: 1.5mm deep, 60-degree included angle, 3mm pitch, 21 serrations total. The spine serrations are designed for cutting rope, webbing, 550 cord, and seatbelts under tension — tasks that require a dragging cut with the spine leading rather than the edge. They do not compromise the primary edge or the bayonet ring mounting zone (which begins at 15mm from the guard, outboard of the serrated zone).

### 6.3 Surface Treatment

**Process:** Physical vapour deposition (PVD) titanium aluminium nitride (TiAlN) coating — referred to commercially as black TiAlN or AlTiN depending on the aluminium-to-titanium ratio.

**Specification:** 3–4 micron coating thickness, applied at 400°C substrate temperature, producing a columnar microstructure with approximately 3,200 HV surface hardness. The coating composition is 65% aluminium nitride / 35% titanium nitride — the higher aluminium fraction improves high-temperature oxidation resistance (relevant for any heat application of the blade) and darkens the colour from the golden TiN to a flat matte charcoal-black.

**Coverage:** The entire blade surface including spine, flats, false edge, and serrations. The coating terminates at 0.5mm from the cutting edge — a 0.5mm uncoated band at the actual edge. This uncoated band serves two purposes: sharpenings do not require removing hard coating (the 3,200 HV coating cannot be removed by a ceramic rod — it would require diamond abrasive), and the uncoated edge zone remains the only visible non-black surface on the assembled knife, confirming visually that the edge is present and that the blade is oriented correctly.

**Reflectance:** Less than 2% in the 400–700nm visible spectrum — essentially zero visible reflection from any angle.

**Corrosion resistance:** Equivalent to 316L stainless steel in salt spray testing. The PVD coating is a continuous hermetic film that excludes oxygen and moisture from the underlying steel surface. Corrosion can only occur where the coating is breached — at the uncoated edge strip and at any mechanical damage to the coating.

### 6.4 Guard

**Material:** 7075-T6 aluminium, CNC-machined from billet, anodised black to MIL-A-8625 Type III specification (hard anodise, 25 micron coating, 400 HV surface hardness).

**Geometry:** 92mm wide × 22mm tall × 14mm thick. D-profile cross-section — flat face toward the blade, radius face toward the grip. The flat blade face provides a positive stop for the blade in the guard slot and a contact surface for the bayonet ring mounting force.

**Bayonet ring:** 22mm internal diameter ring drilled through the dominant-hand-side quillon of the guard. The ring internal diameter of 22mm is matched to the EF88 muzzle device outer diameter of 21.5mm — the 0.5mm clearance allows mounting without binding and with minimal lateral play. The ring wall thickness is 3mm — sufficient to withstand the bending moment applied during bayonet use, calculated at approximately 45 N·m at the ring-guard junction.

**Bayonet catch:** A spring-loaded retention lever recessed into a 20mm × 10mm × 6mm pocket machined into the flat face of the guard on the opposite side from the ring. The lever is 3mm thick stainless steel 17-4 PH, spring-loaded with a 0.5mm × 8mm stainless leaf spring. The catch engages the EF88 bayonet lug — a 17mm × 5mm rectangular protrusion on the underside of the barrel — when the bayonet ring is fully seated over the muzzle. The catch releases by thumb pressure of approximately 12N — operable with a gloved hand. Engagement and disengagement: under 3 seconds.

### 6.5 Grip

**Material:** G10 fibreglass laminate panels. G10 is a woven fibreglass cloth laminate cured under heat and pressure with epoxy resin. Its properties: dimensional stability across -40°C to +130°C, water absorption less than 0.1% by weight after 24-hour immersion, compressive strength 550 MPa, Rockwell M hardness 110. G10 does not absorb blood, mud, fuel, or solvent — critical for a survival knife that will be used in field medical scenarios.

**Geometry:** Two panels, each 125mm long × 30mm wide × 9mm thick. The panels have a 3mm hemispherical dimple pattern — identical to the war pick and shovel grip — at 8mm spacing across the full gripping surface. Three M4 × 0.7 stainless steel through-bolts with 3mm Allen-key socket heads secure each panel through the full-length tang. The bolt pattern is 25mm, 62mm, and 100mm from the guard face, providing even pressure distribution and preventing panel splitting.

**Grip diameter:** 28mm minimum, swelling to 33mm at the palm swell at 65mm from the guard face. The swell is produced by a 1.5mm thickness increase in both panels over a 30mm zone — machined by CNC milling to a 50mm radius profile.

**Colour:** G10 panels are available in olive drab, flat dark earth, and black. Standard specification: flat dark earth (FDE), which provides a non-reflective earth-tone that is camouflage-neutral across Australian operating environments.

### 6.6 Pommel

**Material:** Ti-6Al-4V, turned from bar stock, 35mm diameter × 22mm long. The pommel threads M22×1.5 onto the full-length tang that passes through the grip panels — the tang extends 25mm beyond the rear grip panel face and is threaded for the pommel. The pommel is secured with a thread-locking compound (Loctite 243 or equivalent, removable with heat) and can be disassembled with a strap wrench for tang maintenance.

**Glass-breaker:** 6mm tungsten carbide ball insert, 2mm proud of the domed pommel face, press-fit and tack-welded — identical specification to the war pick butt cap.

**Lanyard hole:** 5mm diameter hole through the pommel body, perpendicular to the blade axis.

### 6.7 Sheath

**Body material:** 3mm Kydex (acrylic-polyvinyl chloride thermoplastic sheet), thermoformed to the exact external geometry of the assembled knife including guard and grip. Kydex retains its formed shape indefinitely from -40°C to +93°C — it will not soften in vehicle storage or stiffen in cold conditions. It is waterproof, resistant to all common solvents and fuels, and can be re-formed in boiling water if impact damage alters the retention geometry.

**Retention:** A single adjustable retention screw (M4 × 0.7 stainless, 4mm Allen key) at the guard zone varies the interference between the Kydex and the guard. Factory setting provides an 8N draw force — enough retention to survive running, jumping, and rolling without drawing; light enough for a single deliberate draw. The retention setting is documented on the sheath body with a laser-engraved scale.

**Audible retention confirmation:** The Kydex deforms slightly as the guard passes the retention ridge and snaps back — producing an audible click that confirms full seating. This click can be felt through gloves and heard at 3 metres in quiet conditions.

**Ceramic sharpening rod:** A 150mm × 8mm diameter fine ceramic rod (400 grit) in a dedicated 12mm internal diameter sleeve bonded to the back face of the sheath body. The rod is retained by a 10mm flange at the top of the sleeve — it cannot fall out during movement. Ceramic rods maintain their flat geometry indefinitely and do not dish like whetstone surfaces; they are appropriate for the CPM-3V blade at 60 HRC because the ceramic (approximately 1,800 HV) is hard enough to abrade the steel.

**Attachment:** MOLLE-compatible lash tab system with a 60-degree forward cant — this positions the handle at an angle that allows a reverse-grip draw (fingers curling toward the body) which is faster for utility deployment than a forward-grip draw. A secondary 25mm ring at the sheath base accepts a leg strap for thigh carry when the pack is not worn.

**Integrated fire steel carrier:** A 55mm × 8mm tube pocket on the sheath front face carries a backup ferrocerium rod — a secondary to the primary fire kit, ensuring the operator has fire-starting capability if the main kit is separated.

## 7. INDIVIDUAL FIRST AID KIT (IFAK)

### 7.1 Overview

The IFAK is a pre-staged trauma kit designed for immediate life-threatening haemorrhage control and tension pneumothorax management — the two most survivable causes of preventable death in combat casualties. It is not a general medical kit; it contains no medications, no antibiotics, no analgesics, and nothing requiring medical training beyond Combat First Aid (CFA) or Tactical Combat Casualty Care (TCCC). Every item in the IFAK can be employed by any ADF-trained soldier in under 90 seconds.

The IFAK is mounted externally on the pack left shoulder strap, not inside the pack. External mounting is mandatory: a casualty who cannot open their pack cannot access an internal IFAK. The ADF's current TCCC doctrine requires the IFAK to be accessible to the casualty themselves or to a buddy in any position — standing, prone, seated, supine. The left shoulder strap mount satisfies all four.

### 7.2 Pouch

**Material:** 500D Cordura, red exterior with a white cross on all four visible faces (sewn embroidery, not printed — embroidered identification survives UV, abrasion, and washing without fading). Interior: bright orange 210D nylon liner.

**Dimensions:** 160mm wide × 120mm tall × 60mm deep. This is sized to contain exactly the items specified — no excess volume that allows contents to shift.

**Access:** The pouch opens on three sides via a single YKK AquaGuard 8 zipper forming a U. The entire front face of the pouch opens flat, allowing all contents to be seen simultaneously. A pull tab on the zipper is 80mm long neon orange webbing — visible with low vision and graspable with a gloved hand at maximum arm extension.

**Attachment:** MOLLE-compatible on the back face, attached to the left shoulder strap with four columns of MOLLE. A secondary sternum strap clip on the lower face of the pouch prevents the pouch from swinging forward during movement. The pouch can be stripped from the shoulder strap in under 5 seconds for use as a standalone kit.

**External CAT pocket:** A dedicated elastic pocket on the right face of the pouch (operator's right when the pouch is mounted on the left shoulder strap) holds one CAT tourniquet in the partially-pre-staged position — the band is threaded through the buckle and the windlass is loosened to one-hand application width. This pocket is specifically designed so the CAT can be grasped and applied to the dominant arm by the non-dominant hand without opening the main pouch. A 30mm wide elastic band with a pull-the-dot press stud retains the CAT.

### 7.3 Contents

**Tourniquet (×2):**
Combat Application Tourniquet (CAT), Generation 7. One in the external elastic pocket (see above), one inside the main compartment. The CAT Gen7 is the current ADF-issued tourniquet standard and has the highest published evidence base of any windlass tourniquet. Effective for all limbs on 50th–95th percentile ADF male limb circumferences.

**Haemostatic gauze (×2):**
QuikClot Combat Gauze, 3-inch × 4-yard. Z-fold packed for one-handed deployment. QuikClot Combat Gauze is impregnated with kaolin — an aluminosilicate mineral that activates factor XII (the contact activation pathway) of the coagulation cascade, accelerating clot formation by approximately 3× versus plain gauze. TCCC guidelines list Combat Gauze as the haemostatic agent of choice for junctional wounds (groin, axilla, neck) where a tourniquet cannot be applied.

**Chest seal — vented (×1):**
HyFin Vent Compact Chest Seal. Vented chest seals allow air to exit the pleural space during exhalation (preventing tension pneumothorax) while preventing air from entering during inhalation. Indicated for open chest wounds (sucking chest wounds). The compact format (90mm diameter × 8mm thick) fits in the pouch without displacement of other items.

**Chest seal — non-vented (×1):**
HyFin Chest Seal, non-vented version. Used when the mechanism of injury is unclear or when the wound is a small-calibre penetrating wound unlikely to have created a flutter valve effect. Carried alongside the vented version — the treating soldier selects based on presentation.

**Decompression needle (×1):**
14-gauge × 3.25-inch (83mm) needle thoracostomy device, pre-packaged with a one-way flutter valve on the hub. Indicated for tension pneumothorax — a life-threatening condition where air accumulates under pressure in the pleural space, compressing the mediastinum and reducing cardiac output. Recognisable by: absent breath sounds, tracheal deviation, hypotension, distended neck veins. Insertion point: 2nd intercostal space, midclavicular line. The 3.25-inch length is specified for ADF male chest wall thickness at the P95 — a shorter needle (2.5 inch, the older standard) fails to reach the pleural space in approximately 15% of adult males.

**SAM splint (×1):**
91cm × 11cm aluminium core / polyethylene foam SAM splint, rolled. Usable as an arm splint (forearm, wrist), leg splint (ankle, foot), cervical collar component, or improvised traction component. The 91cm length covers all adult limb segments except the femur, which requires a traction splint not carried in the IFAK.

**Nitrile examination gloves (×2 pairs):**
Size large (fits 80th-percentile ADF male hand), powder-free, 0.15mm wall. Two pairs — one pair for the treating soldier and a spare.

**Trauma shears (×1):**
89mm blade, stainless steel, bandage-cutting tips (blunt lower blade with protective protrusion). Shears are superior to the knife for clothing removal because the blunt lower blade runs along skin without risk of laceration, and the cutting is perpendicular to the clothing rather than requiring a dragging motion. Stored in a dedicated elastic loop inside the main pouch.

**Permanent marker (×1):**
Fine-tip permanent marker in a waterproof sleeve. Used to mark tourniquet application time on the casualty's skin or forehead — the TCCC TQ time standard. Waterproof designation — the time marking must survive precipitation and sweat.

**Emergency bandage (×1):**
6-inch Israeli bandage (Emergency Trauma Dressing). A pressure bandage with an integrated closure bar that converts it to a pressure dressing without additional hardware. Usable as a wound packing reinforcement, abdominal evisceration cover, and improvised eye patch.

## 9. FIRE, SHELTER, AND BIVOUAC

### 9.1 Ferrocerium Fire Rod

**Specification:** 75mm × 12mm diameter ferrocerium rod — a cerium-iron alloy (approximately 70% cerium, 24% iron, 4% lanthanum, 2% other rare earths). The cerium alloy produces sparks at approximately 3,000°C — significantly above the autoignition temperature of all common tinder materials including damp natural tinder, which autoignites at approximately 230°C. The temperature advantage over a standard ferro rod (approximately 2,400°C for a magnesium-dominant composition) provides reliable ignition of damp tinder that a standard rod fails on.

The 12mm diameter is the correct size for field use — smaller rods (6–8mm) are exhausted quickly and are difficult to grip with cold or wet hands; larger rods (15mm+) add unnecessary weight. At 12mm diameter and 75mm length, this rod contains approximately 8,500 strikes — at 5 strikes per fire-lighting attempt and 3 attempts per fire, approximately 560 fires. At one fire per day, this is a 1.5-year supply.

**Striker:** A 3mm thick × 35mm wide hardened steel striker with a 90-degree working edge, retained on a 150mm Dyneema lanyard attached to the rod. The striker edge is maintained at 90 degrees by the lanyard attachment geometry — the lanyard prevents the operator from inverting the striker. The striker can be used as a scraper for adhesive removal and as a rough file for field metal work.

**Handle:** The rod is pressed into a 30mm length of TPE sleeve at the non-working end, providing grip and protecting the rod face during storage.

**Waterproofing:** The rod is stored in a small polyethylene tube with a press-fit cap — the tube is 15mm diameter × 90mm long, snap-fit, and provides protection against the sustained moisture absorption that reduces ferrocerium spark temperature after extended immersion. The tube cap has a 4mm lanyard hole.

## 10. NUTRITION SYSTEM

### 10.1 Overview

The nutrition system for this field kit integrates two purpose-designed products developed under the same research programme: the TACT-1 Mark II compact ration bar and the Advanced Sports Nutrition Powder (ASNP) energy drink. This section summarises the nutrition system for the field kit specification. Full technical detail is documented in TRP-2026-021 (TACT-1 Mark II Research Paper) and the ASNP Technical Specification Document.

### 10.2 TACT-1 Mark II Ration Bar

- Energy: 842 kcal (3,525 kJ)

## 11. PERSONAL HYGIENE

### 11.1 Overview

Personal hygiene in the field is an operational issue, not a comfort issue. Skin breakdown from sustained moisture, abrasion, and UV exposure reduces the operator's ability to perform tasks — blistered hands cannot grip weapons or ropes; infected feet cannot carry a load; UV-damaged skin is more susceptible to infection from minor abrasions. The hygiene kit is designed to address these failure modes at minimum weight and maximum practical field deployability.

The Hemp Harmony luxury body lotion formulation is included as the primary skin maintenance product. Its clinical-grade ingredient set — hemp seed oil GLA/LA barrier repair, Tremella polysaccharide trans-epidermal hydration, Centella asiatica collagen synthesis stimulation, hibiscus elastase inhibition, sea buckthorn omega-7 anti-inflammatory — is directly relevant to the field skin environment. An operator's skin on sustained operations is subjected to sustained UV, repeated wetting and drying cycles, abrasion from equipment contact, and reduced nutrient supply from inadequate caloric intake. A product engineered to repair the lipid bilayer, reduce inflammatory response, and maintain barrier function is not a luxury in this context — it is a force maintenance tool.

## 17. PROCUREMENT AND SUPPLY CHAIN

### 17.1 Manufacture and Assembly

The field kit is designed for Australian domestic manufacture wherever feasible, consistent with ADF procurement preference for sovereign industrial capability. The titanium components — shovel head, war pick head, knife pommel, filter housing — require investment casting or forging capability operating with Ti-6Al-4V. Three Australian manufacturers currently hold the relevant capability: Sutton Tools (Victoria), ANCA (Victoria), and Pacific Titanium (New South Wales). International sourcing from Japanese manufacturers (e.g., NHK Spring, Kobe Steel) provides a second-source option at comparable quality and lower tooling cost for initial prototype runs.

The pack shell is within Australian sewn-goods manufacturing capability — several Victorian and Queensland manufacturers operate military-specification textile production lines. YKK is the sole-source zipper supplier; YKK maintains Australian distribution in Melbourne. All nylon webbing to MIL-W-4088 specification is available from Australian distributors.

The hollow fibre membrane and UV-C module are most cost-effectively sourced from existing specialist manufacturers: Toray Industries (Japan) produces 0.01 micron PVDF hollow fibre to military specification; Klaran (US/Germany) produces military-grade UV-C LED assemblies. Both have existing ADF logistics supply relationships through MINDEF partner companies.

The TACT-1 Mark II ration requires a food-grade manufacturer capable of TGase crosslinking, single-screw food extrusion, and carnauba wax dip coating. No current Australian manufacturer holds this combination of capability in one facility. The production pathway is: TGase crosslinking and spray drying at a dairy ingredient manufacturer (Bega Dairy, NSW; or Fonterra, NZ); extrusion and coating at a confectionery manufacturer (Darrell Lea, NSW; or Nestlé Rowntree, NSW). These are existing military food contract holders.

### 17.2 Phased Development and Procurement Timeline

**Phase 1 — Prototype (Months 1–6):**
Produce 50 complete kits for operator trials. Prioritise the filtration system, entrenching tool, and ration bar for functional testing. Pack and tool prototyping via direct machining (not casting) for dimensional validation before tooling investment.

**Phase 2 — Limited User Trial (Months 7–12):**
Issue 200 complete kits to SOCOMD and 1 RAR for a 6-month operational trial. Collect structured feedback on: pack fit, filtration flow rate, shovel handle ergonomics, war pick balance, ration palatability and satiety. Conduct blind palatability trials for all 40 TACT-1 flavour variants.

**Phase 3 — Design Finalisation (Months 13–15):**
Incorporate trial feedback. Commission production tooling (investment casting dies, extrusion tooling, Kydex thermoforming tools). Establish contracted supply chain for all components.

**Phase 4 — Initial Production (Months 16–24):**
Produce 2,000 kits for ADF special operations and lead infantry units. Complete HPLC mineral stability validation for TACT-1 Mark II (open validation item per TRP-2026-021). Obtain ADF logistics catalogue entries for all kit components.

**Phase 5 — Full Procurement (Months 25–36):**
Scale to 12,000 kit procurement across all dismounted infantry, combat engineer, and special operations units.

### 17.3 Logistics and Maintenance

**Pack:** Annual inspection by unit quartermaster. Zipper replacement at signs of membrane degradation (typically 4–5 years). Shoulder harness foam replacement at 3-year interval (foam compression set reduces load transfer efficiency). Frame stays replacement if bent; frames are a stocked spare part.

**Filtration system:** Membrane replacement at rated capacity or every 3 years. Pump tube replacement every 200 litres or 6 months. UV LED replacement at 10,000 operating hours (approximately 15 years). Battery replacement annually or on depletion.

**Tools:** The titanium tools (shovel, war pick, knife) are effectively maintenance-free except for blade and spike maintenance (periodic sharpening), surface coating touch-up (Parkerising touch-up kit stocked at unit level), and handle grip replacement (TPE over-mould can be replaced by any facility with injection moulding capability).

**IFAK:** Full consumable restock after every deployment or training exercise. CAT tourniquets: inspect for UV degradation of nylon webbing annually; replace every 5 years or on UV-cracking of buckle components. Chest seals: replace at expiry date (typically 5 years from manufacture). Decompression needle: replace at expiry date (sterility). Haemostatic gauze: replace at expiry date (kaolin activity unaffected by age, but sterile packaging integrity is the shelf-life limit, typically 5 years).

### 18.6 Operational Testing Requirements

Prior to full procurement, the following operational validation trials must be completed:

1. **72-hour satiety trial:** Controlled trial with 40 ADF volunteers, TACT-1 Mark II versus IRP over 3 days of simulated operational activity. Primary endpoint: subjective hunger VAS score. Secondary: body composition change, performance on standardised physical tasks.

2. **Pack load carriage trial:** Biomechanical comparison of load carriage system against current ADF-issued pack. Primary endpoint: L4/L5 spinal compressive force at 20kg load on 10km movement. Secondary: shoulder strap pressure distribution, energy expenditure.

3. **Filtration performance trial:** Microbiological challenge testing with E. coli (>6-log reduction), MS2 bacteriophage (>4-log reduction, virus surrogate), and Cryptosporidium parvum oocysts (>3-log reduction). Challenge water: synthetic turbid water at 10 NTU.

4. **Tool fatigue trial:** 5,000-cycle fatigue loading of shovel head at 150% rated load; 500-impact fatigue of war pick spike into steel plate at 400g head mass; knife blade three-point bend to failure.

5. **Environmental exposure trial:** Complete kit exposure to 72-hour tropical rain chamber (50mm/hr precipitation), followed by 72-hour desert heat chamber (55°C), followed by 24-hour cold exposure (-10°C). Functional verification of all components after each phase.
