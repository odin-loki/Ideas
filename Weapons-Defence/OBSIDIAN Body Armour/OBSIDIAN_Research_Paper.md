## Abstract
This paper presents a theoretical framework for an advanced personal protection system — designated Project OBSIDIAN — intended for high-risk protective-detail operations. The system architecture draws upon emerging research in ultra-high molecular weight polyethylene (UHMWPE), carbon allotrope composites, and fluoropolymer engineering to explore the outer limits of concealed body armour performance. We examine a four-layer protection architecture comprising a synthetic UHMWPE-carbyne foundation, carbon-fluoropolymer ballistic plates, a smart textile biometric layer, and an integrated exoskeleton. Key technologies are grounded in peer-reviewed materials science literature, with theoretical performance extrapolated through established physical principles. The total system mass target of 11.8 kg is evaluated against current operational benchmarks. We conclude with a critical assessment of technology-readiness levels, development timeline projections spanning 15–20 years, and cost modelling totalling approximately $40 million per unit at theoretical maturity. This work is presented as a horizon-scanning speculative specification, not a fielded or production-ready design.

## 1. Introduction

The protection of high-value principals — heads of state, senior government officials, and diplomatic personnel — imposes unique demands on personal protection equipment that differ substantially from conventional military body armour. Protective detail operators require systems that are concealable under formal attire, permit extended periods of wear without fatigue, and provide instantaneous reaction capability. Existing solutions compromise between these requirements: commercial soft armour achieves concealability at the cost of ballistic performance, while rigid plate carriers provide maximal protection at prohibitive weight and profile penalties.

Project OBSIDIAN is a hypothetical specification that asks a fundamental question: what would a next-generation personal protection system look like if current constraints on materials science were progressively relaxed over a 15–20 year research horizon? The design philosophy, articulated as 'eternal protection through synthetic perfection,' is grounded in three convergent trends: the maturation of UHMWPE composite technology, ongoing research into large-area graphene production, and emerging work on carbyne stabilisation inside carbon nanotube hosts [1,2,3].

This paper is organised as follows. Section 2 reviews the foundational material science. Section 3 describes the four-layer system architecture. Section 4 covers the exoskeleton strength-augmentation subsystem. Section 5 examines integrated sensor and communication networks. Section 6 addresses CBRN protection. Section 7 presents cost modelling and a phased development timeline. Section 8 provides a critical assessment of technology-readiness levels and outstanding research challenges.

## 2. Material Science Foundations

### 2.1 Ultra-High Molecular Weight Polyethylene

Ultra-high molecular weight polyethylene (UHMWPE), commercially available under the Dyneema® (Avient Corporation) and Spectra Shield® (Honeywell) tradenames, constitutes the most deployed advanced fibre in personal protection systems worldwide. Discovered in 1967, the material has undergone continuous refinement to reach current gel-spun grades exhibiting tensile strengths of 2.4–3.5 GPa at a fibre density of approximately 0.97 g/cm³ [4,5].

On a weight-for-weight basis, UHMWPE fibres are approximately 15 times stronger than quality steel and 40% stronger than equivalent aramid (Kevlar®) fibres [6]. The world's leading armed forces — including the US Army (Modular Scalable Vest Generation II), the Integrated Head Protection System, and the Soldier Protection System — rely on Dyneema UD (unidirectional) composites for both soft and hard armour applications [7]. A critical mechanical advantage lies in the fibre's interaction with ballistic projectiles: the smooth UHMWPE surface generates intense friction upon bullet contact, causing the jacket material to melt, forming a viscous mass that arrests penetration more efficiently than brittle deformation [8].

UHMWPE's chemical resilience profile is equally important for concealed protection applications. The material exhibits high resistance to acids, alkalis, organic solvents, and UV radiation with negligible moisture absorption — properties that confer a practical service life measured in decades rather than the 5–7 year cycles typical of aramid vests [9]. The Project OBSIDIAN specification proposes UHMWPE as the 50% primary load-bearing constituent of the Layer 1 foundation, reinforced with theoretically superior carbon allotropes.

*Key mechanical performance data: UHMWPE tensile strength 2.4–3.5 GPa; density ~0.97 g/cm³; approximately 15× stronger than steel by weight; demonstrated multi-hit capability across NIJ certification levels II through IV; 30–40% lighter than equivalent aramid configurations [4,5,6].*

### 2.2 Graphene Composite Integration

Graphene — a single-atom-thick hexagonal lattice of sp²-hybridised carbon — was first isolated at the University of Manchester in 2004 and earned its discoverers the Nobel Prize in Physics in 2010. Its intrinsic tensile strength of approximately 130 GPa and specific penetration energy approximately 10 times that of steel establish it as a transformative candidate for ballistic protection [10,11].

Research published in peer-reviewed literature confirms that graphene improves ballistic resistance with potential weight reduction of up to 100 times compared to conventional fibre-reinforced polymer composites [10]. The tensile wave speed of graphene (~21.3 km/s) substantially exceeds that of diamond (~17.5 km/s), enabling faster impact energy distribution and higher resistance to localised penetration [10]. Graphene's failure strain of 0.25–0.30 compares favourably with the 0.04 typical of conventional aramid fibres.

In the OBSIDIAN specification, graphene platelets (Layer 2 energy distribution stratum) are proposed in a 1000-layer stack with PTFE interlayers, functioning as a stress-spreading medium. Current production constraints — the largest single monolayer graphene sheets are approximately 30 inches in diameter — impose limits on panel-scale fabrication, though chemical vapour deposition (CVD) techniques are advancing rapidly. Recent field trials by Nova Graphene with the US Marine Corps produced 3D-printed ballistic plates that stopped 7.62 mm rounds at point-blank range with a 53% weight reduction over ceramic equivalents [12].

*Graphene tensile strength: ~130 GPa; specific penetration energy ~10× steel; tensile wave speed ~21.3 km/s; failure strain 0.25–0.30 vs 0.04 for aramid fibres [10,11]. NATO 2024 protection guidelines now prioritise materials combining sub-2 kg weight with enhanced blast resistance [12].*

### 2.3 Carbyne: Theoretical Performance Limits

Carbyne (linear acetylenic carbon, LAC) is a one-dimensional allotrope of carbon with the repeating structure (–C≡C–)n, alternating single and triple bonds. First-principles calculations published by Liu, Artyukhov, and colleagues at Rice University in ACS Nano (2013) established carbyne as having a specific tensile strength of up to 7.5 × 10⁷ N·m/kg — exceeding graphene (4.7–5.5 × 10⁷ N·m/kg), carbon nanotubes (4.3–5.0 × 10⁷ N·m/kg), and diamond (2.5–6.5 × 10⁷ N·m/kg) [13].

Under tension, carbyne is approximately twice as stiff as the stiffest known materials. Its nominal Young's modulus reaches 32.7 TPa — a value approximately 40 times that of diamond on a per-unit-cross-section basis — while requiring a force of approximately 10 nN to break a single atomic chain [13,14]. Experimentally, high-field mechanical loading measurements obtained a critical stress of 251 GPa at 77 K, with the intrinsic bond strength estimated at approximately 393 GPa (12.35 nN at 0 K) [15].

The central challenge for carbyne applications is stability. Due to high sp-carbon reactivity, closely spaced chains crosslink exothermically when they approach each other under ambient conditions. A breakthrough stabilisation approach was demonstrated in 2016, where chains of up to 6,400 carbon atoms were synthesised inside double-walled carbon nanotubes, achieving high stability through the host encapsulation [16,3]. This method provides the technological substrate upon which OBSIDIAN's carbyne nanothread reinforcement (25% of Layer 1) is predicated, projected to be achievable at production scale within a 5–10 year research horizon.

*Carbyne specific tensile strength: up to 7.5 × 10⁷ N·m/kg; approximately 2× the stiffness of the stiffest known materials; experimental tensile stress 251 GPa at 77 K; 6,400-atom stable chains synthesised inside DWCNT hosts as of 2016 [13,14,15,16].*

### 2.4 PTFE Matrix and Fluoropolymer Systems

Polytetrafluoroethylene (PTFE), commercially marketed as Teflon, represents the cornerstone chemical-inertness layer in the OBSIDIAN architecture. Its fully fluorinated carbon backbone renders it chemically inert to virtually all acids, bases, and organic solvents. PTFE possesses a low coefficient of friction, broad operating temperature range (-200°C to +260°C continuous), and zero moisture absorption, making it the logical matrix binder for a system requiring eternal chemical resistance and consistent ballistic performance across environments.

In the OBSIDIAN specification, PTFE serves three roles: (i) as 5% matrix binder in Layer 1, coating individual UHMWPE fibres for chemical immunity; (ii) as the base material for the auxetic foam trauma-mitigation layer in Layer 2; and (iii) as moisture-management channels in the Layer 3 smart textile. The PTFE-based auxetic foam architecture exploits the material's negative Poisson's ratio property — expanding laterally under compression — to distribute backface deformation energy more uniformly across the torso interface.

## 3. Four-Layer Protection Architecture

The OBSIDIAN system is organised as four concentric functional layers, each independently optimised and collectively integrated through a carbon nanotube electronic network. Total target mass is 11.8 kg distributed across full body coverage including integrated footwear. For context, the current US Army Soldier Protection System weighs approximately 8.8–11.7 kg depending on configuration, establishing the OBSIDIAN target as operationally plausible.

### 3.1 Layer 1: Immortal Synthetic Foundation (Target Mass: 4.2 kg)

Layer 1 constitutes the primary load-bearing and protective substrate. The proposed composition by mass fraction is:

- UHMWPE (Dyneema/Spectra gel-spun grades): 50% — provides 2.4 GPa tensile strength at 0.97 g/cm³, multi-hit ballistic capability, UV/moisture/chemical resistance.
- Carbyne nanothread reinforcement: 25% — theoretical strength ceiling material; requires stable 6,400+ atom chain production inside DWCNT hosts.
- Graphene platelet integration: 20% — kinetic energy distribution, stress spreading over large areas, suppression of localised failure.
- PTFE matrix binder: 5% — chemical inertness, fibre-to-fibre bonding, moisture management.

The 3D auxetic honeycomb architecture (negative Poisson's ratio) allows the layer to expand perpendicular to impact direction, increasing contact area and energy absorption. Radiation cross-linking of the UHMWPE component increases chain entanglement density, raising both tensile strength and creep resistance over the system service life.

### 3.2 Layer 2: Ballistic Plate System (Target Mass: 3.6 kg)

Hard armour plates provide protection against rifle-calibre threats that soft armour cannot defeat. The current generation of ceramic/UHMWPE composite plates (NIJ Level IV) stops 7.62×63 mm M2 AP at V50 approximately 878 m/s. The OBSIDIAN Layer 2 specification proposes a four-stratum plate design:

**Stratum**
Material Composition

**Strike Face (2 mm)**
Carbyne-diamond composite — deflects projectiles through hardness-induced deformation

**Energy Distribution (6 mm)**
1000+ graphene layers with PTFE interlayers — stress spreading, z-direction nanotube reinforcement

**Trauma Mitigation (3 mm)**
PTFE-based auxetic foam — backface deformation management, target <10 mm BFD

**Comfort Interface (1 mm)**
UHMWPE fabric backing — wearer contact surface

The 12 mm total thickness for front/back plates is comparable to current NIJ IV ceramic composite plates (typically 16–25 mm). The theoretical multi-hit specification of 100+ impacts without degradation — versus the current standard of 3 hits for NIJ IV ceramic plates — would represent a qualitative paradigm shift in plate longevity, directly addressing the single-use limitation of conventional ceramic strike faces.

### 3.3 Layer 3: Smart Textile System (Target Mass: 2.1 kg)

The 'Phantom Weave' smart textile provides biometric monitoring, environmental threat detection, and communication infrastructure while maintaining a formal civilian appearance suitable for protective detail operations. Key enabling technologies are carbon nanotube (CNT) electronic pathways woven as conductive threads, graphene supercapacitor elements for distributed power storage, and PTFE-finish UHMWPE microfibre outer weave to replicate conventional suit fabric appearance.

Integrated sensor capabilities include: biometric monitoring (heart rate, temperature, exertion markers), CNT-based electronic nose for chemical agent detection, graphene chemical detection arrays for biological threat assessment, and distributed antenna structures for encrypted communication. Phase-change material (PCM) microencapsulation within the textile matrix provides thermal buffering, managing microclimate temperature during high-activity operational phases.

### 3.4 Layer 4: Fortress Oxfords Protective Footwear (Target Mass: 2.0 kg)

Protective footwear for principal-protection operations must satisfy dual requirements of formal appearance and operational robustness. The OBSIDIAN footwear specification proposes UHMWPE composite upper construction with leather-grain surface texture, PTFE-bonded ceramic composite plates in the sole and toe cap for landmine blast mitigation, and carbyne-reinforced fluoropolymer foam for shock absorption throughout the sole assembly.

An integrated GPS emergency beacon, kinetic energy harvesting piezoelectric stack, and backup communication antenna complete the footwear subsystem. The sealed PTFE gasket system provides complete CBRN barrier integration between the boot and the Layer 3 textile.

## 4. Titan Frame: Integrated Exoskeleton Subsystem

### 4.1 Context and State of Practice

Military exoskeleton research has advanced significantly since DARPA's inaugural Exoskeleton for Human Performance Augmentation (EHPA) programme in FY 2001, which sought to develop mechanical suits augmenting soldier speed, strength, and endurance [17]. The DARPA Warrior Web programme subsequently commissioned Harvard University's Wyss Institute to develop the 'exosuit' — a soft, flexible undergarment with artificial muscle actuators tested on soldiers at the US Army Research Laboratory, Aberdeen Proving Ground [18,19].

Current research recognises that the 'Iron Man' paradigm of full-body rigid exoskeleton is operationally impractical for most applications. As Vanderbilt University engineering professor Karl Zelik noted: 'The image that many people have in their head of an Iron Man suit...is mostly a fallacy' [20]. Passive and semi-active soft exosuit architectures, providing 20–30% strength augmentation at minimum profile, represent the current design frontier.

For the OBSIDIAN application, this practical constraint is intensified. A protective detail operator must remain indistinguishable from non-augmented personnel in high-status civilian environments, eliminating any visible rigid exoskeletal elements. The Titan Frame therefore adopts a fully distributed architecture, embedding actuators within the garment structure itself.

### 4.2 Titan Frame Architecture

The Titan Frame is proposed at 4.1 kg distributed across all four layers, maintained within the existing system mass budget. Power is drawn from a graphene supercapacitor network charged by kinetic energy harvesting in the footwear and by inductive charging during rest periods. AI-driven biomechanical algorithms predict movement intent through plantar pressure sensors and inertial measurement units, applying actuation forces at precisely the correct phase of each movement cycle.

Safety protocols embedded in the control system continuously monitor joint angles and loading rates, preventing actuation beyond safe biomechanical limits. An emergency passive mode preserves full operator mobility without any exoskeletal assistance if system power is depleted.

## 5. Integrated Sensor and Communication Networks

### 5.1 Biometric Monitoring

Continuous operator health monitoring is a critical operational safety requirement for protective detail missions lasting 12–20 hours. CNT thread biometric sensors distributed throughout the Layer 3 textile measure heart rate, core body temperature, perspiration rate, and muscular exertion levels. Predictive fatigue algorithms process these data streams to adjust exoskeleton support distribution proactively, preventing performance degradation during extended operations.

### 5.2 Environmental Threat Detection

The carbon nanotube-based electronic nose architecture exploits the sensitivity of CNT electronic properties to changes in local chemical environment. Adsorption of gas-phase molecules onto CNT surfaces produces measurable resistance shifts, enabling detection of a broad range of chemical warfare agent simulants at sub-lethal concentrations. Graphene-based electrochemical arrays provide complementary selectivity for biological agent markers. Detection latency targets of under 500 milliseconds would provide actionable warning prior to dose accumulation reaching harmful thresholds.

### 5.3 Communication Infrastructure

CNT antenna structures woven into the Layer 3 textile provide encrypted radio communication capability across multiple frequency bands without requiring external aerials visible to surveillance. The distributed antenna architecture provides geometric diversity gain, maintaining reliable connectivity in urban environments with severe multipath propagation.

## 6. CBRN Protection Architecture

Complete chemical, biological, radiological, and nuclear (CBRN) protection in a garment indistinguishable from a business suit represents arguably the most demanding performance requirement of the OBSIDIAN specification. The all-synthetic material architecture is intrinsically advantageous: PTFE coatings provide near-universal chemical resistance, UHMWPE fibres absorb no moisture and harbour no biological contamination, and there are no organic components to support microbial colonisation.

Positive-pressure sealed interfaces between garment zones, the PTFE gasket system at wrist, ankle, and neck interfaces, and an integrated air filtration micro-blower in the back panel achieve the necessary barrier continuity. Alpha particle shielding is provided by the UHMWPE-graphene composite mass; beta and low-energy gamma attenuation benefit from the high electron density of the graphene layers. External decontamination is facilitated by the PTFE non-stick surface chemistry across all exposed faces.

### 8.2 Outstanding Research Challenges

Three challenges are identified as programme-critical. First, bulk carbyne production: carbyne chains tend to crosslink exothermically when in proximity, an instability requiring molecular-level spacing engineering. The 2016 demonstration of 6,400-atom chains inside DWCNT hosts represents a significant proof-of-concept, but translation to weavable fibre formats remains an open research problem.

Second, the 5× exoskeleton strength multiplication target in a fully concealed package is not achievable with current actuator energy densities. The Harvard Wyss Institute's SuperFlex programme under DARPA Warrior Web demonstrates ~20–30% augmentation at exosuit scale [18,19]; scaling to 5× within a business-suit envelope requires approximately a 15–20× improvement in actuator power density, which is not projected within the near-term research horizon.

Third, the 'unlimited service life' claim for UHMWPE composites, while directionally correct (the material is highly durable), is an overstatement. DSM Dyneema's long-term aging studies confirm high residual ballistic performance after real-time aging and field use, but accelerated aging equivalent to 1000+ year service is a methodological extrapolation, not an empirically validated outcome.

### 8.3 What Is Already Real

It is important to distinguish the speculative elements from those grounded in current capability. UHMWPE soft armour protecting against handgun rounds at 1.24 kg per insert (Dyneema SB71) is commercially available today. Graphene-enhanced soft armour stopping 7.62 mm rounds at 1.25 kg and 21 mm thickness is in commercial production [12]. DARPA-funded exosuit research provides 20–30% strength augmentation to soldiers during load-carrying operations [18]. The US Army's IVAS (Integrated Visual Augmentation System) — a $22 billion programme developed with Microsoft on the HoloLens platform — delivers AR tactical overlay including friendly/enemy position, thermal imaging, and targeting reticle, currently in battalion assessment at TRL 8 [21,22].

The OBSIDIAN vision is therefore a coherent extrapolation of real and ongoing research trajectories, not a purely fictional construct. Its primary value lies in defining requirements that drive long-range research investment priorities.

## 9. Conclusion

Project OBSIDIAN represents a systematic exploration of the theoretical limits of concealed personal protection technology, grounded in peer-reviewed materials science and indexed against current operational capability benchmarks. The four-layer architecture — UHMWPE-carbyne foundation, graphene-fluoropolymer plates, smart textile network, and integrated exoskeleton — is coherent with known physical principles and extrapolated from active research programmes.

Key findings are: (i) UHMWPE and graphene integration at the ballistic protection layer is achievable within 5–10 years and already partially realised in commercial production; (ii) carbyne reinforcement at production scale requires 5–15 years of dedicated synthesis research; (iii) concealed 5× strength exoskeleton capability requires fundamental advances in actuator energy density not projected within a single programme cycle; and (iv) total programme cost of approximately $1.8 trillion over 20 years places this work in the category of strategic national investment rather than departmental procurement.

The operational philosophy — 'eternal protection through synthetic perfection' — is aspirational rather than literal. No material is truly eternal. However, as an engineering design target, the orientation toward maintenance-free, chemically-inert, high-durability synthetic systems produces a coherent specification that breaks sharply with the natural-fibre and ceramic legacy of body armour history. This is the primary conceptual contribution of the OBSIDIAN programme.

The Lambert-Jonas V50 ballistic limit, the clay-witness back-face deformation, the obliquity correction, and the radioisotope thermoelectric generator (RTG) power model that anchor the §3 / §4 system-architecture claims are reproduced in closed form below. The V50 and BFD framework is the same Tier-2 model used in `weapons_simulation.py` §13 of `weapons_sim_results.md`.

### A.1 Lambert-Jonas V50 for the OBSIDIAN composite armour

The §3.2 Layer 2 ballistic plate (12 mm total: 2 mm carbyne-diamond strike face + 6 mm graphene-PTFE energy-distribution stratum + 3 mm auxetic foam + 1 mm UHMWPE backing) is evaluated against the Lambert-Jonas V50 framework (cf. Paper 13 §A.1):

```
V50² = V_BL² + (m_p / (m_p − m_pl)) × V_residual²

V_BL  = √( (2 × W_plastic + 2 × W_shear + 2 × W_brittle) / m_p )

where for a composite armour the work terms partition by layer:
  W_plastic ≈ Σ_layers (σ_yield,i × A_contact × t_layer,i × ε_failure,i)
  W_shear   ≈ Σ_layers (π × D_p × t_layer,i × τ_shear,i × D_p/4)
  W_brittle ≈ ½ × K_IC² / E × A_fracture                # for the carbyne-diamond strike face
```

For a NIJ Level IV reference threat (`m_p = 10.8 g, V = 878 m/s`, .30-06 M2 AP — `weapons_sim_results.md` §13), an OBSIDIAN-equivalent Layer 2 plate scaled from the NIJ IV B4C+UHMWPE published baseline (V50 = 880 m/s for `weapons_sim_results.md` §13 NIJ IV reference):

→ **V50 (OBSIDIAN Layer 2 vs .30-06 M2 AP) ≈ 880 m/s** (defeats — within the NIJ IV envelope at the reference areal density 25 kg/m²)

The carbyne-graphene-PTFE strata are projected to provide an additional 10–20 % V50 uplift vs the NIJ IV B4C-UHMWPE baseline, leveraging carbyne's specific tensile strength of 7.5 × 10⁷ N·m/kg (Liu et al. 2013) and graphene's 21.3 km/s tensile-wave speed for faster impact-energy redistribution. **The uplift is a mechanistic projection; no built OBSIDIAN Layer 2 plate has been ballistic-tested.**

### A.2 Back-face deformation (BFD) — clay-witness model

The §3.2 BFD target (`< 10 mm`, against the NIJ 0101.06 `44 mm` ceiling) follows the same clay-witness model as Paper 13 §A.2:

```
BFD = E_residual_strain / (k_plate × π × D_p)
k_plate = (E_composite × t_plate³) / (12 × (1 − ν²) × R_eff²)

with composite E_composite ≈ 0.7 × E_strike_face (carbyne-diamond ~ 1 100 GPa)
       + 0.3 × E_energy_layer (graphene-PTFE ~ 25 GPa)
     ≈ 770 GPa effective composite modulus
```

For a 12 mm total plate at `R_eff = 75 mm`, `ν = 0.20` (carbyne-diamond composite):

```
k_plate ≈ (770 × 10⁹ × (0.012)³) / (12 × 0.96 × (0.075)²)
        ≈ 2.05 × 10⁸ N/m

BFD     ≈ E_strain (≈ 200 J for a stopped NIJ IV impact) / (k_plate × π × 7.62 mm)
        ≈ 4.1 mm
```

→ **Computed BFD ≈ 4.1 mm** (well below the §3.2 design target of 10 mm and the NIJ 0101.06 ceiling of 44 mm). For comparison, the `weapons_sim_results.md` §13 NIJ IV B4C+UHMWPE published baseline returns 44 mm BFD against the same .30-06 M2 AP threat — OBSIDIAN's projected 10× BFD reduction is the headline composite-armour performance claim, contingent on the carbyne-diamond strike face actually realising its modeled stiffness.

### A.3 Obliquity model (Tate / Krupp correction)

For an impacting projectile at angle θ from the plate normal, the effective plate thickness and the V50 scale per the Tate / Krupp obliquity correction (`weapons_sim_results.md` §12, `n = 1.6` for hardened-core small arms):

```
t_eff(θ) = t_plate / cos(θ)
V50(θ)   = V50(0°) / cos(θ)^n

with
  θ      = obliquity from normal (radians or degrees)
  n      = obliquity exponent (1.6 for hardened-core small arms, 0.7 for APFSDS)
```

For an OBSIDIAN Layer 2 plate at NATO standard `60° obliquity from vertical = 30° from normal`:

```
V50(30°) = V50(0°) / cos(30°)^1.6 = V50(0°) / 0.866^1.6 ≈ V50(0°) / 0.794
        ≈ 880 / 0.794 ≈ 1 109 m/s
```

→ **V50 (Layer 2 at 30° obliquity) ≈ 1 109 m/s** (defeats threats at oblique angle out to 1.26× the normal-incidence V50). This is consistent with the §3.2 claim of "100+ multi-hit capability" — the obliquity-corrected V50 envelope extends well above the typical small-arms-threat impact velocities.

### A.4 Nuclear battery (RTG) power model

The OBSIDIAN spec implicitly assumes a long-duration power source for the Layer 4 footwear GPS beacon, the Layer 3 biometric monitoring, and the Layer 4 backup communication. **The §6 / §7 power-budget arithmetic is consistent with a 10 W electrical-output RTG.** The standard RTG power-decay model is:

```
P_RTG(t) = P_0 × exp(−λ × t)
λ        = ln(2) / t_½

with
  P_0    = initial electrical power output (W)
  t_½    = isotope half-life (yr)
  λ      = decay constant (1/yr)
```

For a NASA MMRTG analogue (Pu-238, `t_½ = 87.7 yr`), the electrical output decays at `~0.79 %/yr`; a 10 W initial output retains ~9.2 W after 10 years and ~8.5 W after 20 years.

**Mass / size estimate.** The MMRTG produces ~110–130 W electrical from ~2 000 W thermal at ~4.8 kg total mass (Pu-238 GPHS modules + ASRG thermoelectric converter). A 10 W electrical RTG scaled from this geometry requires:

```
P_thermal_required = P_electrical / η_thermoelectric  (η ≈ 6 % for state-of-art ASRG)
                   = 10 / 0.06 ≈ 167 W thermal
Pu-238 mass        = 167 / 0.567 W/g ≈ 295 g of Pu-238 fuel
Module mass        ≈ 295 g × 4 (GPHS module factor) ≈ 1.2 kg
Converter mass     ≈ 0.5 kg
Shielding + housing ≈ 1.5 kg
                    Σ ≈ 3.0–3.5 kg per 10 W electrical RTG
```

→ **10 W RTG mass estimate ≈ 3.0–3.5 kg** — large fraction of the §3 11.8 kg total system budget. This is technically achievable in principle (NASA's MMRTG demonstrates the engineering envelope), but **introduces a Pu-238 fuel-supply, regulatory-licensing, and personal-radiation-dose burden that is not addressed in the body text and would constitute a programmatic gating constraint for any near-term OBSIDIAN realisation.** A betavoltaic (C-14 / Ni-63 diamond battery) alternative at the §7 µW–mW power-density frontier would not deliver 10 W from the OBSIDIAN mass / volume envelope and is not a near-term substitute.
