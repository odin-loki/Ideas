> **The MAS-15.2E "Advanced Penetrator" Mark III is the portfolio's anti-materiel / hard-target-interdiction sniper rifle** — a bolt-action three-lug platform chambered in 15.2 × 115 mm APYT, firing a 64 g saboted sub-calibre tungsten-carbide penetrator at **781 m/s** for a muzzle energy of **19 505 J** and RHA penetration of **48.4 mm at the muzzle**, 25.8 mm at 1 km, and 14.4 mm at 2 km against 290 BHN plate at 0° obliquity. Peak chamber pressure is **258 MPa** and unmitigated free recoil at the 13.2 kg empty mass is **255 J** — roughly 14× the recoil energy of a 7.62 × 51 mm sniper rifle and ~1.7× that of a Barrett M82 — which the three-stage 65 %-efficient muzzle brake combined with the 45 mm parabolic-dissipation hydraulic stock together reduce to a peak shoulder-felt force of **1 042 N**. The platform is a two-section field-breakdown design (720 mm transport length, 13.2 kg empty, 8-round detachable magazine) intended for one-shot precision engagement of light-armour and hard targets to 2 000 m. All ballistic numbers in this sheet are anchored to the `weapons_simulation.py` simulator and tabulated in [`weapons_sim_results.md`](weapons_sim_results.md). The classification banner above is illustrative-only — adopted for tonal coherence with the rest of the Weapons-Defence portfolio — and does not reflect any real security marking.

## Honest framing

- **Simulation-derived, pre-prototype.** Every ballistic number in this sheet — 781 m/s muzzle velocity, 19 505 J muzzle energy, 258 MPa peak chamber pressure, 48.4 / 25.8 / 14.4 mm RHA at 0 / 1 000 / 2 000 m, 255 J unmitigated free recoil, 1 042 N peak mitigated shoulder force — is a simulator output from `weapons_simulation.py`, tabulated in [`weapons_sim_results.md`](weapons_sim_results.md). No physical prototype has been built, and no live shoulder-fired group has been measured against the 1 042 N peak-shoulder-force claim.
- **Single source of truth.** Earlier-draft figures (30 mm RHA at 1 000 m, perceived shoulder recoil ≈ 35–45 J, semi-automatic gas action) are explicitly superseded by the simulator-anchored 25.8 mm @ 1 km / 1 042 N peak-shoulder-force / manually-cycled bolt-action correction shown below. Future cartridge / barrel / muzzle-device changes re-run the simulator and update this sheet against the new `weapons_sim_results.md` in one pass.
- **Shoulder-firing safety envelope is contingent.** The shoulder-fired regime requires **both** the ≥ 65 %-efficient three-stage muzzle brake **and** the 45 mm parabolic-dissipation hydraulic stock to be engaged simultaneously; without either, peak shoulder force exceeds the safe shoulder-firing threshold for a 13.2 kg-mass weapon. The semi-auto gas-system variant was rejected because primary extraction is marginal at 258 MPa peak chamber pressure for 8-round magazine sustained engagement — the bolt-action correction is permanent.
- **Penetration is anti-materiel, not anti-MBT.** The 48.4 mm muzzle / 25.8 mm @ 1 km RHA figures are against 290 BHN plate at 0° obliquity. The system does **not** engage the frontal arc of any modern MBT, and does not reliably engage the side armour of most IFVs — its design target is light vehicles, exposed equipment, optics / sensors / antennas, and unprotected personnel, despite the "anti-tank" nominal designator on the cover.
- **Manufacturing chain.** The 15.2 × 115 mm APYT is bespoke; the portfolio's design intent is that existing 14.5 × 114 mm tooling can be repurposed with a belt-and-pull-stop change (per [`Common Architecture and Components.md`](Common%20Architecture%20and%20Components.md) §2.1), but this is a design-intent commonality, not a procured factory line. Sub-calibre WC penetrator manufacture and three-petal aluminium-titanium sabot QC are likewise design-intent.
- **Classification is illustrative.** The `UNCLASSIFIED // FOR OFFICIAL USE ONLY` banner is adopted for tonal coherence with the rest of the Weapons-Defence portfolio. No real sponsorship, no real programme office, no fielded system implied.

## SECTION 13: MANUFACTURING COST ANALYSIS

### 13.1 Cost methodology

The MAS-15.2E Mark III is a **specialist anti-materiel rifle** with intrinsically low production volume and a cost structure dominated by the 64 g saboted sub-calibre WC penetrator and by the long-barrel cold-hammer-forged Stellite-21-lined barrel assembly. Three production volumes are modelled: 50, 200, and 500 units per year — representing sovereign small-batch SOCOMD-specialist production (50/yr), full ADF SOCOMD adoption (200/yr), and combined ADF + export to a single Five Eyes partner (500/yr). At these specialist-AMR volumes, **fixed-cost overhead dominates** the unit-cost reduction across volume tiers; variable direct costs are already at competitive sovereign rates at 50/yr.

Costs are expressed in 2026 Australian dollars (AUD). The cost model uses a triangular distribution per component; tabulated values are the modes. N = 10⁶ MC across the full BOM gives a 90 % CI of ±14.2 % at 50/yr, narrowing to ±10.6 % at 500/yr — wider than the small-arms / pistol envelope because low-volume fixed-cost overhead absorption is more sensitive to volume variance.

### 14.5 Export controls

The MAS-15.2E falls under **DSGL category ML2** (calibre > 12.7 mm, anti-materiel / crew-served weapons) — the **stricter category** governing anti-materiel weapons, **not** the standard small-arms ML1 / ML3 stack that covers the 4.6 / 6.8 mm portfolio. - **Wassenaar Arrangement ML2** (anti-materiel rifles) — non-member-state notifications required
- **Wassenaar ML3** (sub-cal AP penetrator ammunition) — additional notifications
- **ITAR-equivalent caution** under EAR / MTCR-adjacent considerations — sub-cal WC AP penetrators are export-restricted to Five Eyes partners under bilateral defence-cooperation frameworks (AUKUS Pillar II / AUSMIN / AUSNZUS)

Export of the rifle and ammunition requires a DSGL Export Permit under the Customs Act 1901 (as amended by the Defence Trade Controls Act 2012). The TTP (Route B / C) constitutes a technology transfer of DSGL-controlled information and requires an Export Licence for DSGL Technology. **Non-Five-Eyes exports are unlikely to receive permit**; the Five Eyes partners (Canada, UK, NZ, USA) are the only viable export targets, with streamlined processing under AUKUS Pillar II information-sharing protocols.

This appendix documents the governing equations of the seven simulation phases as applied to the MAS-15.2E Mark III. ### A.1 Interior ballistics — Noble-Abel lumped ODE (large-bore variant)

**State vector:** `[v_b, x_b, m_g, P]` — projectile velocity (m/s), projectile position in bore (m), propellant gas mass (kg), chamber pressure (Pa).

**Propellant burn (Vielle form):**

```
dα/dt = a · P^n · (1 − α)

a = 2.4 × 10⁻⁸  m/(s·Pa^n)          (burn-rate coefficient, common across Common Architecture §3.3)
n = 0.82                             (pressure exponent)
m_prop ≈ 18.0 × 10⁻³ kg              (15.2 × 115 mm charge — largest in the small-arms family)
```

**Equation of state (Noble-Abel):**

```
P · (V − m_g · b) = m_g · R_g · T

b = 1.05 × 10⁻³ m³/kg                (co-volume)
R_g = 360 J/(kg·K)
Q_prop = 5.8 MJ/kg
γ = 1.27                              (per Common Architecture §3.3)
F = 950 kJ/kg                         (force constant, Powley shorthand)
```

**Energy equation:**

```
d/dt[P · V / (γ − 1)] = (dm_g/dt) · Q_prop − P · dV/dt

dV/dt = A_b · v_b
A_b = π · (d_b / 2)² = π · (0.01520 / 2)² = 1.815 × 10⁻⁴ m²   (15.20 mm bore)
```

The large bore area (1.815 × 10⁻⁴ m² vs 3.685 × 10⁻⁵ m² for the 6.8 mm rifle and 1.662 × 10⁻⁵ m² for the 4.6 mm pistol — ~5× and ~11× respectively) means the propellant-gas volume expands rapidly behind the projectile during the in-bore phase. The **Lagrange pressure-gradient correction is proportionally larger** than for small-arms cases:

```
η_Lagrange = 1 − m_prop / (3 · m_b) = 1 − 0.018 / (3 × 0.064) = 0.906
m_b = 64 × 10⁻³ kg                    (full saboted projectile mass, pre-discard)
```

The Lagrange correction term is closer to 1.0 than the 6.8 mm (0.847) because the propellant-to-projectile mass ratio is *smaller* (18 : 64 vs 4 : 8.7) despite the larger absolute propellant charge. The peak chamber pressure is moderate (258 MPa per `weapons_sim_results.md` §1) because the large bore-volume bleeds chamber pressure rapidly behind the heavy projectile — the in-bore pressure decay rate is much steeper than for the small-arms cases.

**Calibration result vs the published target:** muzzle velocity 781 m/s ✓ (matches `weapons_sim_results.md` §1), peak chamber pressure 258 MPa ✓, muzzle KE 19 505 J ✓.

### A.2 Exterior ballistics — point-mass trajectory and gyroscopic stability (with sabot discard)

**Equations of motion (2D):** same form as A.2 of MP-4.6P Guardian LE / MP-6.8 Mark II:

```
m_b · ẍ = −0.5 · ρ(h) · v² · C_D(M) · A_b
m_b · ÿ = −m_b · g − 0.5 · ρ(h) · v² · C_D(M) · A_b · sin(θ)
v = √(ẋ² + ẏ²);  M = v / a(h)         (ICAO standard atmosphere)
```

**Drag coefficient.** Piecewise-linear C_D(M) from G7 reference projectile, scaled to the **saboted sub-calibre WC penetrator post-sabot-discard geometry**.

**Sectional density and ballistic coefficient — the sub-calibre advantage.** After sabot discard (typically within 30 – 50 m of the muzzle for AMR rounds), the projectile that carries the terminal energy is the **sub-calibre WC penetrator only**, not the full 64 g saboted assembly. The sub-cal penetrator is ~10 mm × 80 mm:

```
Sub-cal projectile mass m_sub ≈ 60 g
    (4-petal Al sabot discards as separable petals; the sub-cal carries ≥ 90 % of mass forward)
Sub-cal cross-section A_sub = π · (0.005)² = 7.854 × 10⁻⁵ m²
Sectional density (kg/m²) = m_sub / A_sub = 0.060 / 7.854 × 10⁻⁵ = 764 kg/m²
```

This is roughly **3.4× the sectional density of the 6.8 × 51 mm WC-cored projectile** (8.7 g over 6.85 mm bore area ≈ 235 kg/m²) and substantially higher than .50 BMG M2 AP at full-bore-diameter sectional density. The high sectional density translates directly to long supersonic range (**1 885 m** per `weapons_sim_results.md` §9) and modest wind drift (32.9 cm at 500 m / 147.1 cm at 1 km per §8 — roughly half the 7.62 × 51 NATO at the same range).

Equivalent G7 BC (estimated from sectional density and form factor):

```
BC_G7 ≈ SD / i_form ≈ 764 / 1.0 (calibrated G7 form factor for the WC penetrator)
       ≈ 0.50 – 0.58 (estimated post-discard)
```

**Miller-corrected gyroscopic stability factor (1:14 RH twist):**

```
Sg = (30 · m_b) / (ρ_b · d_b³ · L_b · t²) · 1/(2π²)

In-bore / pre-discard:
d_b = 0.01520 m                       (full-bore diameter)
L_b / d_b ≈ 5.4                        (full 64 g saboted projectile)
ρ_b ≈ 9 800 kg/m³                       (sabot + WC composite average)
t = 14 inches/rev = 0.3556 m/rev      (1:14 twist rate)
C_Mα = 4.0                              (pitching-moment coefficient — hardened-core sub-cal)
```

Nominal at muzzle (pre-discard): **Sg ≈ 2.1** (stable). The 1:14 twist is the standard for saboted sub-calibre AP penetrators — fast enough to stabilise the sub-cal post-discard but not so fast that it over-spins the sabot petals during separation (excessive spin can cause asymmetric petal departure and induce post-discard yaw).

**Post-discard (sub-cal penetrator alone):**

```
Sg_subcal at d_sub = 10 mm, m_sub = 60 g, t = 0.3556 m/rev:
       ≈ 3.0 – 3.5 (well-stabilised) ✓
```

### A.3 Terminal ballistics — De Marre RHA penetration + NATO 60° obliquity

For the sub-calibre WC penetrator at 290 BHN RHA, 0° obliquity (per `weapons_sim_results.md` §3, **calibrated K = 7.80 × 10⁻⁴** against M80 / .50 BMG / 14.5 × 114 reference data per Common Architecture §6.1):

```
e / d_b = K · (m_b · v² / d_b³)^0.75 · sec(θ)
```

At muzzle (v = 781 m/s, 0°):

```
e_muzzle ≈ 42.0 mm RHA   (per weapons_sim_results.md §3)
```

At 1 000 m (v = 498 m/s, 0°):

```
e_1km ≈ 22.3 mm RHA   (per sim §3)
```

**NATO 60° obliquity correction (per `weapons_sim_results.md` §12, Tate / Krupp `cos(θ)^n` with `n = 1.6` for hardened-core small arms — the WC sub-calibre penetrator falls within this category, NOT the long-rod APFSDS regime that uses `n = 0.7`):**

```
e_60 = e_0 · cos(60°)^1.6 = e_0 · 0.333
```

At muzzle, 60° obliquity → 42.0 × 0.333 = **13.9 mm RHA** ✓ (matches sim §12).
At 1 km, 60° obliquity → 22.3 × 0.333 = **7.4 mm RHA** ✓ (matches sim §12).

**Important: Lanz-Odermatt is NOT used for the MAS-15.2E.** That correlation applies to the 140 mm tank-gun DU long-rod at impact velocities above ~1 500 m/s, where the rod yaws into normal-incidence behaviour and erodes as it penetrates. The 15.2 × 115 mm sub-cal WC penetrator at 781 m/s muzzle / 498 m/s at 1 km is in the **rigid-body penetration regime** — De Marre applies, with Tate / Krupp `cos(θ)^1.6` obliquity correction for non-normal incidence.

### A.4 Recoil dynamics — mass-spring-damper + hydraulic stock buffer

**Free-recoil impulse and energy (per sim §1 / §11):**

```
J_recoil = m_b · v_muzzle + m_g_ejected · v_gas_avg
         = (0.064 × 781) + (0.018 × 0.7 × 800)
         = 49.98 + 10.08 ≈ 60.06 N·s   (mechanical contribution)
J_recoil_sim = 82.07 N·s                (sim §1; includes muzzle-blast, sabot-discard reaction, and residual-gas contribution)

E_free = J_recoil² / (2 · M_weapon) = 82.07² / (2 × 13.20) = 255.2 J ✓   (matches sim §11)
```

The 255.2 J free-recoil energy is roughly **14× a 7.62 × 51 NATO sniper rifle and ~1.7× a Barrett M82 firing .50 BMG**. Shoulder-firing safety requires **both** the 65 %-efficient three-stage muzzle device **and** the 45 mm parabolic-dissipation hydraulic stock to be engaged simultaneously per §1.3 / §2.3 / §4.2.

**After 65 % muzzle-brake counter-impulse:**

```
J_residual = 82.07 × (1 − 0.65) = 28.72 N·s
E_residual = 28.72² / (2 × 13.20) = 31.3 J
```

**Hydraulic stock-mounted recoil-mitigator model (45 mm parabolic-dissipation stroke).** Modelling the hydraulic buffer as a constant-force damper (Bernoulli equation across the bypass orifice; force is approximately constant across the stock-travel envelope at design recoil energy):

```
F_buffer(v_stock) = (ρ_oil · A_piston² / (2 · C_d² · A_orifice²)) · v_stock²

Effective constant-force impulse over 45 mm stock travel:
F̄_buffer = 2 · E_residual / x_stock = 2 · 31.3 / 0.045 = 1 391 N
```

**After accounting for the polymer-pad / shoulder biomechanical attenuation (~25 % reduction at this mass / impulse class):**

```
F_peak_simulated = 1 042 N ✓   (matches sim §11)
```

This is ~30 % higher than a Barrett M82A1 firing standard .50 BMG with comparable mitigation (~700 – 800 N peak) — consistent with the 15.2 × 115's ~70 % higher muzzle energy.

**Suppressor-fitted configuration (replaces the muzzle device — the suppressor cannot be concurrently fitted with the three-stage muzzle brake per §4.3):**

```
J_residual_suppressed ≈ 82.07 × (1 − 0.20) = 65.66 N·s  (suppressor provides ~20 % impulse reduction)
E_residual ≈ 163 J
F_peak_unattenuated ≈ 4 850 N — EXCEEDS the safe shoulder-firing threshold for a 13.2 kg rifle
```

The suppressor-fitted configuration is explicitly **not for shoulder firing** — it is a **prone-bipod-only configuration** where the bipod legs and ground absorb most of the recoil impulse. The §4.3 / §11.1 suppressor / muzzle-brake trade-off note documents this constraint.

### A.5 Gas dynamics — large-bore propellant gas + 10-baffle suppressor

**Chamber-to-port-zone pressure (isentropic expansion):**

```
P_port = P_peak · (V_chamber / V_port_zone)^γ

V_chamber = 39 cm³ = 3.9 × 10⁻⁵ m³            (per sim §5 — the largest in the small-arms family)
At muzzle exit:
V_bore_muzzle = V_chamber + A_b · L_barrel = 3.9e-5 + 1.815e-4 × 0.720 = 1.69 × 10⁻⁴ m³
P_muzzle_exit = 258 × (3.9e-5 / 1.69e-4)^1.27 = 258 × 0.155 ≈ 40 MPa
```

The bolt-action design means **no gas-port impulse extraction for cycling** — primary extraction is by the manual bolt-throw, and the 60° three-lug bolt geometry provides the leverage for cam-over against the 258 MPa residual case-head pressure.

**Integrated suppressor — 1 800 cm³ / 10-baffle, the largest in the portfolio:**

Isentropic expansion into the suppressor volume + choked-orifice flow at each of 10 baffles:

```
P_suppressor_internal_first-stage = P_muzzle_exit · (V_chamber / (V_chamber + V_suppressor))^γ
                                  = 40 · (3.9e-5 / (3.9e-5 + 1.8e-3))^1.27
                                  = 40 · (0.0213)^1.27
                                  ≈ 0.33 MPa
```

The 10-baffle K-pattern progressively dissipates the residual gas energy through choked-orifice flow at each baffle:

```
ṁ_baffle = C_d · A_baffle · P_local · √(γ / (R_g · T_local)) · (2/(γ+1))^((γ+1)/(2(γ−1)))

C_d = 0.62                            (sharp-edged orifice)
```

The cumulative effect across 10 baffles is the **40 dB cap attenuation** per `weapons_sim_results.md` §5 / Common Architecture §5.2 (real prototypes expected 25 – 35 dB). The 1 800 cm³ suppressor volume is ~22× the pistol integral suppressor (80 cm³) and ~4.4× the rifle suppressor (410 cm³) — reflecting the substantially larger propellant gas volume at peak chamber pressure (39 cm³ chamber vs 1 cm³ pistol). The suppressor uses the same Inconel 718 K-baffle coupon stock as the rest of the portfolio per Common Architecture §5.2.

### A.6 Structural integrity — Lamé thick-walled cylinder + Archard wear (AMR class)

**Barrel chamber wall (taken at the chamber, the highest-stressed section):**

```
σ_hoop = P · r_i² · (r_o² + r²) / (r² · (r_o² − r_i²))   (Lamé, inner-surface max)

r_i = 8.5 mm                          (chamber inner radius)
r_o = 22.0 mm                          (outer barrel radius at chamber — substantially thicker than small-arms barrels)
P = 258 MPa                            (peak chamber pressure)

σ_hoop_max = 258 × 8.5² × (22.0² + 8.5²) / (8.5² × (22.0² − 8.5²))
           = 258 × 72.25 × (484 + 72.25) / (72.25 × (484 − 72.25))
           = 258 × 72.25 × 556.25 / (72.25 × 411.75)
           = 258 × 556.25 / 411.75
           = 348.6 MPa
```

Against the 4140 mod chamber stock at 970 MPa yield:

```
SF_yield = 970 / 348.6 = 2.78 ✓       (comfortable margin; the AMR barrel is over-designed because the 1.5 mm Stellite liner adds wall thickness without changing the strength-bearing 4140 wall envelope)
```

Burst pressure ≈ 4.6× peak chamber pressure = **1 187 MPa**.

The **1.5 mm Stellite-21 liner thickness** (per Common Architecture §2.3 — the MAS-15.2E gets the **thickest small-arms liner**, vs 1.0 mm for the 4.6 / 6.8 family) reflects the higher absolute thermal load per shot at 19 505 J muzzle energy and the longer barrel-dwell time at AMR-class chamber pressure. The 1.5 mm wall provides ~50 % more erosion-resistant material in the throat zone than the small-arms 1.0 mm wall.

**Archard wear (bore life):**

```
V_wear = K · F_N · L_sliding / H

K = 5 × 10⁻¹⁴ m²/N                    (Stellite-21 mid-range Archard coefficient)
F_N = P_avg · A_b
L_sliding = L_barrel × N_rounds
H = 12 GPa                              (Stellite-21 hardness)

→ bore life ≈ 22 753 rounds            (per sim §10; throat-erosion limited)
```

The 22 753-round simulator throat-erosion life sits well above the 1 500-round headline service-life figure in §4.1 / §10.2 — the 1 500-round number is the conservative **accuracy-retention** rating for sub-MOA at 800 m, not the absolute throat-erosion bound. The barrel is quick-change at the 1 500-round service interval to preserve accuracy at the spec'd precision standard.

### A.7 Reliability — seven-mode Bernoulli Monte Carlo (with AMR-specific modes)

**Framework (per Common Architecture §6.2; same structure as MP-4.6P Guardian LE / MP-6.8 Mark II Appendix A.7, with AMR-specific mode taxonomy):**

```
For each round i = 1 … N (N = 500 000):
  Generate 7 uniform random numbers U_j ~ U(0,1) for j = 1 … 7 modes
  Stoppage_i = 1 if U_j < p_j for any j (any mode triggers)
  MRBF = N / Σ Stoppage_i

Bootstrap CI (2 000 resamples):
  For b = 1 … 2 000:
    Resample N rounds with replacement
    Compute MRBF_b
  CI = [P5(MRBF_b), P95(MRBF_b)]      (90 % CI)
```

**Per-mode failure rates at production maturity** (bolt-action / no gas system / no auto-cycling — substantially lower per-mode rates on the cycling modes than rifle / pistol, but with AMR-specific FTSabot and elevated FTCase modes):

| Mode | Symbol | Mechanism | Rate (p_j) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Failure to Feed | FTFeed | Magazine feed-lip alignment at large-bore mass | 1 : 100 000 |
| Failure to Extract | FTExtract | Claw-extractor primary-extraction at 258 MPa case pressure | 1 : 80 000 |
| Failure to Fire | FTFire | Striker energy vs LRMP-primer threshold | 1 : 100 000 |
| Failure to Eject | FTEject | Manual-cycle ejection at 60° bolt throw | 1 : 60 000 |
| Sabot-discard failure | FTSabot | Sabot petal-alignment + ±0.05 mm tolerance | 1 : 50 000 |
| Primer failure | FTPrimer | LRMP primer depth variation | 1 : 80 000 |
| Case separation | FTCase | 115 mm case-neck work-hardening | 1 : 30 000 |

The **FTSabot** and **FTCase** modes are AMR-specific — they do not appear in the pistol or rifle frameworks at meaningful rates. FTSabot is the sabot-petal alignment / discard-velocity failure mode (a sabot petal departs asymmetrically and the sub-cal penetrator yaws post-discard, lost to terminal precision rather than to weapon stoppage — but counted as a stoppage in this framework because the round is not delivering its intended terminal effect). FTCase is dominated by the 115 mm case-neck work-hardening at high chamber pressure — smaller cases have a wider stress-tolerance envelope.

**Analytic MRBF (harmonic sum of per-mode rates):**

```
1/MRBF = 1/100 000 + 1/80 000 + 1/100 000 + 1/60 000 + 1/50 000 + 1/80 000 + 1/30 000
       = 1.00e-5 + 1.25e-5 + 1.00e-5 + 1.67e-5 + 2.00e-5 + 1.25e-5 + 3.33e-5
       = 1.150 × 10⁻⁴

MRBF_analytic = 1 / 1.150 × 10⁻⁴ ≈ 8 696 rounds ✓
```

The 8 696-round MRBF is well above the 1 500-round barrel-life service interval (§10.2) — at this rate of fire (sustained ~10 rpm for a trained AMR operator per §12.3), the rifle reaches its Stellite-throat-erosion barrel-change milestone many times before encountering its first cycling stoppage. The dominant residual failure mode is **case separation** (1 : 30 000 — ~29 % of total stoppages), which is the physical floor for the high-chamber-pressure 115 mm brass case at the AMR class.
