## SECTION 12: TIER-2 SIMULATOR OUTPUTS

This section consolidates the Tier-2 outputs introduced in `weapons_sim_results.md` §6–§13 that bear on the MP-6.8 Mark II. All numbers are taken directly from the simulator output.

### 12.1 Acoustic signature (`weapons_sim_results.md` §6)

Peak SPL (dB) is fitted by the Westin (1975) muzzle-blast correlation, calibrated against published 5.56 carbine (≈ 165/158 dB), 7.62 rifle (≈ 166/159), and .50 BMG (≈ 178/170) anchors. Hearing-protection stack: foam plug −22 dB, double plug + muff −28 dB, TACS personal active cancellation −25 dB on top of double.

| Configuration | Peak SPL (dB) |
|---|---|
| Muzzle, unsuppressed | 166.2 |
| Shooter's ear, unsuppressed | 159.2 |
| Muzzle, integrated 410 cm³ K-baffle suppressor | 126.2 |
| Shooter's ear, suppressed | 119.2 |
| Ear, suppressed + foam plug | 97.2 |
| Ear, suppressed + double plug + muff | 91.2 |
| Ear, suppressed + double + TACS personal | 66.2 |

The 166.2 dB unsuppressed muzzle SPL places the MP-6.8 Mark II in the same acoustic envelope as a service 7.62×51 mm rifle — 26 dB above the OSHA peak-impulse ceiling (140 dB). The unsuppressed shooter-ear figure (159.2 dB) is consistent with the simulator's calibration of a 7.62 rifle to 159 dB. With the suppressor + double plug + muff + TACS active-cancellation stack, ear-level peak drops to 66.2 dB — the shooter is below normal-conversation level, with the active layer providing the dominant 25 dB of peak-pressure reduction at the wearer's eardrum.

|---|---|---|

| 500 m | -268.9 | 65.7 |

Hatcher max-effective range (KE > 80 J personnel-incapacitation threshold): **> 3 500 m** (simulator cap). Supersonic range: **1 030 m** (muzzle 2 398 fps). The MP-6.8 retains supersonic flight past the 1 000 m area-engagement envelope of SECTION 1.3, and the KE > 80 J personnel-defeat range is well beyond any plausible engagement distance — the cartridge is energy-bounded only by the simulator cap, not by the cartridge itself. The 6.8×51 mm class is roughly 70–80 % less wind-deflected than the equivalent 5.56×45 mm at 800–1 000 m (compare 186.9 cm vs the 5.56's 257.8 cm at 800 m in §8).

### 12.5 Penetration vs body armour (`weapons_sim_results.md` §13)

The 6.8×51 mm 8.7 g WC-cored projectile at 731 m/s is **not directly characterised** in §13. The two §13 threats that bracket it most closely are:
- 5.56×45 NATO M855 (4.0 g, 940 m/s) — lighter, faster, steel-tipped lead core
- 7.62×51 NATO M80 ball (9.5 g, 820 m/s) — heavier, faster, lead-cored ball

The MP-6.8's 8.7 g WC-cored projectile sits between the two by mass and below both by velocity, but with a harder-than-lead penetrator. Cited §13 outcomes for the bracketing comparators:

| Armour class | Areal density | M80 ball outcome | M855 outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| Soft IIIA (5.5 kg/m²) | | PERFORATED (V50 383, threat 820) | PERFORATED (V50 537, threat 940) |
| NIJ III (11.2 kg/m²) | | PERFORATED (V50 719, threat 820) | STOPPED (V50 1 008, threat 940; BFD 44.0 mm) |
| NIJ IV (25 kg/m²) | | STOPPED (V50 1 189, threat 820; BFD 44.0 mm) | STOPPED (V50 1 667, BFD 21.0 mm) |
| APES military (35 kg/m²) | | STOPPED (V50 1 407, BFD 28.4 mm) | STOPPED (V50 1 972, BFD 11.6 mm) |
| APES-L police (22 kg/m²) | | STOPPED (V50 1 116, BFD 44.0 mm) | STOPPED (V50 1 564, BFD 26.2 mm) |

Reading the bracket: the MP-6.8 Mark II's WC-cored 8.7 g projectile at 731 m/s **defeats soft IIIA cleanly**, **probably defeats NIJ III** (the WC core is harder than M80's lead core; the threat velocity is below M80's 820 m/s but the WC core compensates), and is **stopped by NIJ IV, APES military, and APES-L police** at all engagement ranges, consistent with the §3 RHA-equivalent penetration of 11.1 mm at the muzzle and 8.1 mm at 300 m. The cartridge is in the modern-body-armour-defeat regime against soft and Level III threats but is not a Level IV-defeating round.

## SECTION 13: MANUFACTURING COST ANALYSIS

### 13.1 Cost methodology

Manufacturing costs are estimated using a first-principles Bill-of-Materials (BOM) model at three production volumes: 5 000, 20 000, and 50 000 units per year. These tiers represent: 5 000/yr — sovereign small-batch facility serving the ADF rifleman programme only; 20 000/yr — full ADF rifle programme plus a single Five Eyes export partner; 50 000/yr — regional pan-Five-Eyes adoption. Costs are expressed in 2026 Australian dollars (AUD). The cost model uses a triangular distribution (low / mode / high) per component; tabulated values are the modes. A N = 10⁶ Monte Carlo run across the full BOM gives a 90 % confidence interval of ± 9.4 % on total unit cost at 5 000/yr, narrowing to ± 7.1 % at 50 000/yr.

The cost model distinguishes **variable direct costs** (materials, variable process labour, per-unit QC) from **fixed-cost overhead** (tooling amortisation, engineering / quality management labour, facility costs). At rifle-class volumes (5 000+/yr), fixed-cost overhead is well-absorbed; the unit-cost reduction across volume tiers is driven by both overhead absorption and assembly-labour rate reductions as the line matures.

## SECTION 15: PROCUREMENT FRAMEWORK

### 15.1 ADF primary procurement pathway

The MP-6.8 Mark II is positioned as the ADF transition rifle from 5.56 × 45 NATO to the 6.8 × 51 mm Common Cartridge regime, paralleling the US Army Next Generation Squad Weapon (NGSW) transition from M4 to XM7. Procurement runs under the Defence Capability Acquisition and Sustainment Group (CASG) Land Forces Materiel programme.

**Phase 1 — Technical evaluation (months 1 – 9):**
- Ballistic testing of 5 000-round 6.8 × 51 mm sample against NIJ III, NIJ IV, and STANAG 4569 Level III armour panels at the Defence Science and Technology Group (DSTG) ballistic range. Acceptance criterion: matches `weapons_sim_results.md` §3 RHA values within ±10 %; defeats NIJ III hard plate (10 mm AR-steel reference) cleanly at 0 / 500 m; performance against NIJ IV does **not** exceed the `weapons_sim_results.md` §13 V50 bracketing (the cartridge is not claimed against NIJ IV).
- 20-rifle endurance test (5 000 rounds per rifle, 100 000 rounds total) for MRBF characterisation. Acceptance: pre-production MRBF ≥ 8 000 rounds (production-standard target ≥ 25 000 rounds per §9.1).
- Independent ergonomics and human-factors assessment (1-week firing trial, 60 riflemen across infantry / SOTG / RFSU communities). Acceptance: ≥ 85 % qualify on the rifle within 16 hours from M4 baseline; ≥ 90 % rate trigger / sights / cycling subjectively superior to the M4.

**Phase 2 — Pilot programme (months 10 – 24):**
- Issue to 500 riflemen across one full infantry battalion. Carry all training rotations; stoppage rate and user-feedback reporting every 60 days.
- Cold-weather trial (Bullecourt range, Tasmania winter, ≤ 0 °C).
- Tropical / humid-environment trial (Townsville, monsoon, JNT).
- Independent armourer assessment of field-strip, quick-change barrel, and shared trigger-pack drop-in across the rifle and PDW.

**Phase 3 — Production procurement decision (months 25 – 36):**
- Independent audit of Phase 2 stoppage and user-feedback data.
- DSGL export-permit lodged for TTP (if Route B sovereign manufacture).
- Procurement contract award.
- First production rifles delivered within 12 months of contract award at the 5 000/yr line rate, ramping to 20 000/yr at year +3.

### 15.2 AFP secondary procurement pathway

The Australian Federal Police's specialist tactical (Tactical Response Team / TRT) and protective-services (Specialist Response Group / SRG) communities have a hard-armour-defeat mandate that the current M4-based suite does not reliably meet. ## APPENDIX A — Simulation Model Reference Equations

This appendix documents the governing equations of the seven simulation phases as applied to the MP-6.8 Mark II. The full Python implementation is in [`weapons_simulation.py`](../weapons_simulation.py). All numeric outputs cited below are from [`weapons_sim_results.md`](../weapons_sim_results.md). Calibration references and model assumptions are documented in [`Common Architecture and Components.md §6`](../Common%20Architecture%20and%20Components.md).

### A.1 Interior ballistics — Noble-Abel lumped ODE

**State vector:** `[v_b, x_b, m_g, P]` — bullet velocity (m/s), bullet position in bore (m), propellant gas mass (kg), chamber pressure (Pa).

**Propellant burn (Vielle form):**

```
dα/dt = a · P^n · (1 − α)

α(t) = m_g(t) / m_prop            (burn fraction)
a = 2.4 × 10⁻⁸  m/(s·Pa^n)         (burn-rate coefficient, common across Common Architecture §3.3)
n = 0.82                           (pressure exponent)
m_prop ≈ 4.0 × 10⁻³ kg             (6.8 × 51 mm propellant charge)
```

**Equation of state (Noble-Abel):**

```
P · (V − m_g · b) = m_g · R_g · T

b = 1.05 × 10⁻³ m³/kg              (co-volume)
R_g = 360 J/(kg·K)                  (propellant gas constant)
Q_prop = 5.8 MJ/kg                  (specific energy)
γ = 1.27                            (isentropic exponent — per Common Architecture §3.3)
F = 950 kJ/kg                       (force constant, Powley shorthand)
```

**Energy equation (first law, isentropic approximation):**

```
d/dt[P · V / (γ − 1)] = (dm_g/dt) · Q_prop − P · dV/dt

dV/dt = A_b · v_b
A_b = π · (d_b / 2)² = π · (0.00685 / 2)² = 3.685 × 10⁻⁵ m²   (6.85 mm bore)
```

**Bullet equation of motion:**

```
m_b · dv_b/dt = A_b · P · η_Lagrange − F_friction

η_Lagrange = 1 − m_prop / (3 · m_b) = 1 − 0.004 / (3 × 0.0087) = 0.847
F_friction ≈ 0.03 · A_b · P
m_b = 8.7 × 10⁻³ kg                 (8.7 g WC-cored projectile)
```

The Lagrange correction is more pronounced for the 6.8 mm rifle than for the 4.6 mm pistol (η_Lagrange = 0.847 vs ≈ 0.927) because the propellant-to-projectile mass ratio is larger and the projectile rides the Lagrange pressure gradient over a longer 406 mm bore.

**Calibration result vs the published target:** muzzle velocity 731 m/s ✓ (matches `weapons_sim_results.md` §1), peak chamber pressure 307 MPa ✓, muzzle KE 2 324 J ✓.

### A.2 Exterior ballistics — point-mass trajectory and gyroscopic stability

**Equations of motion (2D):**

```
m_b · ẍ = −0.5 · ρ(h) · v² · C_D(M) · A_b
m_b · ÿ = −m_b · g − 0.5 · ρ(h) · v² · C_D(M) · A_b · sin(θ)

v = √(ẋ² + ẏ²)          (total velocity)
M = v / a(h)             (Mach number; a(h) from ICAO standard atmosphere)
```

**Drag coefficient.** Piecewise-linear C_D(M) from G7 reference projectile table, scaled to the 8.7 g WC-cored spitzer geometry. G7 BC = 0.260 per §2.1 of this spec (G1 ≈ 0.515). At muzzle Mach 2.13 (sea-level standard): C_D ≈ 0.265.

**Miller-corrected gyroscopic stability factor (1:8 RH twist):**

```
Sg = (30 · m_b) / (ρ_b · d_b³ · L_b · t²) · 1/(2π²)

d_b = 0.00685 m                     (bore diameter)
L_b / d_b ≈ 4.09                     (28 mm projectile length / 6.85 mm bore)
ρ_b = 11 500 kg/m³                   (WC + steel-rear + CuNi3Si jacket composite average)
t = 8 inches/rev = 0.2032 m/rev      (1:8 twist rate)
C_Mα = 4.0                           (pitching-moment coefficient — hardened-core spitzer)
```

Nominal at muzzle: **Sg ≈ 1.85** (stable; threshold Sg > 1.4). The bullet remains stable through the supersonic envelope and through the transonic transition at ~ 1 000 m supersonic boundary per `weapons_sim_results.md` §9.

### A.3 Terminal ballistics — De Marre RHA penetration

For the 8.7 g WC-cored projectile against 290 BHN RHA at 0° obliquity (per `weapons_sim_results.md` §3, **calibrated K = 7.80 × 10⁻⁴** against M80 7.62 NATO @ ~10 mm RHA / .50 BMG M2 AP @ ~20 mm / 14.5 × 114 B-32 @ ~30 mm reference data per Common Architecture §6.1):

```
e / d_b = K · (m_b · v² / d_b³)^0.75 · sec(θ)

e        = penetration depth (m)
d_b      = 0.00685 m
m_b      = 8.7 × 10⁻³ kg
v        = impact velocity (m/s)
K        = 7.80 × 10⁻⁴               (small-arms WC-cored calibration)
θ        = obliquity angle from normal
```

At muzzle (v = 731 m/s, 0° obliquity):

```
e_0 ≈ 11.1 mm ✓                       (matches weapons_sim_results.md §3)
```

At 500 m (v ≈ 499 m/s, 0°):

```
e_500 ≈ 6.5 mm ✓                      (matches §3)
```

At 1 000 m (v ≈ 344 m/s, 0°):

```
e_1km ≈ 3.9 mm ✓                      (matches §3)
```

**NATO 60° obliquity correction (per `weapons_sim_results.md` §12 — Tate / Krupp `cos(θ)^n`, n = 1.6 for hardened-core small arms):**

```
e_60 = e_0 · cos(60°)^1.6 = e_0 · 0.333
```

At muzzle, 60° obliquity → 11.1 × 0.333 = **3.7 mm RHA** ✓ (matches sim §12).

**Important note on the long-rod regime.** Lanz-Odermatt is **NOT** used for the MP-6.8 — that correlation applies to the 140 mm tank-gun DU long-rod APFSDS at impact velocities above ~1 500 m/s, where the rod yaws into normal-incidence behaviour and erodes as it penetrates (and where n = 0.7 applies in the obliquity correction). The 6.8 × 51 mm WC-cored projectile at 731 m/s muzzle is firmly in the rigid-body small-arms regime — De Marre with n = 1.6 obliquity is the correct correlation.

### A.4 Recoil dynamics — mass-spring-damper

**Bolt-carrier-group equation of motion (during gas-piston impulse delivery and recoil stroke):**

```
m_BCG · ẍ_BCG = J_piston(t) − k_recoil · x_BCG − c · ẋ_BCG − F_friction

m_BCG = 0.325 kg                     (bolt carrier group mass, per §4.2)
k_recoil ≈ 6 N/mm                     (Inconel X-750 recoil spring, per §4.3)
c ≈ 0.45 N·s/m                        (estimated viscoelastic damping)
J_piston(t) = ∫ A_piston · P_chamber-port(t) dt   (over piston travel ~22 mm)
```

The short-stroke-piston design (per §4.1 and Common Architecture §2.3) integrates the bolt-carrier impulse over **piston travel (~22 mm)**, not over port-transit time. This differs from the pistol / delayed-blowback action documented in `MP-4.6P Guardian LE.md` Appendix A.5, where impulse is integrated over the 50 µs port-transit time. The piston decouples from the BCG at travel-end → BCG carries pure momentum into the recoil-spring compression.

**Free-recoil impulse and energy (per sim §1 / §11):**

```
J_recoil = m_b · v_muzzle + m_g_ejected · v_gas_avg
         = (0.0087 × 731) + (0.004 × 0.7 × 600)
         = 6.36 + 1.68 ≈ 8.04 N·s   (mechanical contribution)
J_recoil_sim = 9.62 N·s              (sim §1; includes muzzle-blast contribution)

E_free = J_recoil² / (2 · M_weapon) = 9.62² / (2 × 4.10) = 11.29 J ≈ 11.3 J ✓
```

**After 35 % brake counter-impulse (per sim §11; muzzle device is a 3-stage compensator providing 35 % impulse redirection):**

```
J_residual = 9.62 × (1 − 0.35) = 6.25 N·s
E_residual = 6.25² / (2 × 4.10) = 4.77 J

Parabolic energy dissipation over 20 mm stock travel:
F_peak_unattenuated = 2 · E_residual / x_stock = 2 · 4.77 / 0.020 = 477 N

After ~25 % polymer-pad / shoulder-biomechanical attenuation:
F_peak_simulated = 358 N ✓           (matches sim §11)
```

The six-position adjustable stock is treated as a rigid stock for recoil-force calculations — felt recoil is dominated by the 35 % brake counter-impulse and parabolic energy dissipation across the 20 mm stock-travel envelope, not by the position-adjustment range.

### A.5 Gas dynamics — short-stroke gas piston (distinct from direct impingement)

**Port-zone pressure (isentropic expansion from peak chamber pressure):**

```
P_port = P_peak · (V_chamber / V_port_zone)^γ

V_chamber = 3.5 cm³ = 3.5 × 10⁻⁶ m³           (per sim §5)
V_port_zone ≈ V_chamber + A_b · x_port
           ≈ 3.5 × 10⁻⁶ + 3.685 × 10⁻⁵ · 0.30
           ≈ 1.46 × 10⁻⁵ m³                     (port located ~300 mm down-bore)

P_port = 307 × (3.5e-6 / 1.46e-5)^1.27 = 307 × 0.155 ≈ 47.6 MPa
```

**Piston impulse (integrated over piston travel, not over port-transit time):**

```
J_piston = ∫₀^{t_decouple} A_piston · P_piston(t) · dt

A_piston ≈ 7.1 × 10⁻⁵ m²              (~9.5 mm piston rod diameter)
Δt_piston ≈ 1.2 ms                     (estimated; includes pressure-decay along the piston stroke)

J_piston ≈ A_piston · P̄_piston · Δt_piston ≈ 7.1e-5 × 23e6 × 1.2e-3 ≈ 1.96 N·s
J_BCG = J_piston · η_transfer ≈ 1.96 × 0.85 ≈ 1.67 N·s
```

The short-stroke piston operates differently from the pistol delayed-blowback (or from a direct-impingement gas system): the piston travel terminates at a fixed mechanical stop, transferring all impulse to the BCG over a short fixed travel; the BCG then carries the impulse rearward to compress the recoil spring. **The impulse integral runs over piston travel (~22 mm) rather than over port-transit time (~50 µs in the pistol case)** — a fundamentally different integration window from the pistol mass-spring derivation in `MP-4.6P Guardian LE.md` §6.1 / Appendix A.5.

**Three-position regulator (per §4.1)** adjusts the piston-port effective area to compensate for suppressed firing (Position 2, suppressor backpressure increases gas-port flow) or adverse conditions (Position 3, increased gas tap for marginal-cycling conditions), maintaining BCG cyclic timing across firing modes.

### A.6 Structural integrity — Lamé thick-walled cylinder + Archard wear

**Barrel chamber wall (the highest-stressed section, taken at the chamber):**

```
σ_hoop = P · r_i² · (r_o² + r²) / (r² · (r_o² − r_i²))   (Lamé, inner-surface max)

r_i = 5.85 mm                         (chamber inner radius — fired-round freebore)
r_o = 14.5 mm                          (outer barrel radius at chamber)
P   = 307 MPa                          (peak chamber pressure)

σ_hoop_max = 307 × 5.85² × (14.5² + 5.85²) / (5.85² × (14.5² − 5.85²))
           = 307 × 34.22 × (210.25 + 34.22) / (34.22 × (210.25 − 34.22))
           = 307 × 34.22 × 244.47 / (34.22 × 176.03)
           = 307 × 244.47 / 176.03
           = 426.4 MPa
```

Against the 4140 mod vacuum-arc-remelted chamber stock at 970 MPa yield:

```
SF_yield = 970 / 426.4 = 2.27 ✓       (comfortable margin; > 2 target)
```

Burst pressure ≈ 4.0× peak chamber pressure = **1 228 MPa**.

The bore wall away from the chamber sees substantially lower pressure (the propellant gas expands behind the bullet; pressure decays as the bullet moves down-bore) — chamber dimensions are sizing-critical, not bore-mid dimensions.

**Archard wear model (bore life):**

```
V_wear = K · F_N · L_sliding / H

K = 5 × 10⁻¹⁴ m²/N                    (Stellite-21 + hard-chrome composite, mid-range Archard)
F_N = P_avg · A_b                      (average normal force, peak chamber × bore area)
L_sliding = L_barrel × N_rounds        (cumulative sliding distance over weapon life)
H = 12 GPa                              (Stellite-21 hardness; the throat-erosion-limiting surface)

→ bore life ≈ 80 398 rounds           (per sim §10; throat-erosion limited)
```

The 80 398-round simulator throat-erosion life sits well above the 25 000-round headline service-life figure in §3.1 / §8.2 / §9.1 — the 25 000-round figure is the conservative **accuracy-retention** rating for sub-1-MOA at 100 m, not the absolute throat-erosion bound. The quick-change three-lug barrel system (§3.2) extends operationally useful service life beyond the accuracy-retention milestone.

### A.7 Reliability — seven-mode Bernoulli Monte Carlo

**Framework (per Common Architecture §6.2; identical structure to MP-4.6P Guardian LE Appendix A.7):**

```
For each round i = 1 … N (N = 500 000):
  Generate 7 uniform random numbers U_j ~ U(0,1) for j = 1 … 7 modes
  Stoppage_i = 1 if U_j < p_j for any j (any mode triggers)
  MRBF = N / Σ Stoppage_i

Bootstrap CI (2 000 resamples):
  For b = 1 … 2 000:
    Resample N rounds with replacement
    Compute MRBF_b
  CI = [P5(MRBF_b), P95(MRBF_b)]      (90 % CI)
```

**Per-mode failure rates at production maturity (Tier-2 surface engineering — chrome carrier, NP3 internals, Stellite throat insert, precision 7075-T6 magazine, 100 % ammunition QC):**

| Mode | Symbol | Mechanism | Rate (p_j) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Failure to Feed | FTFeed | Feed-ramp friction + BCG stroke margin | 1 : 250 000 |
| Failure to Extract | FTExtract | Extractor hook depth + spring preload | 1 : 200 000 |
| Failure to Fire | FTFire | Striker energy vs primer threshold | 1 : 150 000 |
| Failure to Eject | FTEject | Ejector geometry + port-bleed timing | 1 : 100 000 |
| Gas-system fouling | FTGas | Carbon adhesion at piston / port | 1 : 300 000 |
| Primer failure | FTPrimer | Primer depth variation + sensitivity | 1 : 200 000 |
| Case separation | FTCase | Brass work-hardening at 51 mm case neck | 1 : 400 000 |

**Analytic MRBF (harmonic sum of per-mode rates):**

```
1/MRBF = 1/250 000 + 1/200 000 + 1/150 000 + 1/100 000 + 1/300 000 + 1/200 000 + 1/400 000
       = 4.00e-6 + 5.00e-6 + 6.67e-6 + 10.00e-6 + 3.33e-6 + 5.00e-6 + 2.50e-6
       = 36.50e-6

MRBF_analytic = 1 / 36.50e-6 ≈ 27 397 rounds  (per-mode harmonic sum in this appendix)

Portfolio lifecycle MC (`weapons_sim_results.md` §23): MRBF analytic **15 656** / simulated **15 000**; FTF rate **1:55 000**; felt recoil **1.631 ft·lb** — authoritative spec targets in §9.1.
```

The dominant residual failure mode at production maturity is FTEject (1 : 100 000 — ~27 % of total stoppages). This is the physical floor for a gas-piston rotating-bolt design at 307 MPa peak chamber pressure with the specified ejection geometry; further reduction requires re-designing the ejector face / port-bleed timing rather than additional surface engineering.
