# COGNIMAX-PRO

*Rational Design of a Multi-Modal Cognitive Enhancement Formulation for Individuals Receiving Antipsychotic Therapy: Molecular Pharmacology, Mechanistic Synergies, and Translational Considerations*

*A Theoretical Pharmaceutical Design Document*

**NOTE: CogniMax-Pro is a hypothetical formulation described for theoretical and educational purposes only. Several constituent compounds \(MetaCholine-1, NeuroplastiCholine, CholinoBuproion\) are fictional novel molecules that have not been synthesised or tested. This document does not constitute medical advice.**

## Abstract
Individuals living with schizophrenia and related psychotic disorders treated with antipsychotic medications—particularly the widely prescribed second-generation agents paliperidone and olanzapine—face a two-layered cognitive burden: the intrinsic, disease-related neuropsychological deficits that precede medication, and the potential for iatrogenic cognitive impairment driven by anticholinergic, dopaminergic, and histaminergic receptor interactions of the antipsychotics themselves. No pharmacological strategy currently holds regulatory approval for the treatment of antipsychotic-era cognitive impairment, and cognitive dysfunction remains the strongest predictor of functional disability in this population. We present a theoretical pharmaceutical design document for CogniMax-Pro, a speculative multi-modal cognitive enhancement formulation comprising both established pharmacological agents \(xanomeline extended-release analogue, berberine phytosome, alpha-GPC, phosphatidylserine, NMN, PQQ, CoQ10\) and three novel fictional compounds \(MetaCholine-1, NeuroplastiCholine, CholinoBuproion\) that have not yet been synthesised or tested. The formulation targets five mechanistic pillars: \(1\) M1/M4 muscarinic receptor agonism for cognitive-synaptic enhancement; \(2\) AMPK/metabolic pathway activation; \(3\) neurotrophin signalling augmentation via TrkB activation; \(4\) catecholaminergic facilitation of prefrontal circuits; and \(5\) mitochondrial bioenergetic support. Each component's molecular pharmacology is described, mechanistic synergies are analysed, and the formulation is contextualised within the published literature on cognitive interventions in antipsychotic-treated populations. The formulation's dual-layer extended-release design is described, and translational feasibility is critically assessed.

**Keywords:** *antipsychotic; cognitive enhancement; schizophrenia; muscarinic agonist; xanomeline; berberine; AMPK; BDNF; alpha-GPC; polypharmacology; nootropic*

## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 Cognitive Dysfunction in Schizophrenia: The Unmet Need

Schizophrenia affects approximately 1% of the global population and imposes disproportionate functional disability driven not primarily by positive psychotic symptoms, but by the pervasive cognitive impairments that characterise the illness. These deficits span at least seven neuropsychological domains: processing speed, attention/vigilance, working memory, verbal learning and memory, visual learning and memory, reasoning and problem-solving, and social cognition. They are present before illness onset, stable across the illness course, approximately 1–2 standard deviations below the performance of healthy comparison groups, and only marginally responsive to available antipsychotic treatments \(Harvey & Keefe, 2001; Keefe et al., 1999\).

Current antipsychotic drugs—whether first-generation \(typical\) or second-generation \(atypical\)—achieve their primary efficacy through dopamine D2 receptor blockade. All available agents share this fundamental mechanism, and meta-analyses have consistently demonstrated that cognitive improvements with atypical antipsychotics are, at best, modest \(effect sizes 0.17–0.46\) and may largely reflect practice effects or the removal of the additional cognitive impairment imposed by first-generation agents' anticholinergic and sedative properties \(Woodward et al., 2005\). A growing body of evidence now suggests that antipsychotic medication may in some respects actively worsen specific cognitive domains—a significant interaction showing verbal learning and memory decline in paliperidone/risperidone-treated patients relative to placebo-treated psychosis patients and healthy controls has been reported in a randomised clinical trial \(Soni et al., 2023\).

The widely used second-generation antipsychotics paliperidone \(the active metabolite of risperidone\) and olanzapine present contrasting cognitive profiles. Olanzapine has demonstrated selective improvement in verbal learning, memory, and verbal fluency in prospective studies, but its anticholinergic muscarinic M1 binding properties are predicted to limit broader cognitive gains and may contribute to context-specific working memory impairment \(Harvey et al., 1999; Keefe et al., 1999\). Paliperidone's lower muscarinic affinity compared to olanzapine might theoretically favour less cognitive interference via cholinergic mechanisms, yet clinical data suggest it shares similar limitations in improving attention and working memory \(Harvey et al., 2005\).

### 1.2 The Cholinergic Hypothesis and New Therapeutic Directions

The cholinergic hypothesis of cognitive dysfunction in schizophrenia has been reinvigorated by convergent neurobiological evidence including post-mortem data showing reduced muscarinic M1 and M4 receptor expression in schizophrenia prefrontal cortex; PET imaging studies correlating muscarinic M1 binding potential with cognitive performance; and, most compellingly, the clinical success of xanomeline-trospium \(COBENFY\) in the FDA's September 2024 approval for schizophrenia. Xanomeline, an M1/M4-preferring muscarinic agonist, demonstrated cognitive improvements in verbal learning and short-term memory in an early proof-of-concept study by Shekhar et al. \(2008\), and more recently, pooled analysis of the Phase 3 EMERGENT trials demonstrated meaningful PANSS total score reductions with broader cognitive benefits \(Horan et al., 2025\).

The M1 receptor's cognitive relevance operates through multiple mechanisms: M1 receptors on prefrontal pyramidal neurons enhance delay-related firing critical to working memory maintenance via interactions with NMDA receptors \(Galvin et al., 2021\); M4 receptor activation on striatal cholinergic interneurons modulates dopamine release in a manner that reduces positive symptoms without the extrapyramidal burden of D2 blockade \(Correll et al., 2024\). This dual M1/M4 mechanism of xanomeline thus addresses both cognitive and positive symptom domains simultaneously, representing a fundamentally different pharmacological strategy from all prior antipsychotics.

The CogniMax-Pro formulation is designed to extend this muscarinic cholinergic strategy with additional pharmacological dimensions that address the metabolic, neuroplastic, and bioenergetic dimensions of cognitive dysfunction in antipsychotic-treated individuals.

## 2. The Cognitive Impairment Profile Targeted by CogniMax-Pro

### 2.1 Intrinsic vs. Iatrogenic Components

The cognitive impairment encountered in antipsychotic-treated patients comprises at minimum two partially separable components. The first is the intrinsic, illness-related neuropsychological deficit present from the prodromal phase, likely reflecting neurodevelopmental anomalies in prefrontal-hippocampal circuitry, GABAergic interneuron pathology, glutamatergic hypofunction at NMDA receptors, and reduced cholinergic tone. This component is 1–2 standard deviations below normative performance across multiple domains and is largely unresponsive to all currently available pharmacological treatments \(Keefe & Harvey, 2012\).

The second component reflects iatrogenic effects of antipsychotic medications. First-generation antipsychotics with high anticholinergic burden demonstrably impair verbal memory, learning, and executive function via muscarinic M1 antagonism \(Joshi et al., 2021; Ballesteros et al., 2018\). While second-generation agents including olanzapine and quetiapine have lower intrinsic anticholinergic affinity, the cumulative anticholinergic load of polypharmacy \(antipsychotic \+ anticholinergic antiparkinsonian medication \+ other drugs with incidental muscarinic antagonism\) can be substantial. Emerging evidence from randomised controlled trials suggests that paliperidone/risperidone treatment is specifically associated with verbal learning and memory decline relative to placebo, with moderate to large effect sizes \(Soni et al., 2023\).

Metabolic dysfunction—common with olanzapine and paliperidone due to histamine H1 and adrenergic α1 receptor antagonism driving weight gain, insulin resistance, and dyslipidaemia—represents a third dimension of the cognitive burden. Insulin resistance impairs hippocampal glucose utilisation, reduces BDNF expression, and promotes neuroinflammation through advanced glycation end-product accumulation. The metabolic component of antipsychotic-associated cognitive impairment is addressable pharmacologically and represents a distinct target for CogniMax-Pro's metabolic components.

## 3. CogniMax-Pro: Component-by-Component Pharmacological Analysis

### 3.1 XAN-Extended \(Xanomeline Extended-Release\) — 150 mg

Xanomeline \(3-\[4-\(hexyloxy\)phenyl\]-1,2,5,6-tetrahydro-1-methylpyridine\) is a tetrahydropyridine-based M1/M4-preferring muscarinic acetylcholine receptor agonist initially developed for Alzheimer's disease and subsequently investigated as an antipsychotic. Its selectivity for M1 \(Ki ~2 nM\) and M4 \(Ki ~7 nM\) over M2 and M3 receptors, combined with its 'wash-resistant' binding to a secondary lipophilic receptor site, underlies its unique pharmacological profile. In schizophrenia, xanomeline-treated subjects showed significantly greater improvement in total BPRS and PANSS scores versus placebo, with verbal learning and short-term memory showing the most robust cognitive improvements \(Shekhar et al., 2008\). The 2024 FDA approval of xanomeline combined with the peripherally restricted muscarinic antagonist trospium \(COBENFY\) for schizophrenia validates this target and provides a clinical anchoring point for the formulation.

XAN-Extended in CogniMax-Pro is a prodrug variant in which a lipophilic ester group at the phenolic position generates a 12–18 hour controlled-release profile following oral administration. The immediate-release fraction \(45 mg, 30% of total dose\) achieves rapid cognitive activation within 1–2 hours; the extended-release fraction \(105 mg, 70%\) maintains therapeutic plasma concentrations across the dosing interval. Unlike the approved COBENFY formulation, CogniMax-Pro does not co-formulate trospium chloride; consequently, the theoretical xanomeline dose is substantially lower \(150 mg vs. COBENFY's 50–125 mg xanomeline twice daily\) and is designed to leverage synergistic cholinergic facilitation from co-formulated choline precursors rather than relying on high-dose xanomeline monotherapy.

### 3.2 MetaCholine-1 \(MC-1\) — 400 mg \(Novel Fictional Compound\)

MetaCholine-1 is a first-in-class fictional conjugate molecule designed to provide simultaneous cholinergic precursor supplementation \(via the alpha-GPC component\) and AMPK activation \(via the berberine component\) from a single molecular entity that is predicted to release its active constituents in a pH-dependent, tissue-selective manner. The hemisuccinate ester bridge between the berberine C-9 hydroxyl and the alpha-GPC glycerol backbone is theoretically cleaved by carboxylesterases present in brain and liver parenchyma, releasing alpha-GPC for choline transport and phosphatidylcholine synthesis, and berberine for AMPK/LKB1 pathway activation.

This compound is entirely fictional and has not been synthesised or characterised. Its rationale is derived from the independently documented activities of its constituent moieties: alpha-GPC \(L-α-glycerylphosphorylcholine\) is an established cholinergic precursor that crosses the blood-brain barrier and increases cortical acetylcholine synthesis and release; berberine is an isoquinoline alkaloid with demonstrated neuroprotective and cognitive effects in animal models mediated via LKB1/AMPK signalling, inhibition of acetylcholinesterase activity, and PI3K/Akt-dependent synaptic plasticity \(Dan et al., 2023; Yuan et al., 2019\).

### 3.3 The Berberine Component: Published Evidence for Cognitive Effects

Berberine's cognitive-enhancing properties have been extensively characterised in preclinical models of Alzheimer's disease and ageing-related cognitive decline. A systematic review and meta-analysis of 19 animal studies \(n=360 animals\) found that berberine significantly reduced escape latency in the Morris water maze \(SMD = −2.19, 95% CI: −2.50 to −1.88, p < 0.00001\), increased platform crossings \(SMD = 4.27, p < 0.00001\), and time in target quadrant \(SMD = 5.92, p < 0.00001\), demonstrating robust spatial memory enhancement across diverse cognitive impairment models \(Liu et al., 2024\). A network pharmacology study of berberine in ageing-related cognitive dysfunction identified AMPK, PI3K/Akt, and MAPK pathways as key mechanistic nodes, with in vivo confirmation of Akt1, Ctnnb1, Tp53, and Jun as core targets \(Yao et al., 2023\).

Berberine's direct neuroprotective mechanisms include inhibition of acetylcholinesterase via LKB1/AMPK-mediated downregulation \(thereby increasing synaptic acetylcholine\), reduction of neuroinflammatory cytokine production via NF-κB pathway inhibition, enhancement of mitophagy for clearance of dysfunctional mitochondria, and promotion of BDNF expression downstream of AMPK-CREB signalling \(Dan et al., 2023; Yao et al., 2023; Fan et al., 2019\). Importantly, berberine has demonstrated synergistic effects in combination with cholinergic therapies, potentially providing additive benefit within the CogniMax-Pro framework. It should be noted, however, that clinical evidence for berberine's efficacy in human cognitive enhancement is nascent, with no completed randomised controlled trials in schizophrenia populations.

### 3.4 NeuroplastiCholine \(NPC-2\) — 200 mg \(Novel Fictional Compound\)

NeuroplastiCholine is a fictional first-in-class molecule designed as a bifunctional conjugate combining a xanomeline-derived M1/M4 muscarinic pharmacophore with a BDNF loop II mimetic peptide \(sequence: Ac-HSDPARRGEL-NH₂\) connected via a PEG₄ spacer and maleimide-thiol chemistry. The compound is intended to simultaneously activate muscarinic receptors for immediate cholinergic cognitive enhancement while delivering a neurotrophin signal through the xanomeline moiety's BBB transit capability. This compound is entirely hypothetical and no synthesis or biological data exists.

Its mechanistic rationale is grounded in the well-established role of BDNF/TrkB signalling in synaptic plasticity, learning, and memory consolidation. BDNF expression is decreased in the prefrontal cortex and hippocampus of schizophrenia patients, and reduced BDNF has been associated with poorer cognitive performance across multiple domains. Antipsychotic medications show variable and dose-dependent effects on BDNF—some increasing it acutely, others being neutral or negative over long-term treatment. NPC-2's TrkB-activating peptide domain is designed to supplement deficient neurotrophin signalling independently of medication-related BDNF modulation, while the M1/M4-activating xanomeline domain provides a synergistic upstream cholinergic drive for the same CREB-dependent transcriptional pathways.

### 3.5 CholinoBuproion \(CB-1\) — 75 mg \(Novel Fictional Compound\)

CholinoBuproion is a fictional dual-mechanism compound designed to merge bupropion's dopamine transporter \(DAT\) and norepinephrine transporter \(NET\) inhibition properties with M1/M4 muscarinic agonism via integration of the xanomeline tetrahydropyridine pharmacophore. Bupropion \(an approved atypical antidepressant and smoking cessation aid\) inhibits DAT \(Ki ~520 nM\) and NET \(Ki ~840 nM\) without significant serotonergic activity, and its cognitive-activating properties via prefrontal catecholaminergic enhancement are well established in both mood disorder and healthy volunteer studies. No such compound has been synthesised and its dual-pharmacophore design represents speculative medicinal chemistry.

The rationale for combining bupropion-like catecholaminergic activity with muscarinic agonism reflects the well-established interactive regulation of prefrontal cognitive function by acetylcholine and catecholamines. Dopamine D1 receptor activation enhances delay-period firing of prefrontal pyramidal neurons through an 'inverted-U' function that is potentiated by simultaneous muscarinic M1 activation via NMDA receptor facilitation. The theoretical CholinoBuproion compound would exploit this synergy within a single molecule, avoiding the pharmacokinetic complexity of separate combination therapy.

## 4. Established Supportive Components

### 4.1 Alpha-GPC \(L-α-Glycerylphosphorylcholine\) — 600 mg

Alpha-GPC is among the best-characterised oral choline donors for CNS applications, with approximately 90% CNS penetration following oral administration compared to <10% for choline bitartrate. It is directly incorporated into the phosphatidylcholine biosynthetic pathway via the CDP-choline \(Kennedy\) pathway, releasing free choline for acetylcholine synthesis in cholinergic neurons. Multiple randomised controlled trials have demonstrated alpha-GPC's efficacy in Alzheimer's disease and age-associated memory impairment, with improvements in MMSE, ADAS-Cog, and clinical global impression scales observed in 3–6 month trials. Its relevance within CogniMax-Pro is to support the acetylcholine synthesis substrate availability required to maximise the cognitive effects of the muscarinic agonist components.

### 4.2 Phosphatidylserine — 200 mg

Phosphatidylserine \(PS\) is a phospholipid concentrated in neuronal cell membranes, where it facilitates signal transduction, provides an anionic surface for protein kinase C \(PKC\) activation, and supports membrane fluidity critical for ion channel and receptor dynamics. Brain PS levels decline with ageing and have been shown to correlate inversely with cognitive performance in epidemiological studies. Randomised controlled trials of PS supplementation \(300–800 mg/day\) in age-related cognitive decline have demonstrated modest but consistent improvements in memory, learning, and concentration, with a favourable safety profile. PS's inclusion in CogniMax-Pro is intended to provide structural membrane support for the enhanced cholinergic and adrenergic signalling driven by the active compounds.

### 4.3 NAD\+ Precursors: NMN and the Mitochondrial Axis — 150 mg NMN, 75 mg PQQ, 75 mg CoQ10

The metabolic consequences of long-term antipsychotic treatment—particularly with olanzapine, which produces the most pronounced metabolic syndrome of all second-generation antipsychotics—include mitochondrial dysfunction, increased reactive oxygen species production, impaired neuronal energy metabolism, and reduced NAD\+/NADH ratios. These metabolic perturbations are directly relevant to cognitive function: ATP availability constrains synaptic vesicle cycling, membrane potential maintenance, and the energetically demanding processes of long-term potentiation.

β-Nicotinamide mononucleotide \(NMN\) is a direct precursor to NAD\+ biosynthesis, bypassing the rate-limiting NAMPT enzyme and providing efficient replenishment of the NAD\+ pool depleted by antipsychotic-associated metabolic stress. NMN's cognitive effects in aged rodents are robust and recapitulate age-reversal in multiple domains. Human clinical trial data, while still accumulating, suggest NMN \(250–1200 mg/day\) is safe, increases blood NAD\+ levels dose-dependently, and may improve physical and some cognitive parameters in older adults.

PQQ \(pyrroloquinoline quinone\) functions as a redox cofactor and mitochondrial biogenesis activator, promoting PGC-1α expression and mitochondrial proliferation through CREB and ATF-2 pathways. CoQ10 \(ubiquinone\) serves as an essential electron carrier in the mitochondrial respiratory chain \(Complex I–III\) and as a membrane-associated antioxidant. Both compounds have established safety profiles at the proposed doses \(75 mg each\) and mechanistically complement NMN by supporting mitochondrial respiratory efficiency and reducing oxidative phosphorylation-associated ROS generation.

### 4.4 Berberine Phytosome — 400 mg

As described in Section 3.3, berberine's cognitive-relevant effects operate through AMPK, PI3K/Akt, MAPK, and cholinergic pathways. The phytosome formulation \(berberine complexed with phospholipids including phosphatidylcholine and phosphatidylserine\) substantially improves berberine's characteristically poor oral bioavailability \(< 1% standard formulation\) to approximately 30–45% through enhanced lipid solubilisation and reduced P-glycoprotein efflux. This formulation strategy is supported by comparative pharmacokinetic data showing 3–5-fold higher Cmax and AUC with phytosome versus standard berberine formulations.

## 5. Antipsychotic Compatibility Analysis

### 5.1 Paliperidone

Paliperidone's receptor binding profile \(D2, D3, 5-HT2A, 5-HT7, α1, α2, H1\) does not include any significant activity at muscarinic, AMPK, or mitochondrial targets directly relevant to CogniMax-Pro's active components. The primary interaction concern is pharmacokinetic: paliperidone is a substrate of P-glycoprotein \(P-gp\) and several CYP enzymes are peripherally involved in its metabolism. None of CogniMax-Pro's components are predicted to be significant P-gp inhibitors or CYP3A4/CYP2D6 inhibitors at their therapeutic concentrations, suggesting a low pharmacokinetic interaction risk. The muscarinic agonist component \(XAN-Extended\) might theoretically enhance dopaminergic modulation in paliperidone-treated patients by reducing the M4-dependent inhibition of striatal dopamine release, but at the formulation's lower xanomeline doses, this interaction is expected to be pharmacologically subordinate to paliperidone's D2 antagonism.

### 5.2 Olanzapine

Olanzapine presents a more complex interaction profile due to its own modest muscarinic M1 antagonist activity \(Ki ~73 nM\). The direct pharmacodynamic interaction between olanzapine's M1 antagonism and XAN-Extended's M1 agonism is a key concern: these effects are partially competitive at M1 receptors, and the net effect on cognitive M1 receptor activity will depend on the occupancy balance between the two drugs. At standard therapeutic olanzapine doses \(10–20 mg/day\) producing approximately 60–70% M1 occupancy, the 150 mg XAN-Extended dose in CogniMax-Pro is designed to partially override this occupancy through its higher M1 affinity \(Ki ~2 nM vs. olanzapine's ~73 nM\). However, this has not been empirically validated, and the formulation should be considered theoretically incomplete without pharmacokinetic-pharmacodynamic bridging studies in olanzapine-treated subjects.

Berberine at therapeutic doses \(400–500 mg/day\) may modestly inhibit CYP3A4, a pathway relevant to olanzapine's minor metabolic clearance. The magnitude of this interaction at the 400 mg berberine phytosome dose is predicted to be clinically insignificant based on published berberine CYP inhibition data, but monitoring of olanzapine plasma levels would be advisable in clinical practice.

## 6. Dual-Layer Extended Release Technology

CogniMax-Pro's formulation employs a dual-layer capsule technology that separates components into an immediate-release \(IR\) pellet layer \(30% of total dose\) and an extended-release \(ER\) HPMC matrix layer \(70% of total dose\). The IR layer delivers XAN-Extended \(45 mg\), alpha-GPC \(600 mg\), and NMN \(150 mg\) with >90% release within 2 hours post-dose, providing rapid cognitive activation upon morning administration. The ER layer delivers the remaining XAN-Extended \(105 mg\), MetaCholine-1 \(400 mg\), NeuroplastiCholine \(200 mg\), CholinoBuproion \(75 mg\), berberine complex \(500 mg\), phosphatidylserine \(200 mg\), PQQ \(75 mg\), and CoQ10 \(75 mg\) in a controlled linear-release profile over 18–24 hours.

The HPMC K100M matrix employed for extended release is a well-established pharmaceutical polymer for sustained-release oral formulations, providing pH-independent drug release by a combination of diffusion through the hydrated gel layer and erosion of the tablet matrix. The polymer's high viscosity grade \(K100M\) was selected to achieve the target slow erosion rate required for 18–24-hour delivery of the high-dose \(500 mg berberine\) component. The polyethylene glycol 6000 pore former controls porosity within the matrix to modulate the diffusion pathway length and achieve the target release kinetics independently of gastrointestinal transit variability.

## 7. Mechanistic Synergy Analysis

### 7.1 Multi-Level Synergies

The CogniMax-Pro formulation is designed around five mechanistic pillars that converge on shared downstream pathways relevant to cognitive function. The convergence of M1 muscarinic agonism \(XAN-Extended, NeuroplastiCholine\) with NMDA receptor enhancement is the most proximate cognitive synergy: M1 receptor activation directly potentiates NMDA receptor currents via PKC-mediated NR1 phosphorylation, amplifying the NMDA-dependent signal that drives working memory delay-period firing in prefrontal pyramidal neurons. Simultaneous catecholaminergic facilitation via CholinoBuproion's DAT/NET inhibition activates D1 receptors, which provide a second, complementary input to the same NMDA-dependent prefrontal circuit via cAMP/PKA-mediated NR2B phosphorylation.

At the metabolic level, AMPK activation by berberine \(and the MetaCholine-1 berberine component\) increases glucose transporter GLUT3 expression and mitochondrial biogenesis via PGC-1α, enhancing neuronal energy substrate availability. This metabolic support is expected to synergise with NMN \+ PQQ \+ CoQ10's direct mitochondrial bioenergetic enhancement to provide the ATP supply necessary for the energetically expensive processes of LTP induction and synaptic protein synthesis.

At the trophic level, BDNF signalling \(from NeuroplastiCholine's TrkB-activating peptide\) and CREB phosphorylation \(from α7/M1 receptor activation\) converge on the same transcriptional programme: Arc/Arg3.1, AMPA receptor GluA1 subunit, and synaptic scaffolding proteins including PSD-95. This trophic convergence is expected to produce sustained neuroplastic changes with repeated administration that outlast the pharmacological half-life of individual components.

## 9. Translational Challenges and Critical Limitations

### 9.1 The Multi-Component Problem

The principal translational challenge for CogniMax-Pro is the inherent difficulty of demonstrating efficacy, attributing benefit to specific components, and establishing safety for a complex multi-component formulation. Regulatory frameworks for fixed-dose combination products require that each component contribute demonstrably to the clinical benefit, and establishing this contribution through pharmacological dissection in human clinical trials is methodologically demanding. The formulation as designed contains three components \(MetaCholine-1, NeuroplastiCholine, CholinoBuproion\) that do not exist as synthesised compounds and for which no preclinical or clinical data exists—these represent speculative design exercises.

The pragmatic path to clinical translation would require sequential development: first, the established components \(xanomeline extended-release \+ berberine phytosome \+ alpha-GPC \+ NAD\+ precursors\) could be evaluated as an adjunctive combination in a controlled trial of antipsychotic-treated patients; the three fictional novel compounds would require independent synthesis, preclinical characterisation, and Phase 1 safety studies before incorporation into a combination product.

### 9.2 The Muscarinic Agonist Component in Context

The inclusion of a xanomeline extended-release component is the most pharmacologically defensible aspect of CogniMax-Pro. Xanomeline has reached Phase 3 clinical trials and achieved FDA approval \(as COBENFY in combination with trospium\); its cognitive enhancement effects in schizophrenia have been documented in proof-of-concept trials \(Shekhar et al., 2008\) and confirmed in pooled Phase 3 analyses \(Horan et al., 2025; Kaul et al., 2024\). The proposed XAN-Extended prodrug variant addresses xanomeline's historically poor pharmacokinetics \(t½ 4–6 hours standard formulation\) but introduces the regulatory complexity of a novel chemical entity designation rather than the approved drug substance.

The decision to omit trospium chloride from CogniMax-Pro \(present in the approved COBENFY formulation to reduce peripheral muscarinic side effects\) is a notable design choice that would likely result in unacceptable cholinergic side effects at any dose sufficient for cognitive benefit. The published history of xanomeline development without peripheral protection is instructive: early clinical trials with unprotected xanomeline in Alzheimer's disease were terminated due to nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea, and cardiovascular side effects. The COBENFY formulation's innovation was precisely the recognition that peripheral protection via trospium is necessary for therapeutic tolerability. A reformulated CogniMax-Pro would require incorporation of trospium or another peripherally restricted muscarinic antagonist to be clinically viable.

### 9.3 Evidence Gaps for Individual Components

Berberine's preclinical evidence for cognitive enhancement is substantial and mechanistically grounded, but direct clinical evidence in schizophrenia populations is absent. The translational record for cognitive interventions in schizophrenia is poor generally—even compounds with robust preclinical profiles have repeatedly failed to replicate in adequately powered Phase 3 trials. Alpha-GPC and phosphatidylserine have the most direct human clinical evidence, primarily in age-related cognitive decline and mild cognitive impairment populations, but these populations differ substantially from antipsychotic-treated schizophrenia patients in baseline cognitive profile, symptom burden, and medication context.

The NAD\+/mitochondrial components \(NMN, PQQ, CoQ10\) are well-tolerated and mechanistically rational, but their specific relevance to antipsychotic-associated metabolic cognitive impairment has not been tested in controlled trials. The hypothesis that antipsychotic-induced metabolic syndrome impairs cognition through mitochondrial dysfunction is well-supported theoretically but lacks direct clinical intervention data.

## 10. Discussion

CogniMax-Pro represents an ambitious theoretical exercise in rational polypharmacological design for a clinically important but pharmacologically underaddressed problem. The formulation's scientific foundation is uneven: the muscarinic M1/M4 agonist backbone \(XAN-Extended\) rests on well-validated pharmacological rationale and now has FDA-approval validation through the closely related COBENFY; the metabolic and mitochondrial support components \(berberine, NMN, PQQ, CoQ10, alpha-GPC\) have plausible mechanisms and established safety profiles but heterogeneous clinical evidence; and the three novel fictional compounds \(MetaCholine-1, NeuroplastiCholine, CholinoBuproion\) exist only as theoretical constructs without any empirical characterisation.

The failure of antipsychotic medications to adequately address cognitive dysfunction in schizophrenia reflects a fundamental limitation of their common mechanism—D2 dopamine receptor blockade—rather than inadequate optimization of that mechanism. The September 2024 approval of xanomeline-trospium as the first antipsychotic with an entirely non-dopaminergic mechanism in 70 years demonstrates that this limitation can be transcended \(Correll et al., 2024\). CogniMax-Pro can be seen as an extension of this paradigm shift: a formulation designed to maximally leverage the cholinergic, metabolic, and neuroplastic dimensions of cognitive function that lie upstream and downstream of dopaminergic neurotransmission.

Several methodological improvements would strengthen future iterations of this formulation concept: \(1\) incorporation of peripherally restricted muscarinic antagonism \(trospium or equivalent\) to enable effective xanomeline dosing; \(2\) replacement of the three fictional novel compounds with clinically characterised adjuncts such as memantine, sarcosine, or d-cycloserine for glutamatergic support; \(3\) a clinical trial design stratifying patients by cholinergic deficit biomarkers \(M1 PET binding potential, blood cholinergic tone markers\) to identify the subpopulation most likely to benefit; and \(4\) a biomarker-rich Phase 1/2 study establishing dose-response relationships for each component before commitment to the full combination.

## 11. Conclusion

Cognitive dysfunction in antipsychotic-treated schizophrenia remains one of psychiatry's most consequential unmet needs, resistant to the therapeutic advances of the past three decades and closely linked to functional disability, unemployment, and reduced quality of life. CogniMax-Pro is a theoretical multi-modal formulation that attempts to address this need through five pharmacological pillars: muscarinic cholinergic enhancement, AMPK/metabolic pathway activation, neurotrophin signalling augmentation, catecholaminergic prefrontal facilitation, and mitochondrial bioenergetic support.

The formulation's established components—particularly the xanomeline analogue, now supported by the clinical validation of COBENFY \(xanomeline-trospium\), and the berberine-based metabolic support complex—provide a scientifically grounded foundation. The three novel fictional compounds \(MetaCholine-1, NeuroplastiCholine, CholinoBuproion\) represent speculative but theoretically motivated design concepts that require synthesis, preclinical characterisation, and clinical evaluation before any claims of efficacy can be substantiated. The formulation as a whole cannot be recommended for clinical use in its current theoretical form and would require substantial modification—including the addition of peripheral cholinergic protection—to address the well-documented tolerability limitations of unprotected xanomeline exposure.

Nevertheless, CogniMax-Pro illustrates the principle that rational polypharmacological design, grounded in the mechanistic heterogeneity of cognitive dysfunction, may offer a more complete therapeutic strategy than any single-mechanism approach. The recent success of xanomeline-trospium provides an encouraging proof of concept that targeting cholinergic systems outside the dopamine paradigm can produce clinically meaningful outcomes. The challenge for the field is to translate this proof of concept into broader, more durable cognitive improvement across the multiple pharmacological dimensions that contribute to the cognitive burden of antipsychotic-era schizophrenia.
