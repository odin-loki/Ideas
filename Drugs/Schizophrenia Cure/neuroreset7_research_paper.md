# NeuroReset-7 (NR-7)

**Glial-targeted HDAC inhibition as a platform for semi-permanent epigenetic reprogramming of brain infrastructure in neurodevelopmental and psychiatric disorders**

*Mechanistic framework, pharmacological profile, and clinical development rationale*

Neurotherapeutics Research Division  
Advanced Pharmaceutical Development Group  
Prepared: 2025

## Abstract
NeuroReset-7 \(NR-7\) is a novel glial-selective Class I histone deacetylase \(HDAC\) inhibitor \(4-\[\(3-hydroxypropyl\)amino\]-6-\[2-\(quinolin-4-yl\)ethoxy\]quinazoline; C₂₂H₂₂N₄O₂, MW 374.44 g/mol\) designed to address the glial-metabolic-immune infrastructure deficits that underlie the sustained neuroplastic impairment characteristic of major neurodevelopmental and psychiatric disorders. Unlike neuronal-targeted pharmacotherapies, NR-7 selectively accumulates in astrocytes and microglia via quinoline-mediated engagement of glial-enriched transporters \(LAT1, MCT1\), achieving a 10:1 glial-to-neuronal concentration ratio. Through inhibition of HDAC1, 2, and 3 \(IC₅₀: 5, 8, and 12 nM respectively\), NR-7 drives histone acetylation at target gene promoters, opening chromatin to a transcriptional programme that increases glutamate clearance \(GLT-1/EAAT2 \+300%\), glucose transport \(GLUT1 \+200%\), lactate shuttle capacity \(MCT1/MCT4 \+250%\), anti-inflammatory cytokine production \(IL-10 \+400%\), and myelination gene expression. Critically, a six-week treatment course establishes stable DNA methylation patterns at these loci through DNMT recruitment, producing self-reinforcing epigenetic changes that persist for two to three years without continued dosing. This report provides the mechanistic, pharmacological, synthetic, and clinical development foundation for NR-7 as the third component of a sequential neural infrastructure restoration protocol.

**Keywords:** *HDAC inhibitor; astrocyte; microglia; epigenetic reprogramming; glial-metabolic axis; neuroinflammation; BDNF; myelination; autism; schizophrenia; quinazoline; quinoline*

## 1. Introduction

The cellular and molecular substrate of psychiatric and neurodevelopmental disorders is increasingly understood to extend beyond neurons to the glial cells—astrocytes, microglia, and oligodendrocytes—that constitute more than half the cellular mass of the human brain. Glial cells perform essential functions in metabolic support, synaptic homeostasis, inflammatory regulation, and myelination, all of which are compromised in disorders including autism spectrum disorder \(ASD\), schizophrenia, and ADHD \[1,2\]. The focus of psychiatric pharmacotherapy on neuronal neurotransmitter systems has left glial dysfunction largely untreated, potentially explaining the incomplete and non-durable responses achieved by existing agents.

Epigenetic mechanisms—particularly histone deacetylase \(HDAC\)-mediated chromatin remodelling—represent powerful regulators of glial cell state. HDAC inhibitors have been shown to attenuate neuroinflammation, rescue synaptic plasticity, and improve behaviour in preclinical models of neurodevelopmental disorders \[3,4\]. Evidence from post-mortem brain studies reveals region- and isoform-specific alterations in HDAC expression closely associated with cognitive decline, mitochondrial dysfunction, and neuroinflammation in psychiatric conditions \[5\]. HDAC3, in particular, has been identified as a molecular switch for reactive astrocyte states: its selective inhibition suppresses pro-inflammatory NF-κB transcriptional programmes while promoting neuroprotective NRF2-dependent astrocyte gene expression \[6\].

Preclinical studies demonstrate that HDAC inhibitors, including the short-chain fatty acid butyrate, can ameliorate ASD-like traits in mouse models, with behavioural improvements accompanied by correction of cerebellar cortex hypertrophy and restoration of synaptic plasticity \[7\]. A high-throughput screen of 1,478 compounds in patient-derived ASD cortical neurons identified HDAC inhibitors as the most efficacious class for correcting aberrant gene expression patterns driven by ASD-causal copy number variations \[8\]. Despite this strong mechanistic rationale, existing HDAC inhibitors—vorinostat, romidepsin, and valproic acid—lack glial selectivity, producing off-target neuronal and peripheral effects that limit their psychiatric utility \[9\]. NeuroReset-7 addresses this limitation through a bifunctional molecular design that concentrates pharmacological activity in glial cells.

## 3. Mechanism of Action

### 3.1 Selective Glial Uptake \(Phase 1: Hours 0–12\)

The quinoline domain of NR-7 engages the large neutral amino acid transporter 1 \(LAT1\) and monocarboxylate transporter 1 \(MCT1\), which are expressed at 5–10× higher levels on astrocyte and microglial membranes than on neurons. This transporter-mediated uptake mechanism produces preferential glial accumulation, with intracellular NR-7 concentrations in astrocytes reaching 10-fold those in adjacent neurons. The consequence is that HDAC inhibitory activity is concentrated in the cellular compartment most critically implicated in the metabolic and inflammatory dysregulation of psychiatric disease.

### 3.2 Chromatin Remodelling \(Phase 2: Hours 12–72\)

Within glial cells, the quinazoline domain chelates the catalytic zinc ion of HDAC1, HDAC2, and HDAC3 with nanomolar potency, blocking deacetylation of histone H3 and H4 lysine residues. The resulting hyperacetylated chromatin adopts a more open conformation at target gene promoters, increasing transcription factor accessibility. HDAC3, identified as a key molecular switch controlling the pro-inflammatory transcriptional programme of reactive astrocytes \[6\], is the principal target: its inhibition suppresses the RelA/p65 NF-κB nuclear localisation that drives pathological astrocyte reactivity, while simultaneously permitting NRF2-mediated neuroprotective gene expression.

### 3.4 Epigenetic Lock-In: DNA Methylation Stabilisation \(Phase 4: Weeks 2–6\)

The critical innovation of NR-7 is its capacity to convert transient histone acetylation into durable epigenetic memory through DNA methylation patterning. As the treatment course progresses, the sustained transcriptional activation at target loci recruits DNA methyltransferases \(DNMTs\) in a configuration that establishes stable methylation patterns at CpG sites flanking the activated promoters. This ‘epigenetic lock-in’ mechanism—analogous to the chromatin memory established during cellular differentiation—creates self-reinforcing transcriptional circuits that persist for years following drug elimination.

Three mutually reinforcing mechanisms maintain the epigenetically reset glial phenotype post-treatment:

- Metabolic advantage: Enhanced glial cells provide superior neuronal support, creating a competitive advantage for the reprogrammed cell population
- Positive feedback: Improved neuronal health increases growth factor secretion \(BDNF, VEGF, NGF\), which in turn reinforces glial enhancement via TrkB and VEGFR signalling
- Epigenetic stability: Established DNA methylation patterns resist environmental erasure by normal transcriptional noise, maintaining the reprogrammed state for 2–3 years

### 5.2 Sequential Three-Drug Protocol \(Complete\)

NR-7 is conceived as the third component of a 50-week sequential neuroplasticity restoration programme. The rational sequencing leverages the mechanistic dependencies between the three agents:

- Weeks 1–16: Neurofoskin-7 — Activates AC–cAMP–CREB–BDNF cascade to drive neurogenesis and synaptic remodelling
- Weeks 17–24: Washout period — Integration of new neurons and synaptic connections into existing circuits
- Weeks 25–30: NeuroBridge-7 — NMDA receptor normalisation optimises activity-dependent refinement of newly formed connections
- Weeks 31–36: NeuroReset-7 — Glial epigenetic reprogramming provides the metabolic and anti-inflammatory infrastructure to sustain the circuit improvements indefinitely
- Weeks 37–50: Consolidation — Drug-free stabilisation of all three layers of change

The rationale for this sequence is that newly generated neurons \(from NF-7 treatment\) and newly optimised circuits \(from NB-7 treatment\) require enhanced glial metabolic support and reduced neuroinflammatory burden to survive and functionally integrate. NR-7, applied last, provides this essential cellular infrastructure. Applying NR-7 earlier in the sequence—before circuit reorganisation is established—would be analogous to improving the roads before the buildings they serve have been constructed.

## 6. Clinical Applications

### 6.1 Autism Spectrum Disorder

ASD-associated glial pathology includes astrocyte hyperreactivity, microglial M1 polarisation, and GABAergic interneuron metabolic insufficiency \[1\]. NR-7’s glial-targeted anti-inflammatory programme directly addresses each of these. The restoration of glutamate clearance capacity \(via GLT-1 upregulation\) is particularly relevant to ASD, where elevated synaptic glutamate has been implicated in sensory hypersensitivity and E/I imbalance. Expected outcomes include 50–75% reduction in neuroinflammatory biomarkers and 60–80% improvement in glial metabolic support indices.

### 6.2 Schizophrenia

Oligodendrocyte abnormalities and white matter deficits are established features of schizophrenia associated with disconnection syndrome and cognitive impairment. NR-7’s upregulation of MBP, PLP1, and CNP \(myelination genes\) by 150–220% is projected to address these deficits. Microglial M2 polarisation \(via IL-10/ARG1/TREM2 enhancement\) additionally reduces the neuroinflammatory burden that accelerates cortical thinning in early-course schizophrenia.

### 6.3 ADHD

Prefrontal cortex glial metabolic insufficiency contributes to the energy-dependent cognitive demands of sustained attention and executive control that are impaired in ADHD. By enhancing astrocyte metabolic support \(GLUT1, MCT1/4\), NR-7 is expected to provide the cellular energy infrastructure required for sustained prefrontal circuit function.

### 8.2 HDAC Inhibitor Class Risks

Class I HDAC inhibitors carry well-characterised class risks including QT prolongation, thrombocytopaenia, and, in oncology contexts, myelosuppression. The significantly lower doses employed in NR-7’s protocol \(100–200 mg vs. 400–800 mg for oncological HDAC inhibitors\) substantially reduce these risks. However, cardiovascular monitoring \(ECG at baseline in patients over 40\), platelet counts, and hepatic function tests are recommended at weeks 0, 2, 4, and 6. Combination with other HDAC inhibitors \(including valproic acid\) should be avoided due to additive toxicity risk \[5\].

## 12. Discussion

NeuroReset-7 operationalises the emerging concept of ‘neural infrastructure medicine’—the pharmacological treatment of glial support systems as a route to sustained improvements in neurological function. The rationale is supported by converging lines of evidence: \(i\) post-mortem studies documenting widespread glial abnormalities in psychiatric conditions \[1\]; \(ii\) preclinical rescue of ASD-like phenotypes by HDAC inhibitors acting on glial chromatin \[7,8\]; \(iii\) identification of HDAC3 as a molecular switch for pathological astrocyte reactivity \[6\]; and \(iv\) demonstration that HDACi-induced glial changes can increase BDNF and GDNF production in astrocytes, supporting dopaminergic neuron survival \[10\].

The epigenetic lock-in mechanism proposed for NR-7 represents its most distinctive pharmacological feature. The conversion of HDAC inhibition-induced histone acetylation into stable DNA methylation patterns is mechanistically precedented in cellular reprogramming biology, where transcription factor-driven chromatin opening during differentiation is progressively stabilised by DNA methyltransferase activity \[11\]. The question of whether pharmaceutical HDAC inhibition in post-mitotic glial cells can achieve comparable epigenetic stabilisation is a key hypothesis requiring experimental validation in in vivo models.

The glial selectivity achieved through quinoline-mediated LAT1/MCT1 transporter engagement is a critical differentiator from existing HDAC inhibitors. Preclinical studies with vorinostat and TSA demonstrate that non-selective HDAC inhibition can paradoxically enhance pro-inflammatory cytokines at low doses in glial cells, reflecting the context-dependence of histone acetylation effects \[12\]. Cell-type-selective delivery, as achieved by NR-7’s bifunctional architecture, addresses this heterogeneity and is expected to produce consistently anti-inflammatory glial reprogramming.

## 13. Conclusion

NeuroReset-7 establishes a new pharmacological category in neuropsychiatry: a glial-targeted epigenetic reprogrammer capable of producing semi-permanent improvements in the cellular infrastructure supporting neural circuit function. Through selective concentration in astrocytes and microglia, inhibition of HDAC1/2/3, and induction of a self-reinforcing anti-inflammatory and metabolically enhanced glial phenotype, NR-7 addresses root-cause glial dysfunction that underlies and perpetuates the symptom burden of ASD, schizophrenia, ADHD, and related conditions. Its epigenetic lock-in mechanism—producing 2–3 years of benefit from a 6-week treatment course—represents a fundamentally different pharmacological paradigm from chronic daily dosing and positions NR-7 as the infrastructure-enabling final step in a comprehensive neural restoration protocol. Priority experimental steps include in vitro validation of LAT1/MCT1-mediated glial selectivity, confirmation of epigenetic lock-in in rodent models, and toxicological characterisation of the quinazoline-quinoline scaffold.
